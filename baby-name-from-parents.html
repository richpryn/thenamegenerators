    How it works

    Every idea is a real first name given to babies in England and Wales, chosen because it links to both of you. The most popular names come first in each group.

    1. Starts like one of you, ends like the other. The opening of one name and the ending of the other, with at least four letters between them. For Emma and James that gives Jameela, Jamelia, Jemma.
    2. Shares your initials. Names that start with one of your initials. Ones that also have the other initial further in come first, like Elijah, Elsie for Emma and James.
    3. Has a sound from both of you. A run of letters from each name, anywhere in the new one: Ophelia, Daniella, Stephanie for Sophie and Daniel.
    4. Same rhythm. The same number of beats and the same ending as one of your names, so the names sound like a family: Alma, Shams, Rahma next to Emma.

    Names are matched by sound as well as spelling, so Sophie matches names that start "Sof" and Conor matches Konan. Your own names, and other spellings of them, are left out. Choose Girl or Boy to see only names given to girls or boys; Either shows both.

    Press Show blends under the lists for new names made from the start of one name and the end of the other, the same way as the Name Combiner. These are invented, and they're labelled that way.

    Before you choose

    • Say it with the surname. Out loud, a few times, and shouted up the stairs.
    • Check the initials. First, middle and surname together shouldn't spell anything awkward.
    • Look at the rank. A name in the top 10 will probably be shared with a classmate; one past No. 1,000 probably won't be.
    • Keep a shortlist. Tap the bookmark on any name you like, and compare them side by side later.

    Where the names come from

    The names and ranks come from the Office for National Statistics' baby names in England and Wales, 1996 to 2025. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0. The ONS counts each spelling separately and doesn't record accents, so Siobhán is matched as Siobhan.

    The list here has every name given to at least 5 babies in 2025, plus any name in the top 1,500 in an earlier year, which brings back 1990s names like Kirsty and Grant. Double-barrelled names are left out, and so are a few that belong to brands, film characters or famous people.

    Questions

    +How does it find names from both parents?

    It reads both names for their sounds, not just their spelling, so Sophie and Sofia both start with "sof". Then it looks through the official baby name lists for names that start like one of you and end like the other, share your initials, have a sound from each of you, or have the same rhythm as your names.

    +Are these real names?

    Yes. Every name in the four main groups is a real first name given to babies in England and Wales, from the Office for National Statistics lists for 1996 to 2025. Only the Blends group, which is off unless you tick it, has made-up names, and it says so.

    +What does the number next to each name mean?

    It's the name's rank in England and Wales in 2025: No. 1 was the most popular. A year instead, like "2005: No. 120", means the name wasn't ranked in 2025 and shows its best year since 1996. Names are listed most popular first within each group.

    +Why is an accent or a hyphen ignored?

    The ONS lists don't use accents, so Siobhán is matched as Siobhan, and Zoë as Zoe. With a double-barrelled name like Mary-Jane, the first part is used for how a name starts and the last part for how it ends.

    +Do you keep the names I type?

    No. The names are matched in your browser, and the last ones you typed are remembered in this browser only, so they're there next time. Names you bookmark go into the same shortlist as every generator on the site.
