An album title has a different job from a band name: it has to sum up a set of songs and look right on a cover. This album name generator builds titles in the shapes real albums use: a mood and an object, a number, a place, or a dedication, like "Songs for Late Trains".

Where album titles come from

  • A line or idea from the music: Pink Floyd's The Dark Side of the Moon.
  • A phrase borrowed from somewhere else: Radiohead's OK Computer came from a line in the radio series of The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy.
  • A place: The Beatles' Abbey Road is named after the street their studio was on.
  • The story around the record: Fleetwood Mac's Rumours refers to the gossip around the band while they made it.
  • One word: Nirvana's Nevermind.

Choosing a title for your record

  • Listen for a line in your lyrics that sums up the record. The generator's titles are a starting point, but the best title is often already in a song.
  • Think about the cover. Some titles suggest an image straight away: "Notes from the Lighthouse" practically designs itself.
  • Use the title track. Naming the album after its strongest song is common and easy to remember.
  • Search before you print. Album titles aren't usually protected like band names, but sharing one with a famous record makes yours hard to find.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I name my album?

Look for a line in the lyrics that sums up the record, use the strongest song's title, or pick a place or idea connected to how it was made. The generator gives you shapes to start from.

Can I use these titles for my album or EP?

Yes, free and with no credit needed. Search streaming services first so yours isn't lost among records with the same title.
