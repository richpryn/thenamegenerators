Titles from real demonology

The habit of giving demons grand titles is centuries old:

  • The Ars Goetia, the first book of the 17th-century Lesser Key of Solomon, lists 72 demons, each with a rank borrowed from nobility: Bael is a king, Marbas a president, Furcas a knight. The Rank and domain option builds titles the same way.
  • Beelzebub, "Lord of the Flies", comes from Ba'al Zebub, a god of the Philistine city of Ekron in the Bible.
  • Abaddon, called Apollyon in Greek, is "the Destroyer" in the Book of Revelation.
  • Legion takes its name from the line "My name is Legion: for we are many" in the Gospel of Mark.
  • Lucifer means "light-bringer": the Latin name for the morning star.

The seven princes of sin

In 1589 the German theologian Peter Binsfeld matched a prince of Hell to each of the seven deadly sins, and the list is still the basis of countless games and stories:

  • Lucifer: pride
  • Mammon: greed
  • Asmodeus: lust
  • Leviathan: envy
  • Beelzebub: gluttony
  • Satan: wrath
  • Belphegor: sloth

The Sin and vice category follows the same idea: pick the sin your demon rules, and its epithet does the rest (the Gluttonous, Envy Given Form).

Demons and devils in D&D

Dungeons & Dragons draws a line worth knowing: demons are creatures of chaos from the Abyss, while devils are creatures of order from the Nine Hells. Their titles show it:

  • Demon lords: Demogorgon, Prince of Demons; Orcus, Prince of Undeath; Graz'zt, the Dark Prince; Juiblex, the Faceless Lord.
  • Archdevils: Asmodeus, Lord of the Nine Hells; Zariel, Lord of Avernus.

For a demon, wild and violent epithets fit (the Ravager, the Unbound). For a devil, go for rank and order: a Duke or Marquis of somewhere specific, with a title that sounds like a contract.

Building a full demonic title

The most memorable demons stack their titles. Try name, then rank and domain, then an epithet:

  • Vharzul, Duke of Ashes, the Unforgiving
  • Ozrakh the Devourer, Prince of the Sunless Sea

Two titles is plenty for most demons; save the long string for the one at the end of the campaign. And let mortals use the short version: they're afraid to say the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a demon epithet?

A title added to a demon's name that describes its power, sin or domain, like "the Devourer" or "Lord of the Flies". Demons in folklore, religious texts and fantasy often have several.

Where do demon ranks like Duke and Marquis come from?

From grimoires like the Ars Goetia, which gives each of its 72 demons a noble rank: king, prince, duke, marquis, president, earl or knight. The Rank and domain option builds titles the same way.

What's the difference between a demon and a devil?

In D&D, demons are chaotic creatures of the Abyss and devils are orderly creatures of the Nine Hells. In older Christian writing the words are used much more loosely.

Can I give a demon more than one epithet?

Yes, and many famous ones have several. A rank and domain plus one epithet ("Duke of Ashes, the Unforgiving") is a good balance.
