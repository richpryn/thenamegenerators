A short history of the sidekick

The first great superhero sidekick was Robin, who joined Batman in Detective Comics #38 in 1940.

  • Robin's success led to a wave of young sidekicks through the 1940s, many named after their hero with "Kid" or "Boy" attached.
  • The word sidekick dates from the early 1900s. It probably comes from pickpockets' slang, where a "kick" was a pocket and the side pocket was the hardest to pick, so the person always at your side was your side-kicker.
  • Animal sidekicks are just as old as human ones, from cartoon dogs to talking birds.

Naming your sidekick

  • Young heroes often echo their mentor, with Kid, Young or Wonder in front: Kid Frost, Young Comet.
  • Animal sidekicks get a name and a talent: Rocket the Amazing Hedgehog.
  • Funny sidekicks are the ones who'd rather not be there: Doctor Eventually, Kevin from Logistics.
  • Add a role to say what they actually do, from driving the getaway car to keeping the snacks.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Who was the first superhero sidekick?
Robin, who first appeared with Batman in Detective Comics #38 in 1940.
+What's a good name for a sidekick?
Echo the hero (Kid Comet), give an animal a title (Agent Paws), or make it funny (Captain Probably).
+Where does the word sidekick come from?
Probably from pickpockets' slang of the 1800s, where a kick was a pocket: the side pocket was the safest, so a close companion was your side-kicker.
