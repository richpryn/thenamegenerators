How Egyptian names work

Most Ancient Egyptian names are short sentences, usually about a god. Learn a few pieces and you can read them:

  • -mose means "is born": Thutmose, "Thoth is born"; Ramose, "Ra is born".
  • -hotep means "is satisfied" or "at peace": Amenhotep, "Amun is satisfied".
  • Mery- or Merit- means "beloved of": Meritamun, "beloved of Amun".
  • Nefer means "beautiful" or "good": Nefertari, "beautiful companion".
  • Sat- or Sit- means "daughter of": Sitre, "daughter of Ra".

Everyday names were often simpler: Nakht means "strong", and Tamit means "she-cat".

Mummies, curses and real people

  • The word: mummy comes from the Arabic mūmiyā, bitumen, because the dark embalmed bodies were once thought to be coated in it.
  • The process: the Greek historian Herodotus wrote that embalming took 70 days, and that fits what Egyptologists have found.
  • The curse: the idea of a mummy's curse took off after Lord Carnarvon, who paid for the dig, died in 1923, a few months after Tutankhamun's tomb was opened. The 1932 film The Mummy, with Boris Karloff, turned the mummy into a movie monster.
  • Real people: the names here belonged to real Egyptians, and the mummies in museums are real people's remains. Use the names with a bit of respect, even for Halloween.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What does -hotep mean in Egyptian names?
"Is satisfied" or "is at peace". Amenhotep means "Amun is satisfied", and Imhotep means "he who comes in peace".
+Are these real Ancient Egyptian names?
Yes. Every name comes from Ancient Egypt, from pharaohs like Thutmose and Hatshepsut to everyday people like Sennedjem, "sweet brother".
+Where does the word mummy come from?
From the Arabic mūmiyā, bitumen, because embalmed bodies were once thought to have been preserved with it.
