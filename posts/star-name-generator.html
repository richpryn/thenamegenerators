How real stars are named

  • Bayer designations. In 1603 Johann Bayer labelled the stars in each constellation with Greek letters, usually brightest first, followed by the constellation's name in Latin: Alpha Centauri, Beta Orionis. The generator's catalogue style follows the same pattern with invented constellations: Delta Nalorae.
  • Old names. Many bright stars keep names that came into English from Arabic. Aldebaran means "the follower", because it follows the Pleiades across the sky, and Altair comes from a phrase meaning "the flying eagle".
  • Catalogue numbers. Most stars only have numbers, like HD numbers from the Henry Draper Catalogue or Gliese numbers for nearby stars.
  • Star systems. A system takes its name from its main star. In a multiple system each star gets a capital letter (Alpha Centauri A and B), and planets get lower-case letters starting at b: 51 Pegasi b was the first planet found orbiting a Sun-like star.

The International Astronomical Union approves official names. Everything here is invented, so it won't clash with a real star. Choose Sci-fi for coined names and discoverers' stars, Fantasy/myth for stars from legend, Catalogue for codes like Sable-93a, and Poetic for names like The Patient Candle. Add With a description for a line about what the star is, or what it means to the people below.

Naming a star system

  • Name the star first, then give its planets the star's name plus a letter, or names of their own.
  • Use two names for important stars: a catalogue code for the navigators and an old name for everyone else.
  • Binary stars make good settings. Two suns means two sunsets, and maybe nights that never get properly dark.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good star names for a sci-fi story?
A coined name with a Greek letter, like Zanessa Alpha, a discoverer's name, like Kaminski's Star, or a catalogue code like UXT 19147 A. Choose Fantasy/myth or Poetic for stars with a story behind them.
+How do you name a star system?
Name it after its main star. Extra stars in the system get capital letters (A, B), and planets get lower-case letters starting at b, so the first planet of Theta Thessarae would be Theta Thessarae b.
+Are these real stars?
No. The names and catalogue numbers are invented, though they're built the way real star names and designations are.
