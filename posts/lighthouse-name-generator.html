A good lighthouse name is the name of the danger it guards: a point, a head, a rock or a skerry, plus Light or Lighthouse. This lighthouse name generator makes British-style point, head and rock lights, storm-lashed haunted towers, cosy little lighthouses, fantasy sea-lamps and sci-fi beacons, and it can tell you who keeps the lamp burning.

How lighthouses are named

The Pharos of Alexandria, one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world, gave its name to lighthouses across Europe: phare in French and faro in Spanish and Italian. In Britain, lights are named after the place they mark.

  • British names: a Point, Head, Ness, Skerry or Rock, then Light or Lighthouse. A High Light and a Low Light were built as a pair, so ships could line them up to find a safe channel.
  • Stormy names come from the rocks themselves. The first Eddystone lighthouse, off Plymouth, was swept away in the great storm of 1703 with its builder, Henry Winstanley, inside. The Bell Rock lighthouse, finished in 1810, still stands on a reef that is under water at high tide.
  • Cosy names are for a postcard lighthouse: cockles, kittiwakes, cocoa.
  • Fantasy sea-lamps are lanterns, gleams and beacons, kept by dragons, monks and mermaids.
  • Sci-fi beacons keep the old seabird names and add a number.

Choose With the keeper's story for a name and a person. The most famous real keeper's family is the Darlings of the Longstone lighthouse: in 1838 Grace Darling and her father rowed out in a storm to rescue survivors of the wrecked steamer Forfarshire.

Using a lighthouse name

  • For a story, the keeper's story is a ready-made character, and the stormy descriptions are ready-made openings.
  • For a map, put lighthouses on headlands and reefs, where they'd really be needed.
  • For a cosy game or a children's book, the cosy style keeps it gentle.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good lighthouse names?
The place it guards plus Light or Lighthouse: Inverellan Skerry Lighthouse, Balvaig Low Light, Kittiwake Corner Light. For a story, add the keeper, like Selholm High Light, kept for twenty-two years by Edith Stobbs.
+Why do some lighthouses come in High and Low pairs?
A high light and a low light were built so that ships could line them up, one above the other, to steer a safe course into a harbour or channel.
+Where does the word pharos come from?
From the Pharos of Alexandria, the great lighthouse built on the island of Pharos in Egypt. Its name became the word for a lighthouse in Greek, and phare and faro in French, Spanish and Italian.
