How the Story Prompt Generator Works

It works from 4,358 prompts and name parts, which combine into over 1,100,000,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Genre: Fantasy, Mystery, Sci-fi, Spooky, Adventure, Funny, Everyday.
  • Who is it for?: Kids, Teens and adults.
  • What do you need?:
    • Full prompt, e.g. Picture a candy-striped lighthouse where the rain tastes of lemonade. Here, a shy unicorn who knits jumpers for trolls must find out who keeps stealing the stars. The problem? They have only one wish left.
    • Just a character, e.g. A forgetful fairy godparent who is the only one who can see the castle ghost
    • Just a setting, e.g. A goblin market where the shadows have their own shadows
    • Just a plot, e.g. With only a very old dog to help, your hero has to carry a secret message across the Whispering Mountains.
    • Just a twist, e.g. The hero's teacher cast the spell in the first place.

A good story prompt gives you a character, a problem and a reason it's hard, and leaves the rest to you. The Story Prompt Generator makes a fresh one in a click. Pick a genre and an age group, then take a whole prompt or just the piece you need: a character, a setting, a plot or a twist.

Six story prompts to start with

All six came from the generator, for children and for older writers:

  • "A retired dragon who is the only one who can see the castle ghost sets out to return a library book that is 300 years overdue." Fantasy, kids.
  • "A baby phoenix who can talk to bees has to find a new home for a family of fire sprites." Fantasy, kids.
  • "A penguin who has hiccups that won't stop." A funny character on its own, for younger writers to build a story around.
  • "An insurance investigator who receives a letter in their own handwriting sets out to find out why the last lighthouse keeper vanished." Mystery, teens and adults.
  • "The planet they're terraforming already belongs to someone." A sci-fi twist to drop into a story you've already started.
  • "Your hero has to keep the family café going through a hard winter." An everyday plot, with no magic needed.

Genres, ages and parts

  • Seven genres: fantasy, mystery, sci-fi, spooky, adventure, funny and everyday. Spooky prompts for children stay friendly: a ghost train where the ghosts throw a party, not anything that keeps anyone up at night.
  • Two age groups: Kids (6 to 11) gives playful heroes and clear goals; Teens and adults adds complications, secrets and harder choices.
  • Five parts: a full prompt, or just a character, a setting, a plot or a twist. Use the parts on their own to fill a gap in a story you've already started, or roll one of each and put them together.

Turning a prompt into a story

  • Ask what the hero wants, and what's stopping them. The prompt gives you both; the story is how they get round it.
  • Plan the middle. Most stories go wrong halfway through, so decide on one thing that makes the problem worse before you start writing.
  • Save the twist for late on. A twist works best once the reader thinks they know what's going on.
  • In a classroom, give everyone the same prompt and compare how differently the stories turn out.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What is a good story prompt for kids?
One with a fun hero, a clear job to do and something in the way, like a baby phoenix who has to find a new home for a family of fire sprites. Choose Kids (6 to 11) and a genre for prompts like this.
+Can I get just a character or a twist?
Yes. Choose Just a character, Just a setting, Just a plot or Just a twist to get one piece at a time, which is useful when you're stuck partway through a story.
+Can I use these prompts in a lesson or a writing group?
Yes, they're free to use. Giving everyone the same prompt and comparing the stories is a good writing exercise.
