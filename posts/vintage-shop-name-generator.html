  • Sixties Archive: names the decade, so collectors know what to expect.
  • Chintz & Chrome: two eras in three words: floral fabric and diner chrome.
  • Dust & Gold: the promise of every rummage: most of it's dust, some of it's gold.
  • Lot 73 Vintage: borrows the language of auction lots.
  • Cartwright Vintage & Retro: a family name for a shop that wants to feel established.
  • Thrifty Business: a pun that suits a charity-style or budget rail.

Vintage, antique, retro or thrift?

The words mean different things to customers, so pick the one that matches your stock:

  • Vintage is usually used for things at least 20 years old, especially clothes.
  • Antique is usually kept for things around 100 years old or more. These are common trade usages, not legal definitions.
  • Retro means new things made in an old style, or a look borrowed from a past decade.
  • Thrift, preloved and secondhand say it's affordable rather than collectable. "Thrift store" is US wording; in the UK people say charity shop or secondhand shop.

Choose Classic antiques for an emporium or antiques centre, Thrift and resale for a budget rail, and For Instagram if you sell mostly online.

Check it's free before you paint the sign

Before you pay for a sign, a logo or printed bags, check the name is free:

  • Company register: if you form a limited company in the UK, Companies House won't accept a name that's the same as one already registered. In the US, search your state's business register.
  • Trade marks: search the UK IPO or USPTO register for your kind of goods, so you don't clash with an existing brand.
  • Web and social: check the domain and the handles you'll use, since many customers will find you on Instagram first.

Press Check on any name, or use the Business Name Checker, to run those searches in one place. The Business Naming Best Practices guide covers the rest. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Making the name work in the shop

  • Say the era if you specialise. A shop that only sells 1960s and 70s clothes is easier to find if the name says so.
  • Think about the swing tag. Short names fit labels and carrier bags.
  • Selling online too? Check the name works as a handle without spaces.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good vintage shop names?
Names that hint at an era or a second life: Then Again Vintage, Sixties Archive, Chintz & Chrome or Dust & Gold. Puns such as Retro Active suit a clothing rail; a classic name suits an antiques centre.
+How do I check a vintage shop name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it is free as a company name, a web address and a social media handle. In the UK, search the Companies House register; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Do this before you pay for a logo, labels or signage.
+What is a good name for a thrift store?
One that tells people they will find secondhand treasure, is easy to say and still fits a handle. Puns on second, twice and past (Thrift Happens, Worn Again) suit a clothing rail or an Instagram shop; an antiques centre does better with a classic name such as The Old Forge Emporium. Avoid names close to big resale chains and charities, such as Goodwill, Oxfam or Beyond Retro.
+What's the difference between vintage and antique?
In common use, vintage usually means at least 20 years old and antique around 100 years or more. They're trade conventions rather than legal definitions, so describe your stock honestly.
