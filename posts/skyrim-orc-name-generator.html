Every orc you meet in Skyrim has the same kind of name: a short, hard-edged first name, then gro- or gra- and the name of a parent. Once you know the pattern you'll spot it everywhere, from the librarian at the College of Winterhold to the smiths of Markarth. This Skyrim orc name generator builds full names that way, or gives you just the first name if you'd rather add the rest yourself.

What gro- and gra- mean

The second half of a Skyrim orc's name is about family. gro- means "son of" and gra- means "daughter of", and the name that follows is a parent's, most often the father's. So Urag gro-Shub, who looks after the Arcanaeum at the College of Winterhold, is Urag, son of Shub.

You can see both halves of the pattern side by side in Markarth, where the smiths Moth gro-Bagol and Ghorza gra-Bagol share the same family name with a different prefix.

The prefix is lower case and joined to the parent's name with a hyphen. In the generator, full names follow the same rule: men get gro-, women get gra-, and the parent's name comes from the list of male first names.

Names in the orc strongholds

Most of Skyrim's orcs live in four strongholds, each run by a chief: Dushnikh Yal and Mor Khazgur in the Reach, Largashbur in the Rift, and Narzulbur in Eastmarch. Life there follows the Code of Malacath, the Daedric Prince the orcs worship.

Stronghold names come with roles attached. There's the chief, his wives, a forge-wife who keeps the smithy (Gharol at Dushnikh Yal, for example) and a wise-woman who speaks for Malacath. If you're naming a character from a stronghold, it's worth deciding their role too, since it says as much about them as the name does.

Some orcs also earn an epithet. Borgakh the Steel Heart, the chief's daughter at Mor Khazgur who can become your follower, goes by her deed-name rather than a gro-/gra- surname. An epithet suits a character with a reputation: a champion, an exile, a famous smith.

How the first names sound

Skyrim orc first names are short, usually one or two syllables, and lean on hard sounds: g, k, z, sh and rough vowels like u and o.

  • Male names mostly end in a hard consonant: -ash, -ak, -uk, -ag, -og, as in Durgash, Makrag and Lurbuk.
  • Female names nearly always end in -a, especially -ra and -ha, as in Bolarza, Urzoga and Magzura. Same sounds, softer ending.

A few names in the generator, like Urag and Ghorza, appear in the game itself. The rest are new names built from the same sounds, so they'll fit in without belonging to anyone you'll meet in Tamriel.

Putting a name together

  • Start with the first name. Say it out loud: Skyrim orc names are meant to be barked across a stronghold, not whispered.
  • Pick the parent. The parent's name hints at family history. A character named after a famous chief carries that reputation with them, for better or worse.
  • Add an epithet if they've earned one. "the Steel Heart", "the Unbroken", "Ironhand": short and about something they did or are known for.
  • Think about where they live. An orc raised in a city, like Urag in Winterhold, still uses gro-/gra-, but a stronghold orc's name tells you which chief they answer to.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does gro- mean in Skyrim orc names?

"Son of." Its female form, gra-, means "daughter of". The name after it is a parent's, most often the father's, so Urag gro-Shub is Urag, son of Shub.

Do I have to use gro- or gra-?

No. Almost every orc in Skyrim has one, so it's the most authentic choice, but an orc known by a deed-name (like Borgakh the Steel Heart) can drop it. Set Name Format to "First name only" if you want to build the rest yourself.

Are these names from the game?

A few first names, such as Urag and Ghorza, are. Most are new names made from the same sounds, so you can use them without borrowing a character the player has already met.

Can I use these names in other Elder Scrolls games?

Yes. The gro-/gra- pattern is how orcs are named across the Elder Scrolls series, so the names work just as well for Oblivion, Elder Scrolls Online or your own Tamriel campaign.
