Good steampunk city names sound like a Victorian mill town with something mechanical going on: an old English ending, a word from the factory or the clock, and a bit of soot. The Steampunk City Name Generator builds names for the smoky, gear-driven towns of a steampunk world: Victorian mill towns, airship ports, clockwork cities, fog-bound undercities and frontier railheads, with a short description if you want one.

Eight good steampunk city names

All eight came from the generator, across its five styles:

  • Shuttleton: a weaving town named after the loom's shuttle, with a real -ton ending.
  • Clinkerworth: named after the clinker left in the furnace, which is exactly what it smells of.
  • Normington’s Mast: an airship port named after the person who put up the mooring mast.
  • Tetherfield, whose harbourmaster has never once set foot on the ground: a sky port with a story in its description.
  • Twelve Chimes: a clockwork city named after what you hear on the hour.
  • Pinionwick, whose clockwork birds sing the hours from every rooftop: a gear name with the most charming description.
  • Gloombottom: the bottom of the smog, named honestly.
  • Tattersall’s Halt: a frontier railway stop named after a person, the way real halts were.

Where steampunk names come from

The word steampunk was coined by the writer K. W. Jeter in 1987, as a jokey label for Victorian-set fantasy, but the cities go back further. Charles Dickens invented Coketown for Hard Times (1854), a mill town of red brick and smoke, and Manchester was nicknamed Cottonopolis. Steampunk names borrow that world and add brass.

  • Victorian mill towns join a trade word to an English ending: Weftbury, Loombridge, Crucibleton.
  • Airship ports use sailing words: masts, quays, capstans and windlasses. Capstanfield, Gustbury Air Quay.
  • Clockwork cities are named after gears and bells: Dialbury, Eleven Hands, The Turning City.
  • Smog and under names are for the tunnels and the soot: Soothollow, The Lamplit Under.
  • Frontier names are forts, halts and railheads with a founder's surname: Fort Bairstow, Quarrie’s Halt.

Building a steampunk city

  • Give it an industry. A city that makes cloth, clocks or airships should sound like it.
  • Split it in two. The polished upper town and the smoky under-city can have different names, and the gap between them is a story.
  • Name the railway stops. A few halts and junctions make a map feel connected.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good steampunk city names?
Names that sound like a Victorian mill town with a mechanical twist: Shuttleton, Clinkerworth, Twelve Chimes or Pinionwick. For airship ports, try names with mast or quay in them, like Normington’s Mast.
+Who coined the word steampunk?
The writer K. W. Jeter, in 1987, as a playful name for fiction set in a Victorian world with advanced steam technology.
+Can I use these for a steampunk RPG or story?
Yes. They're made up and free to use. Choose With a description for a line about each town.
