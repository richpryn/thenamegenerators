Gnomes collect names the way other people collect buttons. In D&D a gnome gets a name from their mother, their father, a clan elder and assorted aunts and uncles, plus nicknames from everyone else, and ends up with half a dozen or more. With outsiders they settle on three. This gnome name generator gives you those three: a personal name, a clan name and a nickname, like Fizwick “Ticktock” Spindleworth.

Why gnomes have so many names

The fifth-edition Player's Handbook (2014) says gnomes love names. Each relative gives a newborn gnome a name, usually a variation on the name of an ancestor or distant relative, though some are brand new inventions. Nicknames from everyone else may or may not stick.

Other peoples find this exhausting, so when dealing with humans and others who are "stuffy" about names, a gnome uses no more than three: a personal name, a clan name and a nickname, picking the one in each category that is the most fun to say. That last rule is the best guide to a good gnome name.

What gnome names sound like

  • Personal names bounce. Doubled consonants and light endings: -bble, -wick, -bin, -bel, -mip. Say Tibble, Lillibee or Quibbin and it's hard not to smile.
  • Clan names sound like a family workshop: Copperkettle, Spindleworth, Tinkerton. Rock gnomes, D&D's tinkerers, suit the clockwork-sounding ones; forest gnomes suit Mossworth or Jinglebrook.
  • Nicknames are short and earned: Kaboom, Sootface, Twofingers. For a rock gnome inventor, the nickname is often a warning.

All the names here are new, built from those sounds rather than copied from the Player's Handbook.

Giving your gnome more names

The three-name format is the polite version. At the table it's fun to let the rest slip out:

  • Give the full list once. When the gnome first introduces themselves to someone important, let them rattle off six names, then say "but you can call me Pemble."
  • Change the nickname. Nicknames come and go. A gnome who blew up the party's cart can arrive at the next town as "Kaboom".
  • Ask who gave which name. A name from a disapproving aunt is a story in itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many names does a gnome have in D&D?

Usually half a dozen or more, given by parents, the clan elder, aunts, uncles and friends. With outsiders, a gnome uses three: a personal name, a clan name and a nickname.

What's the difference between rock gnome and forest gnome names?

The Player's Handbook gives both the same kind of name. For flavour, rock gnome tinkerers suit workshop-sounding clan names like Copperkettle, and forest gnomes suit names from nature, like Mossworth.

Are these names from the Player's Handbook?

No. They're new names built in the same style, so your gnome won't share a name with an example from the book.

Do deep gnomes use the same names?

Deep gnomes, or svirfneblin, are a separate people who live in the Underdark, with their own culture. These bouncy names suit rock and forest gnomes best; for a deep gnome, pick the plainer ones.
