How punk names work

Punk broke in 1976 and 1977, with the Ramones in New York and the Sex Pistols, the Clash, the Damned and Buzzcocks in Britain. Its names set out to annoy, and they followed a few habits that are still going strong:

  • Stage names as insults. Johnny Rotten, Rat Scabies and Captain Sensible of the Damned, Poly Styrene of X-Ray Spex. All four Ramones took the surname Ramone. The generator's Stage name option works the same way: Gaz Static, Patty Bleach, Lord Disaster.
  • Plain plural bands. The Scallywags, The Backbenchers: something your parents or teachers would call you.
  • A name and a gang, like Siouxsie and the Banshees: Dez Pylon and the Gatecrashers.
  • One hard word: Overspill, Backfire.

Album titles are where punk gets sarcastic: Music for Shopping Centres, Songs for the Unemployable, Ignore the Headlines.

Using a punk name

  • Keep it short enough to shout between songs, and to stencil on a T-shirt.
  • Aim it at something. The best punk names are about a place, a person in charge or a boring rule.
  • Search before you commit. Look the name up on Spotify, Bandcamp and YouTube, and check the social handles are free. Two acts with one name confuse fans and streaming services alike.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good punk band names?
Short, snotty and about something: The Detentions, Council Kids, Plastic Nerves or Second-Hand Neighbours. Add a frontman for a name like Vic Shrapnel and the Mardy Kids.
+How do you make a punk stage name?
Take an ordinary first name and add something unpleasant or dangerous, the way the 70s bands did: Gaz Static, Polly Asbestos, Benny Smog. A fake title works too, like Sergeant Nuisance.
+Are these real punk bands?
No, they're made up in the style of the UK and US scenes. Punk has thousands of small bands, so search Bandcamp and Spotify before you print the T-shirts.
