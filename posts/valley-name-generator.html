How the Valley Name Generator Works

The choices:

  • Name format:

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Good valley names say what's there or who lived there, with a real valley word on the end: Hazeldale, Rookscombe, Wolf Clough. Real valleys are named for a river, a bird, a tree or a long-dead farmer, which is why Ketteldale and Owlcombe sound lived in. This valley name generator builds fantasy, British, elven, dark and hidden valley names, with an optional line about what lies there.

Valley words and where they come from

Britain has a word for every kind of valley, and most of them came from the people who settled there:

  • Dale, from Old Norse and Old English, all over the north: the Yorkshire Dales.
  • Glen, from the Gaelic gleann, a narrow Highland valley: Glencoe.
  • Strath, from the Gaelic srath, a wide, flat-bottomed valley.
  • Combe, from Old English, a short valley in the south-west, and cwm, the Welsh word it's related to.
  • Clough, a steep, narrow valley in the north of England.

The styles follow those words. British valleys use them straight; fantasy valleys use -fold and -reach; elven valleys are vales; dark valleys are gulches, gorges and hollows; and hidden valleys are named for what hides them. Choose With a description for a line about what lies there.

Putting valleys on your map

  • Name the valley after its river, or the river after the valley. Real ones often share a name.
  • Match the word to the region. A glen in the north and a combe in the south tell readers where they are.
  • Give hidden valleys a way in. A pass that opens at midsummer is a quest in itself.

For what runs through it, try the River Name Generator; for what surrounds it, the Mountain Name Generator; and for who lives there, the Village Name Generator. An elven vale needs an elf city.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for a valley?
A feature plus a real valley word: Hazeldale, Rookscombe, Wolf Clough, or a vale for something elven. Choose a style to match the region.
+What's the difference between a dale, a glen and a combe?
They're regional words. A dale is a northern English valley, a glen a narrow Scottish one, and a combe a short valley in the south-west of England. Cwm is the Welsh equivalent of combe.
+Can I name a valley after a river?
Yes, and real valleys often are. Name one first and use it for the other, so your map hangs together.
