Where zombies come from

The zombie comes from Haitian folklore, where a zonbi was a dead person raised and controlled by a sorcerer. The flesh-eating, shambling zombie of modern films comes largely from George A. Romero's Night of the Living Dead (1968), which never actually calls its monsters zombies.

Naming the outbreak

Modern zombie stories usually blame a disease, and give it a name: the T-virus in Resident Evil, the Rage virus in 28 Days Later, Solanum in Max Brooks' The Zombie Survival Guide and World War Z, and the Cordyceps fungus in The Last of Us. A good outbreak name sounds scientific and ominous at the same time. The generator mixes classical names (Lazarus, Cerberus), Greek letters and medical words.

Using the names

  • Funny zombies suit Halloween parties, zombie runs and comedy horror: an adjective and a very ordinary first name.
  • Grim zombies suit horror stories and tabletop games, where a named zombie is someone the survivors once knew.
  • Virus names give a campaign or story its villain before anyone has seen a zombie.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I call my zombie virus?

Something that sounds scientific and ominous: a classical name (Lazarus, Cerberus), a Greek letter or a code (Omega-Necrosis), and a medical word (Strain, Pathogen).

Where does the word zombie come from?

From Haitian folklore, where a zonbi was a dead person raised and controlled by a sorcerer.

What are some famous zombie viruses?

The T-virus (Resident Evil), the Rage virus (28 Days Later), Solanum (World War Z) and the Cordyceps fungus (The Last of Us).
