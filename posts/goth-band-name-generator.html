How goth names itself

Gothic rock grew out of post-punk around 1979, the year Bauhaus released "Bela Lugosi's Dead". Siouxsie and the Banshees, The Cure and the Sisters of Mercy, who took their name from a Leonard Cohen song, shaped the sound, and the Batcave club in London, opened in 1982, gave the scene a home. In Germany, darkwave grew into a scene of its own, with the Wave-Gotik-Treffen festival in Leipzig.

Goth names lean on literature and old Europe:

  • A heroine and her gang: Sylvia and the Somnambulists, Evangeline and the Widows.
  • Cold places and machines for post-punk: Static Arcade, Twin Hotel.
  • German and French for darkwave: Schattenspiel, Les Heures Grises.

Choose Stage name for names like Felix Ravenscroft or single names like Seraphine, and Album title for records like Velvet Ceremonies.

Using a goth name

  • Romance, not gore. Goth is about longing, candles and old houses. Leave the blood to death metal.
  • Foreign words should mean something. Check the German or French says what you think it says.
  • Search before you commit. Look the name up on Spotify, Bandcamp and YouTube, and check the social handles are free. Two acts with one name confuse fans and streaming services alike.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good goth band names?
Romantic, literary and a bit morbid: Isadora and the Ravens, Fields of Black Lace, Cold Station or Ligeia and the Lanterns. A German or French name, like Schattenspiel, suits darkwave.
+What's the difference between goth and darkwave names?
Gothic rock names tend to be Victorian and literary; darkwave, with its synths and German roots, leans towards cold, short names and foreign words.
+Are these real goth bands?
No, they're new names in the style of the scene. Search Bandcamp and Spotify before you use one, as the darkwave revival has a lot of new bands.
