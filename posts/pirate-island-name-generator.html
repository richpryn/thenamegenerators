  • Gold Bar Cay, where the treasure map is scratched onto the shell of a very old tortoise: a treasure hunt with a twist.
  • Port Oakum: a pirate port named after the tarred rope used to seal a ship's planks.
  • Brigand's Wharf, whose governor is a retired pirate with a very fancy hat: a pirate haven with a ruler to match.
  • Ghost Crab Island, where footprints appear in the sand with nobody there to make them: a cursed island that stays spooky without being scary.

Real pirate havens and treasure islands

The age of piracy had real island bases, and their names still turn up in pirate stories.

  • Tortuga, off the north coast of Hispaniola, is Spanish for "turtle", supposedly from the island's shape. It was a famous buccaneer base in the 1600s.
  • Nassau, in the Bahamas, was a pirate haven in the early 1700s, until the British sent a governor to clear the pirates out.
  • Cay and key come from the Spanish cayo, a small low island, which is why the Caribbean is full of them.
  • Treasure Island gave us most of the rest. Robert Louis Stevenson's novel, published as a book in 1883, has the map, the X and the buried gold that every pirate island since has borrowed.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good pirate island names?
Names that promise treasure or trouble, like Lost Map Isle, Groaning Cay or Port Oakum, or silly ones like Moody Parrot Rock for a younger crew.
+What does cay mean in an island name?
A small, low island, often a sandbank on a reef. The word comes from the Spanish cayo, and key means the same, as in the Florida Keys.
+Can I use these for a pirate party treasure hunt?
Yes. Choose Treasure and With a description, and each island comes with a clue to hide.
