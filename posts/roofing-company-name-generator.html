What makes a roofing name work

Roofing has its own vocabulary, and customers trust a name that uses it properly:

  • Roof words: a ridge is the top line of a roof, a hip the sloping edge where two slopes meet, a valley the channel between them and a gable the triangle of wall at the end. A truss is the frame that holds it all up.
  • Specialisms: slaters, thatchers, leadwork, flat roofs, guttering, and fascias and soffits are all searched for. Naming one tells people you do it.
  • Strong names with Storm, Shield or Fortress suit a firm that does repairs after bad weather.
  • Puns work well on vans and ladders: Slate Expectations, Shingle Minded.

Check it's free before you print the sign

A roofing name ends up on a van, a website, a Google Business Profile and a stack of invoices, so it's worth a few minutes' checking before any of that is printed:

  • Press Check on any name above. The Business Name Checker looks up the domain, links to the company register (Companies House in the UK) and opens the trade mark search and social handles.
  • Search your area. Look up the name on a map and in local search results. Two firms with the same name a few miles apart confuse customers and reviews.
  • Avoid names close to a well-known roofing brand or product, which can cause trade mark problems.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, from shortlisting to registering, read our guide to Business Naming Best Practices.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What's a good name for a roofing company?
One that's easy to read on a van and sounds dependable: your surname with Roofing (Hartley Roofing & Guttering), a strong weather word (Storm Ridge Roofing) or a friendly pun (In Truss We Trust). Check the name is free locally before you print anything.
+How do I check a roofing company name is free?
Press Check next to any name to see if it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. Then make sure: in the UK, search Companies House and the UK IPO trade mark register; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. A quick map search for the name in your area helps too.
+Should I put my town in a roofing company name?
If most of your work is local, yes: a name like Hampton Slaters helps people find you when they search after a storm. If you plan to cover a wide area, a strong name like Ridgeline Roofing & Restoration travels better.
