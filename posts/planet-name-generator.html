How the Planet Name Generator Works

It works from 800 names and name parts, which combine into over 270,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Kind of name: Any, Alien-sounding, Survey catalogue, Human colony, Descriptive.
  • Name format:

    • With a numeral, e.g. Ochrewild XI

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Real planets get catalogue numbers; the ones in stories get names. This planet name generator makes both: alien-sounding worlds, survey catalogue designations, human colonies named after home, and descriptive names like Glasswater, with a numeral or the kind of world if you want one.

How real planets are named

Real planets outside our solar system are named after their star, with a lower-case letter in order of discovery.

  • The first exoplanet found around a Sun-like star was 51 Pegasi b, in 1995: the first planet of the star 51 Pegasi. The star itself is "a".
  • Survey numbers: planets found by telescopes like Kepler take the survey's name, as in Kepler-452b.
  • Public names: the International Astronomical Union's NameExoWorlds contests let the public name some, so 51 Pegasi b is also called Dimidium.
  • Our own planets are named after Roman gods, apart from Uranus, who's Greek.

Naming a planet for a story

  • Alien names are coined to sound like nowhere on Earth: Xasephis, Karevos.
  • Catalogue numbers (KTS-4417 b) sound like a world nobody has named yet.
  • Colonies are named by the people who settled them, after home, a founder or a hope: New Meridian, Okafor's Landing, Last Concord.
  • Descriptive names are what the first crew called it: Glasswater, Stormhold.
  • Add a numeral (Velithra IV) for a planet's place around its star, or a world type, like "an ocean world".

Frequently Asked Questions

+How are real exoplanets named?
After their star plus a lower-case letter, from b upwards in order of discovery, like 51 Pegasi b. Some also get public names through the IAU's NameExoWorlds contests.
+What's a good name for an alien planet?
A coined name that sounds like nowhere on Earth, like Xasephis, with a numeral for its place around its star: Velithra IV.
+Why are the planets in our solar system named after gods?
Because the Romans named the ones they could see after their gods. The later ones followed the pattern: Uranus (Greek) and Neptune (Roman).
