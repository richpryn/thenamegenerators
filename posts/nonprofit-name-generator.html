Charity, nonprofit or foundation?

Nonprofit is mostly American wording; in the UK people say charity, not-for-profit or community group. The ending you choose tells people what kind of organisation you are:

  • Trust and Foundation: established, often grant-giving or set up in someone's name.
  • Project: hands-on and local, like a food project or a youth project.
  • Collective and Network: people working together.
  • CIC: in the UK, a community interest company is a separate legal form for social enterprises, with its own rules.

Choose a cause and the generator picks words to suit it: plates and pantries for food, paws and sanctuaries for animals, woodland and rewilding for nature.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Press Check on any name, or use the Business Name Checker, to see whether it's free as a domain and a social handle and to search the company register. Before you commit:

  • Company register: Companies House in the UK, or your state's business register in the US.
  • Trade marks: search the UK IPO or USPTO register for anyone using the name in your field.
  • Locally: search Google Maps for your area, since parents and clients will find you there first.
  • Charity registers: in England and Wales, search the Charity Commission register; charities there with an income over £5,000 have to register. "Charity" is also a sensitive word in UK company names. In the US, check the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search.

For the full process, see Business Naming Best Practices. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Avoid names close to a well-known charity. Besides confusing donors, the Charity Commission can direct a charity to change a name that's too similar to another one.

Names in memory of someone

A foundation set up in someone's memory often uses their first name, as in Sarah's Light Foundation. Choose From a name, or in memory and type the name. Talk it through with the family first, and make sure the name will still feel right on a fundraising page years from now.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good nonprofit names?
Names that say the cause or the people you help and are easy to remember: Spare a Spoon, Little Hearts Project, Woodside Animal Trust or Willow Rewild. Choose a cause for names to match.
+What makes a good nonprofit name?
Say the cause or the people you help, keep it short enough for a banner and a web address, and make sure it does not sound like a big existing charity, which can confuse donors and break the rules on charity names. A hopeful phrase plus a plain ending (Full Plates Food Project, Bright Futures Trust) works well, and a foundation set up in someone's memory often uses their name.
+How do I check a nonprofit name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company name, a domain and a social media handle. Before you commit, search the official registers too: in the UK, Companies House; in the US, your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. In England and Wales, also search the Charity Commission register; in the US, check the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search.
+Is it a charity or a nonprofit?
Nonprofit is mostly US wording. In the UK, a registered charity, a community interest company (CIC) and a not-for-profit group are different things, so use the word that matches how you're set up.
