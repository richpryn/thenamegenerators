Five genres, three formats

Each genre follows the naming habits of its kind of story:

  • Fantasy names pair an invented or rare first name with a landscape surname: Siona Moonbrook, Gareth Bramblewood.
  • Modern names mix first names and surnames from many backgrounds, the way a real school register or office does: Kian Walker, Efua Danso.
  • Historical names lean on older English choices. In the 1500s and 1600s Puritan families gave children virtue names like Prudence and Patience, and Bible names like Gideon and Ezra were common, so Gideon Whitmore sounds like he belongs in a period drama.
  • Sci-fi names are short, international and easy to say over a radio: Katya Holm, Bram Haldane.
  • Funny names sound almost plausible, which is where the joke is: Horace Snoozleby, Lettice Muddlestone.

Choose First name only when you already have a family name, Full name for a new character, or Name and a role for a quick NPC or a writing prompt.

Making the name work in your story

  • Vary your cast. Give main characters different first letters and different lengths, so readers never mix them up.
  • Say it out loud. If you'll read the story aloud or play it at a table, the name needs to be easy to say.
  • Check it isn't famous. Search the full name before you publish, in case it belongs to a well-known person or character.
  • Use the role as a prompt. "A detective inspector close to retirement" already has a plot.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good character names?
Names that fit the character's world and era, are easy to say, and stand apart from the rest of the cast. A fantasy heroine might be Elowyn Whitlow; a modern one, Rukhsana Okoye. Pick a genre and generate a few until one fits.
+What are good female fantasy character names?
Flowing first names with a landscape surname, like Siona Moonbrook, Miravel Fairwind or Celestine Starcrest. Choose Fantasy and Female for more.
+What are some funny names for characters?
Names that sound almost real, like Horace Snoozleby, Queenie Tumbleweed or Ned Wobblethwaite. Choose the Funny genre; the names work best for comic side characters.
+Can I use these names in my book or game?
Yes, they're free to use. If a character is a major one, search the full name first to make sure it doesn't belong to a famous person or another fictional character.
