How tattoo shop names work

Traditional Western tattooing grew up around sailors' tattoos, and many classic designs carry a meaning. A swallow was said to bring a sailor home, an anchor stands for steadiness, and a panther or tiger for strength. Those designs make good names because they suggest your style before anyone sees your portfolio.

  • Traditional names pair two flash designs: Clover & Eagle Tattoo.
  • Cute and fine line names are small and soft: Wren Ink, Little Snowdrop Tattoo.
  • Cool names add metal, fire or animals: Iron Serpent Tattoo.
  • Modern names use one design word: Etch & Co., Sketch Collective.

Studio, parlour and shop all work. "Collective" suits a shop with several resident artists.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Press Check on any name, or paste one into the Business Name Checker, to see whether the domain and social handles are free and to jump to the company register and trade mark searches. Then:

  • Search the company register. In the UK that's Companies House; in the US, your state's business register.
  • Search trade marks with the UK Intellectual Property Office or the USPTO, especially if you'll sell products or open more than one site.
  • Look locally. Search Google Maps for tattoo studios near you: a similar name a few streets away will confuse customers even if it's legally fine.
  • Register with the council. In the UK, tattooists and their premises must be registered or licensed with the local council before they start work, and tattooing anyone under 18 is illegal.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For more on choosing and checking a name, read our guide: Business Naming Best Practices.

Naming for artists who move

Many tattooists work as resident or guest artists in someone else's shop. If you might move, use your own name or a personal brand for your Instagram, and keep the shop name for the premises. Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good tattoo shop names?
Names with character that suggest your style: Heart & Mermaid Tattoo or Steel Swallow Tattoo for traditional work, Seedling Fine Line Tattoo for delicate pieces, or Monoline Tattoo for a modern studio.
+What are good tattoo shop names with meaning?
Traditional tattoo symbols make names with a story: an anchor stands for steadiness, a swallow for coming home safely, a rose for love, a compass or lighthouse for finding your way, and a horseshoe for luck. Pair two ("Anchor & Swallow Tattoo") or add your street or town. Keep it easy to say and spell, and check no other studio nearby uses something similar.
+How do I check a tattoo shop name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. In the UK, also search Companies House; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Check your local area too, as another business nearby with a similar name can confuse clients.
+Do I need a licence to open a tattoo shop in the UK?
Yes. Tattooists and their premises must be registered or licensed with the local council, which checks hygiene, before you start work. Contact your council's environmental health team.
