This Victorian name generator uses first names that were genuinely popular in 19th-century Britain, with English surnames, and can add a title or one of the period's more surprising jobs, like knocker-upper or mudlark.

Popular Victorian names

The most common names stayed remarkably stable through the century: William, John, George, Thomas and James for boys; Mary, Elizabeth, Sarah and Ann for girls. Around them came fashions:

  • Royal names such as Albert, Alfred and Victoria spread after the royal family made them popular.
  • Flower and jewel names, like Violet, Rose and Lily, became fashionable for girls later in the century.
  • Latin number names, like Septimus (seventh), were given in large families.
  • Florence took off after Florence Nightingale's fame in the Crimean War.

Victorian jobs

The With a Victorian job format adds a real occupation from the period:

  • Knocker-upper: woke workers by tapping on windows with a long pole before alarm clocks were common.
  • Mudlark: searched the Thames mud at low tide for anything worth selling.
  • Costermonger: sold fruit and vegetables from a barrow in the street.
  • Crossing sweeper: swept a path across muddy streets for a tip.

A job turns a name into a character straight away.

Using the names

  • Titles mattered. Mr, Mrs and Miss were used constantly; first names were for family and close friends.
  • For steampunk, pair a Victorian name with an inventor's job.
  • For earlier centuries, see the Medieval and Anglo-Saxon name generators.

Frequently Asked Questions

What were popular Victorian names?

William, John, George, Thomas and James for boys; Mary, Elizabeth, Sarah, Ann and Emma for girls, with fashions like Florence, Ada, Albert and flower names later in the century.

What was a knocker-upper?

Someone paid to wake workers by tapping on their windows with a long pole, before alarm clocks were common.

Are these real Victorian names?

Yes, the first names were all in use in 19th-century Britain. The surnames are English surnames, combined at random.
