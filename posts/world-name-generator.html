How fantasy worlds are named

Many fantasy worlds are named for what they mean to the people who live there. Norse myth has Midgard, the "middle enclosure" where people live, joined to the other worlds by the tree Yggdrasil. Tolkien's Middle-earth comes from the Old English middangeard, the same idea, and his whole world is Arda. D&D's settings have their own: Toril is the world of the Forgotten Realms, and Krynn the world of Dragonlance.

  • High fantasy names are soft and bright, with endings like -eth, -aris and -vael.
  • Dark fantasy names are English compounds of grief and decay: Dreadbarrow, Rustfall.
  • Mythic names are big ideas made into places: Stormanvil, Heavenhalls.
  • Fey names come from the hedgerow: hawthorn, bramble and thistle.

Naming your world

  • Decide who's speaking. The people of a world might call it "the earth" or "the land"; scholars and gods might have a grander name.
  • Keep it short. You'll say it a lot, so two or three syllables work best.
  • Name the planes too. A world with a fey realm next door needs both names: try a high-fantasy world and a fey court.

Then fill it in: the Continent Name Generator for its landmasses, the Island Name Generator for its seas, and the Kingdom and Village name generators for the people. For a world in space, try the Planet Name Generator.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good fantasy world names?
Short names that suit the tone: Seraneth or Ildathera for high fantasy, Mournhollow for dark fantasy, Fateloom for a world of the gods, Brackenwild for a fey realm.
+How do you name a fantasy world?
Think about what the people living there would call it, often just "the world" or "the middle land", as with Midgard and Middle-earth, then make a name that sounds like your story.
+Can I use these names for my D&D campaign?
Yes, they're made up and free to use. If you're publishing, search the name first in case another book or game already uses it.
