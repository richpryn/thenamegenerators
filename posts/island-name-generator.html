How the Island Name Generator Works

It works from 1,275 names and name parts, which combine into over 33,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Style: Any style, Fantasy, Tropical, Mysterious, Pirate, Cosy.
  • Name Format:
    • Name only, e.g. Acorn Cay
    • With a description, e.g. Isle of Old Crowns, reached by stepping stones that are never in the same place twice

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Good island names say what the island is like before anyone lands: a soft fantasy name like Isle of Vaelis, a tropical cay, a pirate's reef or a rock nobody visits twice. Every map needs a few islands, and this island name generator gives you fantasy isles for your D&D campaign, tropical cays, pirate hideouts, eerie rocks lost in the fog and cute names for the island in your cosy life-sim game, with a short description if you want a story to go with it.

How islands get their names

Real island names are full of old words for "island", which is why so many end the same way:

  • -ey and -ay come from the Old Norse ey, island. Anglesey, Orkney and Lundy all have it, and Lundy means "puffin island".
  • Holm is a small island, as in Steep Holm and Flat Holm in the Bristol Channel.
  • Ynys is Welsh for island: Anglesey is Ynys Môn in Welsh.
  • Skerry, from the Norse sker, is a rocky islet too small to live on.
  • Cay and key come from the Spanish cayo, a small, low island, which is why Florida has the Keys.
  • Atoll is a ring of coral round a lagoon. The word comes from Dhivehi, the language of the Maldives.

The generator borrows these: fantasy names use "Isle of" and the Norse ending, tropical names use cays and atolls, mysterious names use skerries and rocks, pirate names use reefs, keys and captains, and cosy names are anything you could eat with a cup of tea. Choose With a description for a line of story, like Isle of Vintrelle, where storm-mages train on the cliffs each winter.

Putting islands on your map

  • Name a chain together. Islands near each other often share a name, like "the Runic Isles", with each island named separately.
  • Let sailors name the dangerous ones. A rock that wrecks ships gets a blunt name, like Weeping Rock.
  • Give each island one thing it's known for, which is what the description format is for.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good fantasy island names?
Names with an old word for island in them, like Wizardsey (with the Norse -ey ending) or Isle of Saldrenne. Choose Fantasy, and add a description for a line of story.
+What's the difference between a cay, a key and an atoll?
A cay or key is a small, low island, often of sand or coral; both words come from the Spanish cayo. An atoll is a ring of coral round a lagoon.
+Can I use these names in a game or novel?
Yes, they're free to use. A few may match tiny real islands somewhere, so search a name first if you're publishing.
