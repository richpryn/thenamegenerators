How the Polish Name Generator Works

It works from 6,680 names and name parts, which combine into over 7,600 possible results.

The choices:

  • Gender: Any, Male, Female.
  • Popularity: Any, Popular with babies, Most common overall.
  • What you get:
    • First name and surname, e.g. Jonasz Wojtczak
    • First name only, e.g. Donata
    • Surname only, e.g. Olejnik

Where the names come from: These are real recorded names. Every first name and surname is a real name from Poland's PESEL register, published by the Ministry of Digital Affairs (Ministerstwo Cyfryzacji) on dane.gov.pl under CC0 1.0: the first names given to babies born in 2025, the first names of living people in the register in January 2026, and the most common men's and women's surnames. Full names join a real first name to a real surname of the same gender at random, so they are new people. Names in English or Ukrainian spelling (David rather than Dawid, Oleksandr rather than Aleksander) are left out, and so are the names of famous Poles.

The labels on each name: Real name a real name, found in historical records or in real use. Real parts a new combination of real names or name parts, made by this generator. Our Naming Library explains how we check names.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Polish names come with letters English doesn't have (ł, ś, ż) and surnames that change with the person they belong to. This Polish name generator uses real names from Poland's PESEL register, the national list of residents: the names parents chose for babies in 2025, the names most common across every age, and the surnames Poles actually have, in the right form for a man or a woman.

Eight good Polish names

All eight came from the generator:

  • Ignacy Nowak: Nowak is the most common surname in Poland, and Ignacy is back among the most popular boys' names.
  • Aleksandra Kamińska: a classic pairing, with the feminine -ska ending.
  • Witold Kamiński: the same surname in its masculine form, with a strong, old-fashioned first name.
  • Jagna Zielińska: Jagna is short and bright, and it is one of the names parents are choosing now.
  • Konstancja Król: król is Polish for 'king', and like Nowak it stays the same for men and women.
  • Franciszek Wójcik: Franciszek is a favourite for baby boys again, and Wójcik is one of the five most common surnames.
  • Bronisław Wiśniewski: a name with the weight of a grandfather in a family saga.
  • Karolina Zielińska: easy for English speakers to say, and very Polish all the same.

Why Polish surnames change for women

Many Polish surnames are adjectives, and in Polish an adjective agrees with the person it describes. So the masculine endings -ski, -cki and -dzki become -ska, -cka and -dzka for a woman: a man called Kowalski has a sister or wife called Kowalska. The register lists men's and women's surnames separately, and the generator gives each person a surname in their own form.

Surnames that aren't adjectives, such as Nowak, Wójcik, Mazur and Król, are the same for everyone. If you write a Polish family, keep this straight: a woman called Lewandowski will look wrong to any Polish reader.

Popular Polish names, now and across every age

Choose Popular with babies for the names given to children born in 2025, or Most common overall for the names most people living in Poland have.

  • Babies in 2025: Zofia, Zuzanna, Maja, Laura and Hanna led for girls; Nikodem, Antoni, Leon, Jan and Aleksander for boys.
  • Everyone in the register: Anna is the most common woman's name, held by over a million women, followed by Katarzyna and Małgorzata. Piotr, Krzysztof and Tomasz lead for men.
  • Old names are back. Antoni, Franciszek, Ignacy and Stanisław, names many people link with great-grandparents, were all in the boys' top ten for 2025.

For a character born in the 1970s or 80s, the Most common overall names fit better; for a child in a story set today, use Popular with babies.

Name days and short forms

Many Poles celebrate their name day (imieniny), the feast day of the saint who shares their name, and for some it matters as much as a birthday. Friends bring flowers and sing Sto lat ('a hundred years'). A story that mentions a character's name day will ring true to Polish readers.

Everyday life runs on short forms: Kuba for Jakub, Kasia for Katarzyna, Gosia for Małgorzata. Some short forms are now given as names in their own right; Kuba and Antek were both registered for babies in 2025, and you'll find them in the generator.

Getting the letters right

  • Keep the accents. Ł, ś, ć, ń, ó, ż, ź, ą and ę are separate letters in Polish, so Wójcik without the accent is a different spelling.
  • Ł sounds like an English w, so Michał ends with a w sound and Łucja starts like "woo".
  • W sounds like v, sz like sh and cz like ch: Wojciech is roughly "VOY-chekh" and Kowalczyk "ko-VAL-chik".
  • Names are real people's names. Each first name and surname belongs to many real Poles, so choose a full name that isn't a well-known person's before you use it in print.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are the most common Polish surnames?
Nowak, Kowalski, Wiśniewski, Wójcik and Kowalczyk, in that order, for both men and women (as Kowalska and Wiśniewska for women). Choose Surname only to see more of the most common surnames.
+Why do some Polish surnames end in -ski and others in -ska?
-ski is the masculine form and -ska the feminine one, because these surnames work like adjectives. Choose Male or Female and the generator gives the right form.
+What are popular Polish girl names?
In 2025 the most popular were Zofia, Zuzanna, Maja, Laura and Hanna. Choose Female and Popular with babies for more.
+What are popular Polish boy names?
Nikodem, Antoni, Leon, Jan and Aleksander led in 2025, with old favourites like Franciszek and Stanisław close behind. Choose Male and Popular with babies.
+What is the most common first name in Poland?
Anna, held by over a million women in the PESEL register; for men it is Piotr. Choose Most common overall for names like these.
+Are these real Polish names?
Yes. Every first name and surname comes from Poland's official PESEL register, published as open data. Full names are made by pairing them at random, so they aren't real individuals.
+Can I use these names for a character or a game?
Yes, they're free to use. They're real names, so if you pick a full name for a book or film, check it isn't a well-known person's.
