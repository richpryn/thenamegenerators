How the Mountain Name Generator Works

It works from 2,160 names and name parts, which combine into over 55,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Style: Any style, Fantasy, Dwarven, British, Volcanic and dark, Japanese.
  • Name Format:

    • With a description, e.g. The Drakgoth Mountains, that has smoked for a thousand years

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Good mountain names describe what you see from a distance: a shape, a colour, a danger. Dragontooth Peak tells you it's sharp, Smokemaw that it's still smoking. This mountain name generator gives you fantasy peaks and ranges for your D&D maps, dwarven mountains, British bens, fells, tors and pikes, dark volcanoes and Japanese mountains with what their names mean, with a line of description if you want one.

How real mountains got their names

Britain's hills carry words from every language spoken there:

  • Ben comes from the Gaelic beinn, mountain, as in Ben Nevis.
  • Pen is the Brittonic and Welsh word for head or top, as in Pen y Fan, and moel is Welsh for a bare hill.
  • Fell comes from the Old Norse fjall, and is how hills are named across the Lake District and the Pennines.
  • Tor, a rocky hilltop, is the word on Dartmoor, and pike means a peak in the north of England, as in Scafell Pike.

Some mountains have more than one name. Snowdon is the English "snow hill"; in Welsh it has always been Yr Wyddfa. In Japanese, yama means mountain and -take or -dake a peak, which is why the Japanese style gives you names like Suzume-dake, "sparrow peak", with the meaning alongside.

Mountains in your world

  • Name the range and the peak. A range needs one name; its tallest peak deserves another.
  • Ask who named it. Dwarves living inside a mountain will call it something different from the farmers below.
  • Use the description as a reason to go there: a lost city under the snow, a single rope bridge, a treasury nobody has seen.

For what flows down from the peaks, try the River and Lake name generators. For the woods on the lower slopes, there's the Forest Name Generator, and for what's inside the mountain, the Cave Name Generator. To name the land around it, try the Kingdom Name Generator.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for a fantasy mountain?
Names that describe a shape or a story: Dragontooth Peak, Heavenfang, The Storm-Lord's Ladder. For dwarves, try Anviltooth or Mount Draldrin; for a volcano, Burntop or Smokemaw.
+What does 'ben' mean in mountain names?
Mountain. It comes from the Gaelic beinn, and is used for Scottish peaks such as Ben Nevis.
+What's the difference between a fell, a tor and a pike?
Fell is the northern English word for a hill, from Old Norse. A tor is a rocky hilltop, especially on Dartmoor. A pike is a peak, as in Scafell Pike.
