Where the Elf on the Shelf comes from

The Elf on the Shelf began as a picture book, The Elf on the Shelf: A Christmas Tradition, self-published in 2005 by Carol Aebersold and her daughter Chanda Bell.

  • The rules: the elf watches during the day, flies back to the North Pole each night to report to Santa, and turns up somewhere new each morning.
  • No touching: in the book, an elf that's touched loses its magic, which is why children talk to it rather than pick it up.
  • The name comes first: the book says an elf only gets its magic once the family names it, so the naming is the start of the whole thing.

Choosing your elf's name

A good elf name is easy for a small child to say, and sounds like it came from the North Pole.

  • First names like Pip, Noelle or Nutmeg are the easiest for young children.
  • Full names like Juniper Tinselworth sound official, which helps when the elf leaves notes.
  • Funny names like Agent Frost or Detective Buttons Snowflake suit an elf that gets up to mischief at night.
  • Let the children choose from a few: generate a batch, read out your favourites, and let them vote.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What should I name my Elf on the Shelf?
Something short and festive that your children can say easily, like Pip, Tinsel or Nutmeg. Choose Boy or Girl, generate a batch and let the children pick.
+When does the Elf on the Shelf arrive?
Most families have the elf arrive at the start of December, or the day after Thanksgiving in the US, and leave on Christmas Eve.
+Who wrote The Elf on the Shelf?
Carol Aebersold and her daughter Chanda Bell, who self-published the book in 2005.
