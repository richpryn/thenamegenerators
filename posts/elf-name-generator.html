How the Elf Name Generator Works

It works from 1,097 names and name parts, which combine into over 16,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Gender: Male, Female, Neutral.
  • Style: depends on the gender:
    • Male and Female: Tolkien, D&D, Generic
    • Neutral: D&D, Generic
  • Starts with: depends on the style:
    • Tolkien: Any letter, A, C, E, F, G
    • D&D: Any letter, A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, K, L, M, N, O, P, R, S, T, V
    • Generic: Any letter, A, C, D, E, F, G, I, L, M, N, O, R, S, T, V

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Elf names have a sound you recognise straight away: soft consonants, long vowels and endings like -iel, -wen and -ion. This elf name generator gives you three takes on it. Tolkien gives you real elves from Middle-earth, D&D gives you names in the style of the Player's Handbook, and Generic gives you original names that don't belong to any one world.

Tolkien's elves: names with meanings

Every name in the Tolkien style is an elf from Tolkien's books, and almost all of them mean something in his Elvish languages, Sindarin and Quenya:

  • Legolas: "green leaves" (Sindarin)
  • Elrond: "star-dome"
  • Arwen: "noble maiden"
  • Celebrían: "silver queen"
  • Círdan: "shipwright", fitting for the elf who builds the ships to the West
  • Gil-galad: "star of radiance"
  • Fëanor: "spirit of fire"

Because they're real characters, fans will recognise them. That's perfect for a Middle-earth game or an elf named after a hero of old. For an original character, borrow the building blocks instead (below).

Build your own Tolkien-style elf name

Tolkien built his names from real word parts, and you can do the same:

  • gil star, galad radiance, celeb silver, mith grey, nim white
  • -wen maiden and -iel daughter, for female names (Arwen, Eärwen "sea-maiden", Galadriel)
  • -ion son, and -dil friend or lover (Eärendil, "friend of the sea")
  • -dor land, for names tied to a place

So Celebwen reads as "silver maiden" and Mithion as "grey son". Neither is in the books, but both feel at home there, and you'll know what they mean.

D&D elves: child names, adult names and family names

In Dungeons & Dragons (5th edition), elves go by a child name until they declare themselves adults, sometime after their hundredth birthday, and then choose an adult name. That's a nice detail for a character: a young elf might still be using a short child name, or be about to pick a new one.

D&D elf family names usually come with a translation, which you can use in Common: Galanodel (Moonwhisper), Liadon (Silverfrond), Holimion (Diamonddew). If you give your elf a family name, deciding what it means in Common gives other characters something to call them.

The D&D style here sounds like the Player's Handbook: flowing, with ae, ai and th, like Aelthas, Faenya and Saelvan. A few, like Naivara and Shanairra, are names the Player's Handbook itself suggests. The gender-neutral D&D names are short (Aeris, Faen, Rael), which suits child names well.

Generic elf names

The generic style is for elves outside any particular world. Male names tend to end in -ion, -or or -as (Ithilion, Calenor), female names in -iel, -wen or -a (Ariniel, Elowen, Talindra).

Gender-neutral generic names are nature words: Willow, Rowan, Wren, Briar, Ember. They work well for wood elves, druids or an elf who's taken a name in the common tongue.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Are the Tolkien names real?
Yes. Every name in the Tolkien style is an elf from Tolkien's books (The Hobbit, The Lord of the Rings and The Silmarillion). Use them for Middle-earth games or homages; for an original character, borrow Tolkien's word parts instead.
+What's the difference between the three styles?
Tolkien gives you real Middle-earth elves with meanings in Sindarin and Quenya. D&D gives you names in the style of the Player's Handbook, with ae/ai sounds and family names that translate into Common. Generic gives you original elvish-sounding names that suit any setting.
+How are male and female elf names different?
Mostly in the endings. Female names often end in -iel, -wen or -a; male names in -ion, -or, -as or a consonant. The gender-neutral names avoid those endings, using short names or nature words instead.
+Can I use a Tolkien name for my D&D elf?
In your own game, absolutely. If you're writing something to publish, invent your own name: the famous ones are too recognisable, and Tolkien's word parts give you plenty to work with.
