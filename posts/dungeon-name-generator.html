Good dungeon names tell the players what they're walking into before they light the first torch: a crypt, a mine, a wizard's tower gone wrong. This dungeon name generator makes names for D&D and other fantasy games in eight kinds, from crypts and goblin caves to wizard's lairs and shifting mazes, and can add a one-line hook to get the adventure started.

  • The Mouldering Ossuary of the Silent Order: a crypt with a backstory in the name.
  • The Webbed Hollows: a cave that warns the players about spiders.

  • The Drowned Fane of the Spider Mother: a fane is an old word for a temple.
  • The Singing Iron Workings: a mine with something strange in it.
  • The Old Greymarket Storm Drains: an under-the-city dungeon named like a real place.
  • The Maze That Breathes: a labyrinth with a name that makes players nervous.

What makes a dungeon name work

The word itself has moved. Dungeon comes from the Old French donjon, the great tower or keep of a castle, and only later came to mean the cells underneath. Since D&D was published in 1974, it has meant any place full of monsters and treasure, from a tomb to a sewer.

  • Say what it is. Catacombs, Ossuary, Warrens, Workings, Cisterns: the kind of place tells players what to expect.
  • Add one detail. Moss-Grown, Bone-Strewn, Smoke-Stained: an adjective is a description the players hear before they arrive.
  • Name it after someone for a lair or a tomb: Morwenna's Puzzle-House, The Pale Crypt of Nerith.

The eight kinds use real words for each place: an ossuary holds bones, an undercroft is the vaulted cellar under a church, an adit is the level entrance to a mine, and firedamp was the miners' name for explosive gas. Choose With a DM hook for a one-line reason to go in, like The Pit of Orvane: The local troll is reasonable, but only if you bring cheese.

Running your dungeon

  • Use the hook as the rumour the players hear in the tavern, then decide whether it's true.
  • Name the rooms too. A dungeon with a Bell Room and a Drowned Stair is easier to map and remember.
  • Link it to the world above. A mine belongs to a town; a crypt belongs to a family; a ruin belonged to someone's castle.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good D&D dungeon names?
Names that say what kind of place it is and add one detail, like The Webbed Hollows, The Singing Iron Workings or The Mouldering Ossuary of the Silent Order. Add a DM hook for a reason to go in.
+What does dungeon originally mean?
It comes from the Old French donjon, the keep or great tower of a castle. The meaning of a prison underneath came later.
+Can I use these in a published adventure?
Yes, they're free to use. Search a name first if you're publishing, in case another adventure already uses it.
