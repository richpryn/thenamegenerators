How the Fantasy District Name Generator Works

It works from 333 names and name parts, which combine into over 2,700 possible results.

The choices:

  • Part of the city: Anywhere, Trade quarter, Market, Street or lane, Rich district, Slum or rough end, Docks, Temple district, Mages' quarter.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

A city map comes alive when its districts have names people actually use. This fantasy district name generator names trade quarters, markets, streets, rich and poor districts, docks, temple precincts and mages' quarters, in the plain, practical way real old cities named their parts.

How real cities named their districts

Old city names are practical. The City of London still has Bread Street, Milk Street and a street called Poultry, named after what was sold there, and Cheapside takes its name from the Old English word for a market. York's Shambles is named after the benches butchers sold meat from. Paris has the Latin Quarter, where the university students spoke Latin.

The generator follows the same habits:

  • Trade quarters are named after the trade: the Tanners' Quarter, the Spicers' Street.
  • Markets after what they sell: the Saltmarket, West Flowermarket.
  • Streets and lanes after a landmark: Lantern Row, Moonglass Alley, Tarry Steps.
  • Rich districts sit up high: the High Terrace, Crown Hill.
  • Rough ends sound it: the Rookery was the name for crowded London slums like St Giles, and Ragfair for the old-clothes markets.

Building a city map

A city feels real when its districts disagree: a temple quarter beside the docks, a rich terrace above a slum. Generate a name for each part, then give the city a few streets that link them. If players ask for directions, "down Swan Row and past the Cloisters" sounds better than "the north district".

Frequently Asked Questions

+What do you call a section of a fantasy city?
A district, quarter or ward. A quarter often has a theme (the Mages' Quarter), a ward is an official division (the City of London still has 25), and smaller areas are named after their main street, square or market.
+How do you name fantasy streets?
After what's there or what happens there: a trade, a landmark, a sign or a person. Add a real ending like Row, Lane, Steps, Close or Court. Choose Street or lane for names like Lantern Row.
+Can I use these names for a D&D city?
Yes. They're made up and free to use in your game, map or book. For a published work, it's worth searching a name first in case another book already uses it.
