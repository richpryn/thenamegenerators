Eight good construction company names

All eight came from the generator, across its styles:

  • Fair & Square Building: a promise of honest quotes in three words.
  • Square One Construction: a catchy phrase that also means starting fresh.
  • Carter & Sons General Contractors: the classic family firm, with a trade in the surname.
  • Millbrook Builders & Joiners: a place-style name that sounds local and established.
  • Ashlar: a modern one-word name from the trade: ashlar is finely cut, squared building stone.
  • Dovetail & Co.: named after the joint, a sign of careful work.
  • Steadfast Construction Co.: strong and dependable, for bigger projects.
  • Whitfield Fine Homes: a luxury name for high-end builds and renovations.

Where good building names come from

Most building firms are named one of three ways, and each sends a different message:

  • A family name with & Sons, Brothers or Family: it says the firm has a reputation to protect. Slater and Mason started as surnames for the trades themselves.
  • A word from the trade: a keystone locks an arch together, a lintel carries the wall over a door and a truss holds up a roof. They sound skilled without being boastful.
  • A strong image: stone, oak, iron, a ridge or a summit. Stoneridge Contracting and Iron Ridge Builders suit groundworks and larger contracts.

If you specialise, say so: extensions, loft conversions, brickwork or groundworks are what customers type into a search box.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Signboards, hoardings, vans and quotes all carry the name, and each costs money the moment it's printed, so check the name first. Press Check next to any name to open the Business Name Checker, which looks it up in one go:

  • The company register: Companies House in the UK, or your state's business register in the US. A limited company's name can't be the same as one already registered.
  • Trade marks: the UK IPO and USPTO registers. Registering a company doesn't stop someone else owning the name as a trade mark.
  • A domain and social handles, so customers can find you under the same name everywhere.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, read our guide, Business Naming Best Practices.

Making the name work on site

  • Think about the site board. Your name goes outside every job you do, so a clear, short name is free advertising.
  • Keep it honest. Avoid words like Chartered or Institute in a company name: Companies House treats some words as sensitive and they need permission.
  • Leave room to grow if you might move from renovations into new builds.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good construction company names?
Names that sound solid and trustworthy: a family name like Carter & Sons, a trade word like Ashlar or Dovetail & Co., or a strong image like Steadfast Construction. Adding your town helps local customers.
+How do I check a construction company name is free?
Press Check next to any name to see if it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. Then make sure: in the UK, search Companies House and the UK IPO trade mark register; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. A quick map search for the name in your area helps too.
+What are some construction company names with meaning?
Many of the best come from the trade itself. A keystone is the central stone that locks an arch together, a cornerstone is the first stone laid, a plumb line shows a wall is truly upright and a lintel carries the wall over a door. They suggest strength and careful work.
+Should a building company name say what work it does?
If you specialise, yes: extensions, lofts or brickwork are what customers search for. A general builder can use a broader name and list the services underneath.
