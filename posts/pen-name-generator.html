  • Format:
    • Any, e.g. Agnes Hale
    • Full name, e.g. Michael Selkirk

Eight good pen names

All eight came from the generator, one from each genre plus a set of initials:

  • Rosie Hartley: romance, warm and easy to remember, with "heart" hiding in the surname.
  • Kate Hallam: crime, short and plain, the kind of name that looks right above a dark cover.
  • Isolde Thorne: fantasy, with an old legend in the first name and a sharp surname.
  • Juno Vance: sci-fi, two syllables of first name and one hard surname.
  • Agnes Crowe: horror and gothic, old-fashioned and slightly ominous.
  • Polly Puddleby: children's, bouncy and fun for a young reader to say.
  • Theodora Fairlie: literary, long, elegant and a little formal.
  • J. M. Ashworth: initials, which say nothing about who wrote the book.

Why writers use pen names

Pen names are as old as publishing, and the reasons haven't changed much:

  • To be read without assumptions. Mary Ann Evans published as George Eliot, and Charlotte, Emily and Anne Brontë first appeared as Currer, Ellis and Acton Bell. Joanne Rowling used initials for Harry Potter, reportedly so boys wouldn't be put off by a woman's name.
  • To try a different genre. Agatha Christie wrote six novels as Mary Westmacott, and J. K. Rowling writes crime as Robert Galbraith, so readers of one series know what they're getting.
  • To publish more. Stephen King wrote as Richard Bachman partly to put out more books than his publisher wanted under one name.
  • For privacy. A pen name keeps your writing apart from your day job, your family or your students.

How genre shapes a pen name

Readers judge a book by its cover, and the author's name is part of the cover. The generator matches first names and surnames to the genre:

  • Romance: soft, pretty first names and surnames with warmth in them (Hartley, Bellamy, Valentine).
  • Crime and thrillers: short, common first names and hard, one- or two-syllable surnames that look good in big capitals.
  • Fantasy: names from legend and nature, with surnames like Ravenscroft and Winterbourne.
  • Sci-fi: short, modern first names and surnames from around the world.
  • Horror and gothic: Victorian first names (Hester, Mordecai, Septimus) and surnames that sound like a churchyard.
  • Children's: friendly nicknames and playful surnames that are fun to read aloud.
  • Literary: longer, more formal names with a classic feel.

Initials work in every genre and hide the writer's gender, which is why they're common in crime, fantasy and literary fiction (P. D. James, J. R. R. Tolkien, A. S. Byatt). The generator leaves out initials that spell something unfortunate.

Before you publish under a pen name

  • Search it. Look the name up on Amazon, Goodreads and social media. A real person may share a made-up name, and you don't want to be confused with another author or anyone well known.
  • Check the handles. If you'll want a website or an author page, see whether the name is free as a domain and on the platforms you use.
  • Keep one name per genre. Many authors use a different pen name for each genre so readers don't pick up the wrong kind of book.
  • Your real name still matters. Publishers and self-publishing platforms usually let you publish under a pen name while contracts and payments use your legal name.

This is general information, not legal advice. For the people in your story, try the Character Name Generator, or the Book Title Generator for what goes above the name.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do I choose a pen name?
Start with your genre, because readers expect a romance author to sound different from a horror writer. Choose it here, generate a few batches, then search your favourites to check they're free.
+What is a good female pen name?
It depends on the genre: Rosie Hartley for romance, Kate Hallam for crime or Isolde Thorne for fantasy. Choose Female and your genre.
+What is a good gender-neutral pen name?
A neutral first name like Rowan, Ellis or Sam works, or use initials, which hide gender completely. Choose Gender-neutral, or Initials under Format.
+Should I use initials as a pen name?
Initials suit crime, fantasy and literary fiction and say nothing about who you are. Choose Initials for names like J. M. Ashworth, or Middle initial for something like Ada J. Voss.
+Is it legal to write under a pen name?
Yes. In the UK and the US you can generally publish under a pen name without registering it, as long as you aren't using it to mislead anyone. Your contracts and payments will still be in your real name.
+Are these real authors?
No. The names are made up and checked against a list of famous authors and pen names. A real person might share one, so search before you use it.
