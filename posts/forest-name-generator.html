Good forest names say what kind of wood it is before anyone walks in: Foxglove Shaw sounds like a Sunday walk, The Hungry Thicket does not. Every map needs a forest, and every forest needs a name the players will remember. This forest name generator gives you enchanted fantasy forests, elven woods, dark and haunted forests for your D&D campaign, and English woods, chases and holts named the way real ones are, with a line of description if you want one.

How real woods and forests got their names

English woods are named in old words that each meant something slightly different:

  • -hurst was a wooded hill and -holt a wood, both Old English, so they turn up in village names as well as woods.
  • Weald is the Old English for forest, and survives in the Weald of Kent and Sussex.
  • Shaw, copse and spinney are small woods; a copse was often cut back for timber.
  • A chase was hunting land held by a lord rather than the king, as at Cannock Chase.

In medieval England a "forest" wasn't always wooded at all: it was land under forest law, kept for the king's hunting. William the Conqueror made the New Forest a royal forest in the 1070s, and Sherwood simply means "shire wood". Fantasy writers borrowed the same habits. Tolkien took Mirkwood from the Old Norse Myrkviðr, and Fangorn means "beard of tree" in his Elvish.

Choose English woods for names like Atherhurst Wood, Elven for Sylumel, Dark for Shroudtangle, or Fantasy for The Wandering Wilds. With a description adds a line of story: Weepbough, which has swallowed three search parties.

Putting a forest on the map

  • Name it from the outside. Villagers on the edge of a forest name it after what they fear or use: firewood, hunting, wolves.
  • Give big forests smaller parts. A great forest might hold a named copse, a chase and a glade, each with its own story.
  • Let the description be a hook. A wood with standing stones that move is an adventure waiting to happen.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for a fantasy forest?
Names that set the mood: enchanted like Honeyweald Forest, elven like the Moonlit Woods of Ithennis, or dark like The Hungry Thicket. For an English-sounding wood, try Foxglove Shaw or Tedholt Chase.
+What's the difference between a wood, a copse and a forest?
Size, mostly. A copse or a spinney is a small wood, a wood is bigger, and a forest is bigger still. In medieval England a forest was also a legal term for royal hunting land, wooded or not.
+Can I use these names in D&D?
Yes. They're made up and free to use. Choose With a description for a forest that comes with an adventure hook.
