Good lake names say what kind of water it is, and the word for lake does half the work: a loch sounds Scottish, a tarn high and cold, a mere quiet and English. This lake name generator gives you fantasy lakes for your D&D map, British-style lochs, meres, tarns and llyns, dark and haunted waters and crystal-clear magical lakes, with a short description if you want a story to go with one.

Meres, lochs, llyns and tarns

Each part of Britain has its own word for a lake:

  • Mere is Old English, used for lakes in England, and is part of Windermere, which means the lake of a Norseman called Vinandr.
  • Loch is the Gaelic word, used across Scotland.
  • Llyn is Welsh. Welsh lake names often describe something: Llyn y Wrach is the witch's lake, Llyn Dwfn the deep lake.
  • Tarn, from the Old Norse tjörn, is a small mountain lake, common in the Lake District.

Oddly, only one of the Lake District's lakes, Bassenthwaite Lake, has "lake" in its name. The rest are meres, waters and tarns.

Choose British for names like these, Fantasy for Lake Lorrendal, Dark for Drowned Maid's Pool, or Crystal for the Starlit Pool.

Lakes in your world

  • Give a lake a reason to matter. A crossing, a border, an island fortress or something underneath the water.
  • Match the word to the region. If the hills are fells, the lakes are probably meres and tarns; if they're bens, use lochs.
  • Use the description as a rumour for an innkeeper to tell.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for a fantasy lake?
Names that suit the region and the mood: Lake Lorrendal or The Lake of the Seven Isles for fantasy, Loch Tarvach or Snipe Tarn for something British, Hangman's Mere for a dark water.
+What's the difference between a loch, a llyn, a mere and a tarn?
Loch is Scottish Gaelic, llyn is Welsh and mere is Old English, all for a lake. A tarn, from Old Norse, is a small lake high in the hills.
+Can I add a story to the lake name?
Yes. Choose With a description for a lake with a line of history or legend, like a crannog on a little island or a monster seen by every grandfather in the village.
