  • Walls & All Decorators: a play on "warts and all" that says you'll do the whole room.

  • Hampton Brush & Roller: a friendly town name. Put your own town in the box.

What makes a painting name work

In the UK the trade is usually called painting and decorating, and many firms put both words in their name. In the US it's more often just painting. The trade's own words make good names:

  • Paints and finishes: emulsion is the water-based paint for walls and ceilings, gloss and satin are finishes, and distemper is an old limewash-style paint, which suits a heritage specialist.
  • Pigments: ochre and umber are earth pigments used for centuries, which is why they sound upmarket.
  • Puns like Emulsion Impossible or Roller Derby stick in the mind. Add a straight slogan so customers know the work is careful.
  • Say what else you do: wallpapering, plastering and exteriors all come up in searches.

Check it's free before you print the sign

A painting name ends up on a van, a website, a Google Business Profile and a stack of invoices, so it's worth a few minutes' checking before any of that is printed:

  • Press Check on any name above. The Business Name Checker looks up the domain, links to the company register (Companies House in the UK) and opens the trade mark search and social handles.
  • Search your area. Look up the name on a map and in local search results. Two firms with the same name a few miles apart confuse customers and reviews.
  • Avoid names close to paint brands, such as a well-known paint maker's name or colour range, which can cause trade mark problems.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, from shortlisting to registering, read our guide to Business Naming Best Practices.

Getting the name seen

  • Make it readable on a van and a yard sign: many decorators win work from the board outside a finished house.
  • Add a slogan: choose Name with a slogan for lines like Prepared properly, painted perfectly.
  • Match the name to your prices. A pun suits friendly, affordable work, and a name like Harrington Fine Decorating suits period homes.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good painting and decorating business names?
Names that promise careful work: Prime & Proper Decorators, Walls & All Decorators, Marshall Painters & Decorators or Palette Painting Co. Add your town for local search.
+How do I check a painting business name is free?
Press Check next to any name to see if it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. Then make sure: in the UK, search Companies House and the UK IPO trade mark register; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. A quick map search for the name in your area helps too.
+What are some funny painting company names?
Good ones play on painting words that sound like everyday phrases: Pigment of Your Imagination, Emulsion Impossible, Gloss Encounters or Ceiling the Deal. Choose Funny in the Style menu for more, and add a straight slogan so customers know you do a proper job.
+Should I call myself a painter or a decorator?
In the UK most firms use both, as in Painters & Decorators, because customers search for either. Decorating usually also covers wallpapering, so use it if you hang paper.
