How the Bard Name Generator Works

It works from 414 names and name parts, which combine into over 510,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Gender:
    • Any, e.g. Dorian the Battle-Bard
    • Male, e.g. Cassius, Voice of the Charge
    • Female, e.g. Sonnet the Song-Weaver
  • College: Any college, College of Lore, College of Valor, College of Glamour, College of Swords, College of Whispers, College of Eloquence, College of Creation.
  • Name format: Name and epithet, Name only.
  • Starts with: Any letter, A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J, L, M, N, O, P, R, S, T, V, W.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Bards collect stories, so their names usually sound like they belong in one. This bard name generator gives D&D 5e bards a first name with a bit of flair and an epithet that fits their Bard College, from the well-read College of Lore to the dagger-juggling College of Swords.

The bard in D&D

A D&D bard uses music and words as magic, and their Bardic Inspiration lets allies roll an extra die.

  • Colleges: bards belong to a College, from Lore and Valor in the Player's Handbook to Glamour, Swords, Whispers, Eloquence and Creation in later books.
  • Real bards were the poets of the Celtic world, keeping the history and praising the deeds of their patrons. Wales still crowns its bards at the National Eisteddfod.

Naming a bard

A bard's name should sound good when announced at the door of an inn.

  • The name (Rosalind, Dashiell, Odette) has a bit of romance about it.
  • The epithet follows the College: a Lore bard might be "the Scholar-Singer", a Swords bard "Sword of the Stage", a Whispers bard "the Smiling Knife".

Frequently Asked Questions

+What's a good bard name?
A name with a bit of romance, like Rosalind or Dashiell, and an epithet for their College, like "the Tale-Spinner".
+What are the bard colleges?
Lore and Valor in the Player's Handbook, plus Glamour, Swords, Whispers, Eloquence and Creation in later books.
+Were bards real?
Yes. Bards were the poets of the Celtic world, and Wales still honours its bards at the National Eisteddfod.
