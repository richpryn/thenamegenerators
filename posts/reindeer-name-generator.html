Where Santa's reindeer names come from

Santa's reindeer were named in a poem: A Visit from St. Nicholas, better known as 'Twas the Night Before Christmas, published in 1823.

  • The eight: Dasher, Dancer, Prancer, Vixen, Comet, Cupid, Donner and Blitzen. The poem first called the last two Dunder and Blixem, Dutch for thunder and lightning.
  • Rudolph came much later, in 1939, when Robert L. May wrote his story for a booklet given away by the Montgomery Ward department store.
  • New names follow the same pattern: speed, light and movement. That's why the classic style has names like Frosthoof and Windlight. The famous nine are left out, so every name is new.

Real reindeer facts

Reindeer are the only deer where both males and females grow antlers.

  • Santa's team is probably female: males drop their antlers in late autumn, while females keep theirs through the winter. A reindeer with antlers on Christmas Eve is most likely a female.
  • Same animal, two names: in North America wild reindeer are called caribou.
  • Built for snow: their hooves spread wide on snow and their noses warm the cold air before it reaches their lungs.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are Santa's reindeer called?
Dasher, Dancer, Prancer, Vixen, Comet, Cupid, Donner and Blitzen, from the 1823 poem 'Twas the Night Before Christmas, plus Rudolph, who was added in 1939.
+Are Santa's reindeer male or female?
Probably female. Male reindeer shed their antlers in late autumn, but females keep theirs through the winter, so reindeer with antlers at Christmas are most likely female.
+What's a good name for a new reindeer?
Something that sounds fast or bright, like Frosthoof or Sparkstep. Choose the funny style for names like Speedy McSleighface.
