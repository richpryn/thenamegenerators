How the Irish Name Generator Works

It works from 8,767 names and name parts, which combine into over 15,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Gender: Any, Male, Female.
  • Era: Any, Popular now, 1990s and 2000s, 1960s and 1970s.
  • What you get:
    • First name and surname, e.g. Tom Kelly
    • First name only, e.g. Arabella
    • Surname only, e.g. Riordan

Where the names come from: These are real recorded names. First names come from the Central Statistics Office (CSO), Irish Babies' Names (tables VSA50 and VSA60, CC BY 4.0): names registered for babies in Ireland from 1964 to 2025, spelt as they were registered. Surnames come from Robert E. Matheson's Special Report on Surnames in Ireland (Registrar-General for Ireland, 1894, public domain), which lists the surnames with five or more births in the 1890 index. Full names join a real first name to a real surname at random, so they are new people. Every surname in Matheson's table is kept, including English ones such as Taylor and Turner, names recorded without their O' (Brien, Neill) and names that are also ordinary words (Hope, Silk); only the report's headings, county names and misreadings in the scans are left out, along with famous people's full names.

The labels on each name: Real parts a new combination of real names or name parts, made by this generator. Our Naming Library explains how we check names.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Irish names range from Irish-language names like Tadhg, Saoirse and Fiadh to the Johns, Marys and Patricks of older generations. This Irish name generator uses the first names actually registered for babies in Ireland since 1964, so you can choose the names of children born now or of their parents and grandparents, with surnames from Ireland's own records.

Eight good Irish names

All eight came from the generator, across the three eras:

  • Tadhg Quinn: Tadhg is one of the most popular boys' names today, and Quinn is found all over Ireland.
  • Rían Phelan: Rían was the most registered boys' name of 2025.
  • Saoirse McGrath: Saoirse is Irish for 'freedom', and a modern favourite.
  • Fiadh O'Hare: Fiadh has been near the top of the girls' list for years, and O'Hare is a northern name.
  • Diarmuid Cronin: an Irish-language first name with a Cork surname, for someone born in the 1990s.
  • Siobhan Dempsey: Siobhan, spelt as the CSO lists it for the 1990s, without the fada.
  • Declan Brady: a name for a character born in the 1960s or 70s.
  • Mairead Foley: Mairead is the Irish form of Margaret, one of the commonest girls' names of that generation.

Irish-language names and their spellings

Many of the most popular names in Ireland today are Irish-language names: Tadhg, Oisín, Fionn and Cillian for boys, Fiadh, Éabha, Saoirse and Caoimhe for girls. The accent is called a fada and it changes the sound: Seán is "shawn", where Sean in English could be read as "seen".

The CSO counts every spelling as its own name, so Rían and Rian, or Róisín and Roisin, appear separately, and the generator keeps them as they were registered. The CSO figures only show fadas from 2018 on, which is why the 1960s and 1970s names give you Siobhan and Grainne rather than Siobhán and Gráinne. Choose whichever spelling suits your character: a fada can say something about a family.

How Irish surnames work

  • O' comes from the Irish Ó, 'grandson' or 'descendant': O'Brien, O'Neill, O'Donnell.
  • Mac and Mc mean 'son': McCarthy, McGrath, McNamara.
  • Fitz came with the Normans and also means 'son of': Fitzgerald, Fitzpatrick.

By the 1890s many families had dropped the O' or Mac, and Matheson's report lists names such as Sullivan and Connor without them. In Irish, surnames also change for women: a daughter's Ó becomes Ní and Mac becomes Nic, so the daughter of Seán Ó Dónaill is Ní Dhónaill. The generator gives surnames in the English form, which is the same for everyone.

Names by era

Irish naming changed fast after the 1960s, so the era you choose says a lot about a character.

  • 1960s and 1970s: Mary, Catherine and Margaret for girls; John, Michael and Patrick for boys, with names like Declan, Brendan and Niall alongside.
  • 1990s and 2000s: Sarah, Emma, Aoife, Ciara and Niamh; Sean, Conor, James and Jack.
  • Popular now (2016 to 2025): Emily, Grace, Sophie and Fiadh; Jack, James, Noah, Fionn and Tadhg.

A family across three generations might be a Patrick, his daughter Aoife and her son Fionn.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are popular Irish girl names?
In 2025 Lily, Éabha and Fiadh were the top three, with Grace, Sadie and Sophie close behind. Choose Female and Popular now for more.
+What are popular Irish boy names?
Rían, Jack and Noah led in 2025, with Oisín, Fionn and Tadhg in the top ten. Choose Male and Popular now.
+What are some old Irish names?
For people born in the 1960s and 70s: Mary, Catherine, Bridget and Siobhan; John, Patrick, Declan and Brendan. Choose 1960s and 1970s.
+What are the most common Irish surnames?
Murphy, Kelly, Walsh, Byrne, Ryan, O'Brien and Smith are among the most common. Choose Surname only; common names come up most often.
+What do O' and Mac mean in Irish surnames?
O' comes from Ó, 'grandson' or 'descendant', and Mac means 'son'. In Irish, women use Ní and Nic instead.
+Why do some names have accents?
The accent is a fada, part of Irish spelling. The generator keeps names spelt the way they were registered, with or without the fada.
+Are these real Irish names?
Yes: the first names were all registered for babies in Ireland and the surnames come from Irish birth records. Full names are paired at random, so they aren't real people.
