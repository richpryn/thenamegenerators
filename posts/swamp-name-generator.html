How the Swamp Name Generator Works

It works from 1,973 names and name parts, which combine into over 46,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Style: Any style, Fantasy, Haunted, British fen, Bayou, Witchy.
  • Name format:
    • Name only, e.g. The Bleak Fens
    • With a description, e.g. The Wandering Mires, where a sleeping dragon lies under the peat

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Good swamp names sound wet, old and a little unwelcoming: Sedgeslough, Gloammere, the Fen of Cold Candles. Every good fantasy map has a stretch of marsh nobody wants to cross. This swamp name generator gives you fantasy marshes, haunted bogs, real-sounding British fens, carrs and mosses, Louisiana bayous and witchy mires, with a short description if you want one.

Eight good swamp names

All eight came from the generator, across its five styles:

  • Gloammere: a fantasy marsh named for the dusk light over still water.
  • Newtmarsh: short, damp and full of small creatures.
  • The Fen of Cold Candles: a haunted fen with lights you shouldn't follow.
  • Will-o'-Wisp Fen: named after the marsh lights of English folklore.
  • Wytheley Carr: a British fen name built from real place-name parts.
  • Black Drove Mire: a drove is an old track across the fens.
  • Pirogue Bayou: a bayou named after the flat-bottomed boats used there.
  • Bog Myrtle Slough: a witchy marsh named after a real bog plant.

Fens, carrs, mosses and bayous

English has a word for almost every kind of wet ground, and most of them are old:

  • Fen comes from Old English. The Fens of East Anglia were mostly drained for farming from the 1600s, and their drained fields still carry names like -drove and -ings (from the Norse for meadow).
  • Carr, from Old Norse, is wet woodland, usually alder and willow.
  • Moss is the northern English and Scots word for a peat bog, as in Chat Moss near Manchester.
  • Levels are low, flat land by the sea or a river, like the Somerset Levels.
  • Bayou is Louisiana French, borrowed from the Choctaw word for a slow stream, and the Bayou style uses Cajun French surnames and swamp wildlife.

Fiction has its own famous swamps: Tolkien's Dead Marshes are the template for a marsh full of something you shouldn't look at. The Haunted style borrows from folklore instead: a hinkypunk is an old West Country name for a will-o'-the-wisp, and a gytrash is a northern English spirit of lonely roads.

Using swamps on your map

  • Put them where water gathers: at a river mouth, behind a sand bar, or in a valley with nowhere to drain.
  • Give them a reason to cross. A causeway, a ferry or a shortcut makes a marsh a place for adventures, not just a blank space.
  • Choose With a description for a ready-made hook, like Lilymarsh, where travellers follow the glow-moths or get lost.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good swamp names?
Names that sound wet and old: Gloammere, Newtmarsh, Black Drove Mire, or the Fen of Cold Candles for a haunted one. Choose British fen for real-sounding names, or Bayou for Louisiana.
+What's the difference between a fen, a bog and a marsh?
Broadly, a marsh is wet grassland, a fen is a peat wetland fed by ground water and a bog is one fed by rain. In place names the words are used loosely, so pick the one that sounds right.
+What is a carr?
A wet woodland of alder or willow. The word comes from Old Norse and is common in place names across northern and eastern England.
