Where dragon names come from

  • Dragon comes from the Greek drakōn, a great serpent. Old English had draca (which gives us drake) and wyrm, and Old Norse ormr: all words for snakes before they were words for dragons.
  • Norse myth has the great named dragons: Fáfnir, a dwarf turned into a dragon by greed for gold and killed by Sigurd; Níðhöggr, who gnaws at the roots of the world tree; and Jörmungandr, the serpent that circles the world.
  • Beowulf's dragon has no name at all. It wakes when a thief steals a cup from its hoard, and burns the land in revenge.
  • Chinese dragons are a different creature: long, wingless, linked to water, rain and good fortune rather than fire and greed.

Famous dragons and their names

  • Smaug: Tolkien called the name "a low philological jest", from an old Germanic verb meaning to squeeze through a hole. Bilbo, flattering him, calls him Smaug the Tremendous.
  • Glaurung, Tolkien's first dragon, is called the Father of Dragons.
  • Balerion the Black Dread, in Game of Thrones, shows how much an epithet adds. Daenerys named her own dragons after family: Drogon after Khal Drogo, Rhaegal and Viserion after her brothers.
  • Odahviing, in Skyrim, is a name and a Shout at once: in the dragon language it means "snow hunter wing". Every dragon name in the game is three words of power.

Seven kinds, two sounds

The start of each name carries the element and the ending carries the sound:

  • Fire draws on words for burning and forges (ign-, pyr-, and Surt- after the fire giant of Norse myth). Frost on Norse and Greek words for ice and snow (hrim-, khion-). Storm, earth, shadow, sea and venom each have their own.
  • Harsh endings (-uzul, -agor, -arax) give a growling, ancient name for something that burns villages.
  • Sleek endings (-yra, -essa, -ith) give a smoother name, for a cunning dragon, a young one, or one that reads as female.

Giving a dragon a title

Dragons in stories rarely have just one name. They collect titles from the people who fear them (the Black Dread), from what they've done (Burner of Cities), and from flattery: Bilbo calls Smaug the Chiefest and Greatest of Calamities to keep him talking.

Ursula Le Guin's Earthsea goes further: a dragon's true name gives power over it, so the name everyone uses is only a mask. A dragon with a public title and a secret name is a ready-made quest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which kind should I pick for a D&D dragon?

Match the breath weapon: fire for a red dragon, frost for a white, storm for a blue, venom for a green. For a black dragon (acid) try venom or shadow, and for the metallic dragons pick by home: sea for bronze, earth for copper, fire for brass, or frost for silver.

Are there male and female dragon names?

The names suit any dragon. The harsh sound tends to read as male and the sleek one as female, so choose the sound that fits your dragon.

Are these dragons from books or games?

No. The names are built from parts, and famous dragons like Smaug, Alduin and Drogon aren't in the lists, so your dragon won't be mistaken for one.

Can I use these names in D&D or my book?

Yes, they're free to use and you don't need to credit the site.
