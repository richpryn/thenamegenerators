How the Dragon Name Generator Works

It works from 10,633 names and name parts, which combine into over 4,300,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Sound: Harsh, Sleek, Female.
  • Kind of Dragon: Any, Fire, Frost, Storm, Earth and mountain, Shadow, Sea, Venom and swamp, Light, Primordial.
  • Name Format:
    • Name only, e.g. Temarax
    • Name and epithet, e.g. Vharakar the Wildfire
    • Species, e.g. Hemlockscale
  • Starts with: depends on the name format:
    • Name only and Name and epithet: Any letter, A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J, K, L, M, N, O, P, R, S, T, U, V, Y, Z
    • Species: Any letter, A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, K, L, M, N, P, R, S, T, V, W

Where the names come from: Every name is invented. Each name joins a root for its element, mostly from Greek, Latin or Norse words (ign- for fire, hrim- for frost), to an invented ending. None is a dragon from a book, film or game.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

A dragon's name should sound old, heavy and slightly dangerous to say out loud. This dragon name generator builds names from sounds that suit each kind of dragon, from fire and frost to sea and venom, harsh or sleek. Add an epithet and your dragon gets a reputation too: the Ashen, Bane of Kings, Lord Beneath the Mountain.

Where dragon names come from

  • Dragon comes from the Greek drakōn, a great serpent. Old English had draca (which gives us drake) and wyrm, and Old Norse ormr: all words for snakes before they were words for dragons.
  • Norse myth has the great named dragons: Fáfnir, a dwarf turned into a dragon by greed for gold and killed by Sigurd; Níðhöggr, who gnaws at the roots of the world tree; and Jörmungandr, the serpent that circles the world.
  • Beowulf's dragon has no name at all. It wakes when a thief steals a cup from its hoard, and burns the land in revenge.
  • Chinese dragons are a different creature: long, wingless, linked to water, rain and good fortune rather than fire and greed.

Famous dragons and their names

  • Smaug: Tolkien called the name "a low philological jest", from an old Germanic verb meaning to squeeze through a hole. Bilbo, flattering him, calls him Smaug the Tremendous.
  • Glaurung, Tolkien's first dragon, is called the Father of Dragons.
  • Balerion the Black Dread, in Game of Thrones, shows how much an epithet adds. Daenerys named her own dragons after family: Drogon after Khal Drogo, Rhaegal and Viserion after her brothers.
  • Odahviing, in Skyrim, is a name and a Shout at once: in the dragon language it means "snow hunter wing". Every dragon name in the game is three words of power.

Nine kinds, two sounds

The start of each name carries the element and the ending carries the sound:

  • Fire draws on words for burning and forges (ign-, pyr-, and Surt- after the fire giant of Norse myth). Frost on Norse and Greek words for ice and snow (hrim-, khion-). Storm, earth, shadow, sea and venom each have their own. Light dragons take their names from words for dawn and brightness, like Phosara, Keeper of the Morning, and primordial dragons sound like the oldest things alive: Ormrorvax, the First of All Dragons.
  • Harsh endings (-uzul, -agor, -arax) give a growling, ancient name for something that burns villages.
  • Sleek endings (-yra, -essa, -ith) give a smoother name, for a cunning dragon or a young one.
  • Female names have their own endings, like -ova, -isse, -ynne and -iel: Lysora, Helisse, Brontynne. With an epithet they become Solisse the Molten or Fyria, Breaker of Castles.

Giving a dragon a title

Dragons in stories rarely have just one name. They collect titles from the people who fear them (the Black Dread), from what they've done (Burner of Cities), and from flattery: Bilbo calls Smaug the Chiefest and Greatest of Calamities to keep him talking.

Ursula Le Guin's Earthsea goes further: a dragon's true name gives power over it, so the name everyone uses is only a mask. A dragon with a public title and a secret name is a ready-made quest.

Dragon species names

Some stories name dragons by species rather than one by one. The How to Train Your Dragon books by Cressida Cowell, and the DreamWorks films made from them, are the best-known example: each kind of dragon gets a name that describes how it looks or what it does.

Choose Species for original names in that style. None of them are dragons from the films:

  • A body part: Crag Clubtail, Lavaspine, Toxinhorn.
  • A habit: Tufted Smoke Gouger, Prism Rammer, Morning Snapper.
  • A place, like a real animal's common name: Marsh Clubtail, Western Hailstone Brute.

Give a species name to a whole kind of dragon, then use the other formats to name the individuals: a Marsh Clubtail called Grunova. This site isn't connected to DreamWorks or the How to Train Your Dragon books.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Which kind should I pick for a D&D dragon?
Match the breath weapon: fire for a red dragon, frost for a white, storm for a blue, venom for a green. For a black dragon (acid) try venom or shadow, and for the metallic dragons pick by home: sea for bronze, earth for copper, fire for brass, or frost for silver.
+Are there male and female dragon names?
The names suit any dragon. The harsh sound tends to read as male and the sleek one as female, so choose the sound that fits your dragon.
+Are these dragons from books or games?
No. The names are built from parts, and famous dragons like Smaug, Alduin and Drogon aren't in the lists, so your dragon won't be mistaken for one.
+Can I use these names in D&D or my book?
Yes, they're free to use and you don't need to credit the site.
+How do I make a How to Train Your Dragon style dragon name?
Describe the dragon the way a naturalist would: a body part, a habit or the place it lives, like Crag Clubtail, Prism Rammer or Western Hailstone Brute. Choose Species for original names in that style.
+What are good female dragon names?
Names with softer endings like -ova, -isse and -ynne: Lysora, Helisse, Brontynne. Choose Female under Sound, and add an epithet for something like Solisse the Molten.
