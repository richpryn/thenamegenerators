How law firms are named

Most law firms are named after people. A partnership traditionally takes the surnames of its founders, and many keep them long after those partners have gone, because the name carries the reputation. Newer and smaller practices are freer: a town, a single word or a phrase about clarity works, provided it doesn't overstate what you are.

  • Be accurate about size. "& Partners" or "Group" on a one-person practice can mislead clients.
  • Don't promise outcomes. Words like "Win" or "Guaranteed" invite complaints and, for regulated lawyers, regulatory trouble.
  • Say what you do if you specialise. A family or immigration practice is easier to find when the name says so.

Protected words: in England and Wales, "solicitor" and "barrister" are protected titles. Solicitors' firms are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), whose rules say a firm's name must not be misleading, and barristers by the Bar Standards Board. In the US, each state bar sets the rules for firm names. The names here leave out "Solicitors" and "Attorneys": add the word yourself only if you are entitled to use it.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Every name here has a Check button. Press it before you order business cards, and run through these yourself:

  • The company register: Companies House in the UK, or your state's business register in the US.
  • Trade marks: search the UK IPO or USPTO for the same or a similar name in your field.
  • Domains and handles: a matching .co.uk or .com, and the same handle on the platforms your clients use.
  • Your regulator: if you are a solicitor, check your firm name against the SRA's rules; in the US, check your state bar's rules on trade names.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good law firm names?
Founders' surnames joined with an ampersand (Pickering & Chalmers), one surname with Law (Ormsby Law), a town (Hampton Legal Centre) or a calm modern word (Kestrel Law). Keep it plain and honest about who you are.
+What should I name my law firm?
Most firms use the founders' surnames ("Hartley & Cobb", "Hartley Law"), which clients trust and which grows with you. A solo lawyer can use their own name or the town; a practice that does one thing, such as family or immigration law, does well to say so. Keep it plain and honest: a name must never promise results or suggest a size you aren't.
+Can I put "Solicitors" or "Attorneys" in my firm's name?
Only if you are entitled to. In England and Wales, "solicitor" and "barrister" are protected titles: solicitors' firms are regulated by the SRA, whose rules say a firm's name must not be misleading, and barristers by the Bar Standards Board. In the US, each state bar sets the rules for firm names, and many limit trade names and phrases like "& Associates". The names here leave those words out, so add them yourself only if they apply to you. In a UK company name, "Trust" and "Chartered" are sensitive words that need approval.
+How do I check a law firm name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. In the UK, search Companies House; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Check your local area too, as two similar names nearby confuse customers.
