How real and fictional stations are named

Real space stations have mostly been given short, hopeful names:

  • Salyut, Russian for "salute", was the name of the Soviet Union's first stations; Salyut 1 launched in 1971.
  • Mir, which flew from 1986 to 2001, means both "peace" and "world" in Russian.
  • Skylab, America's first station (1973), was named for what it was: a laboratory in the sky.
  • The International Space Station kept a plain, descriptive name because it belongs to several countries at once.
  • Tiangong, China's station, means "heavenly palace".

Fiction tends to go one of two ways: a code or number, like Deep Space Nine and Babylon 5, or a name that tells you who runs it. The generator does both:

  • Military stations are forts, pickets and fleet yards, often with a callsign: Watchpost Yankee Eight, Glaive Station.
  • Research stations are arrays and listening posts named after scientists or scientific words: Nyberg-Brightman Array, Station Calculus.
  • Trading hubs borrow from spices and old coins: The Paprika Market, Guilder Emporium.
  • Mining outposts name the ore and the hole: Bismuth Refinery 52, Gallagher's Motherlode.
  • Luxury stations sound like grand hotels: The Aurelian Retreat, Marchmont Casino & Spa.
  • Derelict stations are named after the last thing anyone said, or felt: Lusk's Silence, Kessler's Regret.

Using a station in your story

  • Give it a nickname. Crews never use the official name: Bismuth Refinery 52 becomes "the Fifty-Two".
  • Let the name date it. A station named after a war, a founder or a company tells players how old it is and who built it.
  • Use the description as a hook. "Warmer than it should be" is the start of a session.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for a space station?
Names that say what the station does: Fort Castellano for a military post, Listening Post Fulcrum for research, Freeport Allspice for trade, Mica Vein for a mine, Château Orrery for luxury, or Station Threnody for a derelict.
+What were real space stations called?
Salyut and Mir (Soviet and Russian), Skylab (American), the International Space Station, and China's Tiangong, which means heavenly palace.
+Can I get a story hook with the name?
Yes. Choose With a description for a line like "where salvage crews refuse to go past the third deck".
