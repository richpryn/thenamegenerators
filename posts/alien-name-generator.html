The best alien names sound like they come from a real language: one with its own sounds, which a human mouth can only nearly manage. This alien name generator gives each kind of alien its own sound, from the clicks of a hive species to the soft vowels of an ocean world, and can add a role and a home.

How science fiction names its aliens

  • Give a species its own sounds. Star Trek's Klingon was built as a real language by the linguist Marc Okrand, full of harsh sounds English rarely uses. Vulcan names follow a pattern too: men's often begin with S (Spock, Sarek), women's with T' (T'Pol).
  • The apostrophe usually stands for a sound English doesn't write: a click, or a catch in the throat. Use it consistently and it means something; scatter it and it's decoration.
  • Names humans can't say. Lovecraft called Cthulhu's name a human attempt at sounds no human throat could make. Some aliens in fiction just give humans a nickname to use instead.
  • Names that are descriptions. Some fictional species name themselves with phrases, like a translated title. It's an easy way to make an alien feel truly different.

Four kinds of alien

  • Insectoid or hive: hard clicks and buzzes, with an apostrophe for the click. K'rrak, Ts'zek.
  • Aquatic: open vowels and liquid sounds, as if spoken underwater. Ulawae, Lioloa.
  • Energy or crystal: beings who name themselves by what they do. Hums-at-Dawn, Folds-Through-Glass.
  • Galactic empire: long, grand names for admirals and consuls. Velion, Sarianthe.

Building a species

  • Pick a sound and stick to it. Every member of a species should sound related. Generate a batch, keep the ones that feel like one language, and bin the rest.
  • Let the name show the body. A species with mandibles can't say m or b; a species that sings might have names in two tones.
  • Give them a word for us. What an alien species calls humans says more about them than anything else.
  • Give them a ship and a crew. Name their vessels with the Spaceship Name Generator, their machines with the Robot Name Generator, and the humans they meet with the Cyberpunk Name Generator.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What makes a good alien name?
Sounds that go together and don't sound like English, used consistently across a species. Short, odd names are easier for readers to remember than long ones.
+Why do alien names have apostrophes?
In science fiction an apostrophe usually marks a sound English doesn't write, like a click or a glottal stop. Here, the insectoid names use it that way.
+Can I use these names in my game or story?
Yes, they're free to use and you don't need to credit the site.
+How do you name an alien character?
Start with what the species is like, then give its names a sound to match: clicks and hard consonants for an insectoid, soft vowels for an aquatic species. Keep the same sounds across the whole species so their names feel related. Choose a kind of alien here for names built that way.
+How do you come up with a good alien name?
Avoid random letters. A good alien name is easy to say out loud, has a sound that fits the species, and uses one or two unusual touches, like an apostrophe or a doubled vowel, rather than many.
