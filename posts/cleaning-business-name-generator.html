What makes a cleaning name work

Cleaning is a trust business: people are letting you into their homes or offices, often with a key. The name should sound tidy and dependable before anyone reads your reviews.

  • Domestic or commercial? Homeowners respond to warm, friendly names like Sunny Side Maids. Offices and facilities managers look for names that sound like a contract they can sign: Blueline Facility Services, Vantage Office Cleaning.
  • Puns work, up to a point. Mop Stars and Done & Dusted are memorable on a van, but a commercial client may prefer something plainer. Pick one style and keep it.
  • Say what you clean if you specialise: carpets, windows, end-of-tenancy or ovens. Customers search for exactly those words.
  • Your town or your name helps local search and makes you sound like a real person rather than a franchise.

Choose Name with a slogan to pair a name with a line for your leaflet, such as Rise & Shine – Home is where the clean is.

Check it's free before you print the sign

A van wrap, uniforms and a stack of leaflets all carry the name, and each costs money the moment it's printed, so check the name first. Press Check next to any name to open the Business Name Checker, which looks it up in one go:

  • The company register: Companies House in the UK, or your state's business register in the US. A limited company's name can't be the same as one already registered.
  • Trade marks: the UK IPO and USPTO registers. Registering a company doesn't stop someone else owning the name as a trade mark.
  • A domain and social handles, so customers can find you under the same name everywhere.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, read our guide, Business Naming Best Practices.

Getting the name seen

  • Keep it readable at a distance. A van is read at 30 miles an hour, so short names and big letters win.
  • Set up a Google Business Profile with the exact same name, so map searches and reviews all point to you.
  • Match the handle. If the full name isn't free on Instagram or Facebook, add your town rather than random numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good cleaning business names?
Short, friendly names that hint at a clean result, like Dust Till Dawn, Little Wren Cleaning or Keystone Contract Cleaning for offices. Adding your town or first name, as in Tidy Homes by Sarah, helps local customers find and trust you.
+How do I check a cleaning business name is free?
Press Check next to any name to see if it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. Then make sure: in the UK, search Companies House and the UK IPO trade mark register; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. A quick map search for the name in your area helps too.
+What's a good slogan for a cleaning business?
Keep it short and promise the result, not the work: We clean, you relax or Your home, spotless. Choose Name with a slogan in the Format menu to see names paired with one.
+Should I put my own name in my cleaning business name?
It often helps for a small, local business: customers like knowing who's coming to their home. If you plan to grow and take on staff, a name without your own in it may be easier to sell or expand later.
