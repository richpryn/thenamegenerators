How photographers name their business

Clients book a photographer for their style as much as their skill, so the name should match the pictures they'll see on your website.

  • Your own name is the most common choice, because people book a person: Sarah James Photography. It's easy to find, but harder to sell or grow into a studio with other photographers.
  • Puns and plays on camera words (focus, snap, developing, shutter) suit family, pet and event work: Snap Decision Portraits.
  • Soft nature and material words (linen, clay, clover, film) suit an Instagram aesthetic and a light, airy style.
  • French and fine-art words (Maison, Atelier, Fine Art) signal a luxury price.

Add the kind of work you do if you specialise. Clients usually search for "wedding photographer" or "newborn photographer" plus their town, and a name with those words in it matches what they type.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Every name here has a Check button, which opens the Business Name Checker: it looks up domains, social handles, the company register and trade mark searches in one place. Before you order signs or cards:

  • Company register. In the UK, search Companies House; a limited company's name can't be the same as one already registered. In the US, search your state's business register.
  • Trade marks. Search the UK Intellectual Property Office or the USPTO for marks in your line of work. Registering a company doesn't give you trade mark rights.
  • Domain and handles. Try the .co.uk or .com and the Instagram and Facebook names at the same time, and change the name slightly rather than settle for a messy handle.
  • Local search. Look up the name with your town. Two similar businesses a few miles apart confuse customers and reviews.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, read Business Naming Best Practices.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good photography business names?
Names that suit the pictures you take: a pun like Hocus Focus Photo Co. for family work, a soft name like Linen & Grain for a film-style look, or your own name for weddings. Pick a style and field to get names that fit.
+Should I name my photography business after myself?
Many photographers do, because clients book a person as much as a business: "Sarah James Photography" tells them who will turn up. A name that isn't your own is easier to sell or grow into a studio with other photographers. Try the "With my name" style for both shapes.
+Should I put "wedding" or "newborn" in my business name?
Only if that's all you plan to shoot. It helps people searching for that kind of photographer find you, but it's awkward if you later want to do families or events.
+How do I check a photography business name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. In the UK, search Companies House; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Check your local area too, as two similar names nearby confuse customers.
