Mecha fiction has two great traditions. Real robots are mass-produced military machines with model numbers; super robots are one-of-a-kind heroes with names you shout before the finishing move. This mecha name generator names both, with a model number, prefix or class if you want one.

Real robots and super robots

Japanese mecha fiction has two great traditions, and they name their machines differently.

  • Super robots are one-of-a-kind heroes with powers no factory could make. Tetsujin 28-go (1956) and Go Nagai's Mazinger Z (1972) set the pattern: a grand coined name, often shouted as the robot launches.
  • Real robots began with Mobile Suit Gundam (1979): mass-produced military machines with model numbers, ammunition limits and maintenance crews, named like real aircraft.
  • Mecha is short for mechanical, and in Japan the word covers almost any machine.

Naming your mecha

  • Real robot names are model codes and callsigns: KR-07 Harrier, with a class like Heavy Assault Frame.
  • Super robot names are big coined words, often ending in -on or -ion: Voltaxion, Great Kronagon, with a title like the Steel Titan.
  • The famous names are left out, from Gundam to Mazinger, along with the BattleTech and Titanfall names.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What's the difference between a real robot and a super robot?
Real robots are mass-produced military machines with model numbers, like the mobile suits of Gundam. Super robots are unique heroes with special powers, like Mazinger Z.
+What does mecha mean?
It's short for mechanical. In English it usually means giant piloted robots, especially from Japanese anime and games.
+What's a good name for a mech?
A model code and a callsign for a real robot, like KR-07 Harrier, or a grand coined name for a super robot, like Voltaxion.
