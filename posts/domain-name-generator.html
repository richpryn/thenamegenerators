What makes a good domain name

  • It's short. Aim for 15 letters or fewer: short names are easier to type on a phone and fit on a business card. The Short style keeps to eight letters or fewer.
  • It passes the say-it-aloud test. Say the name to a friend and ask them to type it. If they ask "with a hyphen?" or "how do you spell that?", try another. That's why there are no hyphens, numbers or odd spellings here.
  • It's spelt the same in the UK and the US. Words like colour and color, or centre and center, send some of your visitors to the wrong address, so the generator leaves them out.
  • It reads well in lowercase. Capitals don't count in a web address, so BrightLeaf.com and brightleaf.com are the same. Capitals help people read the name on a sign or a logo, but check the lowercase version too: words run together can spell something you didn't mean.
  • It isn't someone else's brand. A trade mark owner can complain about a domain that copies their mark and ask for it to be handed over, so the generator leaves out brands, chains and famous names.

.com, .co.uk or something else?

.com is still the ending most people type out of habit, wherever they live, so get it if you can. If your customers are mostly in the UK, .co.uk tells them you're a UK business, and the shorter .uk ending is there too. Many businesses register the .com and their country's domain and point one at the other.

Newer endings such as .shop, .store and .blog are often easier to find free, but visitors may still type .com, so look at who owns the .com before you print anything. The Check button looks up the .com and the main endings for your country, such as .co.uk and .uk in the UK.

Building a name from your keyword

Choose Your keyword and type one word that says what you do, such as bakery, yoga or plumbing. The generator puts a friendly word in front of it (GetBakery, BrightBakery) or a building-block word after it (BakeryHub, BakeryLab). Names like these tell visitors what you do before they click, which helps a small local business.

Only the first word you type is used, so choose the one that matters most. Keyword names are popular, which means the .com is more often taken, so try a few brandable names alongside them. Whatever you type, read the lowercase version before you buy: we've checked our own word lists, but we can't check every word you might add.

Check it's free before you buy

The names here are free to use, but that doesn't mean the domain is free. Every name has a Check button, which opens the Business Name Checker. It asks each domain registry's public records whether the .com and your country's endings are registered, and links to social handles, the company register and trade mark searches.

  • "Looks free" means the registry has no record of the domain. Premium and reserved names can still show as free, so your registrar's search has the final word.
  • Trade marks. A free domain can still clash with a trade mark. Search the UK Intellectual Property Office or the USPTO for marks in your line of work.
  • Handles. Check the same name on the social sites you'll use, so your address and your handle match.
  • Don't wait too long. Domains go to whoever registers them first, so once you've decided, register it.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For a blog, the Blog Name Generator has names sorted by topic; for a product, try the Brand Name Generator, and for a startup, the Company Name Generator.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What is a good domain name?
One that's short (15 letters or fewer), easy to say and spell, with no hyphens or numbers, and not close to anyone's brand. Choose Brandable for names like CloverLane, or Short for names of eight letters or fewer.
+How do I check if a domain name is available?
Press Check next to any name. It looks up the .com and your country's domains in the registries' public records, shows whether each looks free, and links to social handle and trade mark searches.
+Should I get a .com or a .co.uk?
If you can, get both and point one at the other. A .co.uk suits a business with UK customers; if you sell worldwide, the .com matters more because people type it out of habit.
+How do I make a domain name from a keyword?
Choose Your keyword and type one word that describes your business, such as bakery. You'll get names like GetBakery and BakeryHub.
+What is a good domain name for a blog?
Choose Blog for names like KettleMusings, which sound like a journal rather than a shop. The Blog Name Generator has more blog names, sorted by topic.
+Should a domain name have hyphens or numbers?
Usually not. People forget the hyphen, and when they hear a number they don't know whether to type "4" or "four". None of the names here use them.
+Can I use these domain names for free?
Yes, they're free to use and you don't need to credit the site. Press Check, then look at trade marks and the company register before you buy the domain or build a brand on it.
