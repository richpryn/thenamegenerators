How the Song Title Generator Works

It works from 100 titles and name parts, which combine into over 6,800 possible results.

Each press gives you 8, 16 or 32 names (choose under "How many"), and you won't see the same one twice until you've been through everything for the choices you've made.

A song title is a hook before anyone has heard a note. This song title generator gives you titles in the shapes songwriters use most: a single striking word, an action with a story in it ("Chasing Satellites"), or a place ("Rooftop Lullaby"). Use one as a title, or as a prompt to write from.

Titles that stick

  • Use the hook. Most memorable titles are the line people sing back: if the chorus repeats a phrase, that's often your title.
  • One word can carry a song if it's vivid: Static, Undertow, Wildfire.
  • A small story beats a big idea. "Waiting for the Last Train" says more than "Loneliness".
  • Search it. Titles aren't protected, but a title shared by a huge hit will bury yours in search results.

Using a title as a prompt

Songwriters often start from a title and write towards it. Generate a few, pick the one that makes you ask a question (who is chasing satellites, and why?), and answer it in the verses.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can song titles be copyrighted?

Generally no: titles are too short to be protected by copyright, which is why many songs share a title. A famous title may still bury yours in search results, so check first. This isn't legal advice.

Should the title be in the lyrics?

Usually, yes. Listeners remember songs by the words they sing, so a title that matches the hook is easier to find.
