This dragonborn name generator builds full names in that order, or gives you a personal name, a clan name or a childhood nickname on its own.

How dragonborn names work

The fifth-edition Player's Handbook (2014) gives dragonborn three kinds of name:

  • A clan name, said first. Dragonborn society is built around clans, and a dragonborn's standing depends on the clan's honour, so putting it first is a statement of loyalty.
  • A personal name, given at birth.
  • A childhood name, a nickname used among clutchmates, either describing them or said with affection. Some stick for life.

So where a human would say "Aldric Thorne", a dragonborn says the equivalent of "Thorne Aldric". The generator's full names follow that order: Morrikathul Kerrak is Kerrak of clan Morrikathul.

What the names sound like

  • Clan names are long, often four or five syllables, with rolling and hissing sounds: -thul, -andor, -iax, -esk. They sound old, because they are.
  • Personal names are short and hard, usually two syllables built on rr, zh, kh and sh: Rhazkar, Torvash, Jhessa.
  • Female names tend to end in a vowel (Kalira, Rhiza, Uzhara); male names in a consonant (Ghazrin, Zorvash).
  • Childhood names are plain words, often in Common: Quickclaw, Bookworm, Runt. They describe a habit or a moment from the egg-clutch years.

All the names here are new, built from those patterns, rather than copied from the Player's Handbook.

Using the clan in your story

The clan name is the most useful part of a dragonborn's name for roleplay. A dragonborn who has dishonoured their clan, or been cast out of it, has a decision to make every time they introduce themselves: say the clan name, hide it or drop it.

  • Proud: uses the full name every time, and expects others to know the clan.
  • Exiled: goes by the personal name or childhood name alone, and changes the subject when asked.
  • Clanless: some dragonborn, raised away from other dragonborn, never learned a clan name at all. Use "Personal name only" and let the party wonder.

Draconic ancestry (red, gold, silver and so on) doesn't change how the name is built, but it's a good guide to sound: a red-blooded clan might favour harsher names than a silver one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do dragonborn say their clan name first?

In D&D's Player's Handbook, dragonborn put their clan name before their personal name as a mark of honour, because a dragonborn's place in the world depends on their clan.

What is a dragonborn childhood name?

A nickname used among clutchmates, the dragonborn they grew up with. It's usually a plain descriptive word, like Quickclaw or Bookworm, and some dragonborn keep it as adults.

Are these names from the Player's Handbook?

No. They're new names in the same style, so your dragonborn won't share a name with an example from the book.

Can I use these in Baldur's Gate 3?

Yes. BG3 uses fifth-edition dragonborn, and you can type any name in the character creator. Clan-first full names can be long, so a personal name alone may fit the name box better.
