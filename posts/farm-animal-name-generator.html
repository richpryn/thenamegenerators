Naming each kind of animal

Pick an animal and the names follow its noises and habits:

  • Lambs and sheep: soft, small names, and baa puns. A lamb raised by hand is often called a pet lamb or, in parts of England, a cade lamb.
  • Cows and calves: moo puns like Moorgaret and Moonica, or a grand title like Prince Cudsworth, after the cud cows chew.
  • Pigs and piglets: classic names like Opal and Huxley, or wildflower names like Speedwell and Meadowsweet.
  • Chickens: cluck puns for hens and grand titles for a cockerel: Sir Clucksalot, Lord Crowington.
  • Ponies, horses and donkeys: sensible names that work on a stable door, like Annabel or Basil.
  • Goats: a young goat is a kid, so the cute goat names are extra small: Pea, Wiggle.
  • Ducks and geese: waddles and honks: Lorraine Waddleton, Countess Honksalot.

Names for a flock, a herd or a toy farm

  • Naming several at once? Pick a theme, like flowers for the hens or grand titles for the geese, so they sound like a family.
  • On a working farm, short names are easier to call across a field.
  • For a toy farm or a story, the funny names give each animal a personality before the game starts.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are cute names for farm animals?
Small, soft names like Thimble, Button, Teacup or Little Bleat. Choose Cute and the animal you're naming.
+What are funny names for chickens?
Cluck puns and grand titles, like Sir Clucksalot, Henelope, Duchess Scratchington or Cluckingham Palace for the hen house. Choose Chicken and Funny.
+What's a baby goat called?
A kid. A baby cow is a calf, a baby pig a piglet, a young duck a duckling and a young goose a gosling.
