A villain's name should make the audience uneasy before the villain does anything. Some use a grand title (Doctor Doom, Baron Zemo, Lord Voldemort), some an ordinary word made sinister (The Joker, The Riddler), and some a name with an epithet. This villain name generator makes all three, in six styles from classic and supervillain to dark lord, game boss, sci-fi and funny, for stories, games, comics and campaigns.

What makes a villain name work

  • A title claims power. Doctor, Baron, Lord, Countess: the villain thinks they deserve respect, and wants you to know it.
  • An ordinary job made sinister is unsettling: The Gardener, The Clockmaker, The Collector. The audience wonders what exactly they collect.
  • Hard sounds help: k, x, v and z appear in a lot of villain names for a reason.
  • An epithet tells the legend: the Undying, the Usurper. Good for fantasy villains with a history.

Types of villains

Most villains fit one of a handful of types, and each suits a different kind of name. These examples all came from the generator:

  • The mastermind plans everything and is rarely in the room when it happens: Madame Enigma, The Diamond Mastermind.
  • The dark lord rules a realm, an army or the end of the world: Zarkhul the Thousand-Eyed, Lazmira, Queen of Ash.
  • The corporate villain does terrible things through paperwork: Director Oren Talbek, Consul Freja Teague.
  • The boss guards a place the heroes have to get through: Gnash, Lord of the Sewers, Eskar, Warden of the Deep Mines.
  • The rival wants the same thing as the hero, and has the manners to match: Duke Mordant.
  • The monster barely needs a name at all, just a reputation: The Locust, Murk the Many-Armed.
  • The tragic villain was something else once, and the name keeps the wound: Sela Talbek the Uploaded, Calvaine the Grave-Born.
  • The petty villain is a comedy of small crimes: Mildred the Pettiest, The Last-Biscuit Taker.

Not every story needs a villain, but most need an antagonist: whoever stands in the hero's way. A rival, a rule or a storm can be an antagonist. A villain is an antagonist who chooses to do harm.

Six styles of villain name

  • Classic: grand titles and ordinary jobs made sinister. Duke Mordant, The Quiltmaker, Fester the Hungering.
  • Supervillain: comic-book names built from science, disaster and a title. Baroness Tungsten, Mister Calamity, The Static Saboteur.
  • Dark lord: harsh invented names with an epithet and a realm, for dark lords and demon lords alike. Archon Ruvaine, Gulgrath the Eternal, Emperor of Cinders.
  • Evil boss: the game boss, named for the place you fight them or the job they do there. Rusk, Tyrant of the Iron Bridge, Ringmaster Scrag, The Blind Jailer.
  • Sci-fi: admirals, directors and things that used to be people. Admiral Kasimir Corvan, The White Signal.
  • Funny: small-scale evil for comedy and children's stories. Countess Mildew, The Wheelie Bin Hijacker, General Ever-So-Slightly-Evil.

Choose Female or Male for titles to match (Countess or Count, Empress or Emperor), and a format for a title and name, a The… name, or a name with an epithet.

Naming the villain for your story

  • Who gave them the name? A name the villain chose (Lord Voldemort, an anagram of Tom Marvolo Riddle's name) tells you about their ego; a name the newspapers gave them tells you about their crimes.
  • Match the hero. A villain's name often mirrors the hero's: light and shadow, order and chaos.
  • Save the real name for the reveal. The name everyone knows can hide a more ordinary person underneath.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do I name a villain?
Pick a shape: a grand title and a name (Baron Malice), an ordinary job made sinister (The Clockmaker), or a name with a dark epithet (Vex the Undying). Then decide who gave them the name, and why.
+Can I use these villain names in my book or game?
Yes, free and with no credit needed. Check a name isn't already a well-known character before you publish.
+What are good villain names?
Names that make people uneasy before the villain does anything: a grand title like Duke Mordant, an ordinary job made sinister like The Quiltmaker, or a name with a dark epithet like Zarkhul the Thousand-Eyed. Pick a style to match your story.
+What's the difference between a villain and an antagonist?
An antagonist is whoever opposes the protagonist, and they don't have to be evil: a rival, a parent or a storm can all be antagonists. A villain is an antagonist who means harm.
+What are good demon lord names?
Harsh names with an epithet and a realm, like Lazmira, Queen of Ash or Gulgrath the Eternal, Emperor of Cinders. Choose the Dark lord style. For lesser demons, try the Demon Name Generator.
+What are good evil boss names?
Name a game boss after where you fight them or what they guard: Gnash, Lord of the Sewers, Rusk, Tyrant of the Iron Bridge or The Blind Jailer. For a corporate villain, the Sci-fi style gives names like Director Oren Talbek.
