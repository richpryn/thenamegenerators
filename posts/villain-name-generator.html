A villain's name should make the audience uneasy before the villain does anything. Some use a grand title (Doctor Doom, Baron Zemo, Lord Voldemort), some an ordinary word made sinister (The Joker, The Riddler), and some a name with an epithet. This villain name generator makes all three, for stories, games, comics and campaigns.

What makes a villain name work

  • A title claims power. Doctor, Baron, Lord, Countess: the villain thinks they deserve respect, and wants you to know it.
  • An ordinary job made sinister is unsettling: The Gardener, The Clockmaker, The Collector. The audience wonders what exactly they collect.
  • Hard sounds help: k, x, v and z appear in a lot of villain names for a reason.
  • An epithet tells the legend: the Undying, the Usurper. Good for fantasy villains with a history.

Naming the villain for your story

  • Who gave them the name? A name the villain chose (Lord Voldemort, an anagram of Tom Marvolo Riddle's name) tells you about their ego; a name the newspapers gave them tells you about their crimes.
  • Match the hero. A villain's name often mirrors the hero's: light and shadow, order and chaos.
  • Save the real name for the reveal. The name everyone knows can hide a more ordinary person underneath.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I name a villain?

Pick a shape: a grand title and a name (Baron Malice), an ordinary job made sinister (The Clockmaker), or a name with a dark epithet (Vex the Undying). Then decide who gave them the name, and why.

Can I use these villain names in my book or game?

Yes, free and with no credit needed. Check a name isn't already a well-known character before you publish.
