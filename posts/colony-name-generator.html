Eight good colony names

All eight came from the generator, across its six styles:

  • New Seville, whose founders planted a tree for every child born on the voyage: a pioneer colony named after home, the oldest habit there is.
  • Goodmorrow Hollow: a hopeful name for a settlement's first morning.
  • Claim Block 11, whose residents voted to unionise, and the head office has gone very quiet: a company town with its politics in the name.
  • Camp Redfern, where every family has a medal in a drawer: a military colony that remembers its war.
  • Paco Assembly, a free town with no money, no locks and no police: a utopian commune with an Esperanto name: paco means peace.
  • Bent Nail Coulee: a frontier camp named after something somebody found there.
  • Spit and Wire Junction, where the doctor is a vet and nobody minds: the frontier at its scrappiest.
  • Mess Hall Meadow, whose elders still feel dizzy under an open sky: a generation-ship colony named after the part of the ship people remember.

How colonies get their names

Real colonies were named in a few predictable ways, and science fiction has borrowed all of them:

  • After home, with New in front: New York, New Zealand, Nova Scotia ("New Scotland").
  • After a patron: Virginia was named after Elizabeth I, the Virgin Queen, and Georgia after George II.
  • After a hope or a virtue: settlers named towns Providence, Concord and Harmony.
  • After the company: company towns took the name of whoever owned the mine or the mill.

The generator follows the same habits, style by style:

  • Hopeful pioneers get New-names and sunny compounds: Larksong Vale, Morrowacre.
  • Corporate colonies are lots, sectors and slogans: Lot 18, Efficiency Village.
  • Military colonies are camps and musters, named after officers or kit: Garrison Shako, Pargeter's Muster.
  • Utopian communes are named after an ideal: Long Table Commune, Open Sky Mutual. A few use Esperanto, the language L. L. Zamenhof published in 1887 to be shared by everyone: Espero (hope), Amikeco (friendship), Libereco (freedom).
  • Frontier camps are named like gold-rush towns, after luck, junk and landmarks: Dead Battery Butte, Purdy's Dugout.
  • Generation ship colonies are named after the ship that carried them for generations, or the part of it people remember: Cryo Meadow, Airlock Wood.

Naming your colony

  • Decide who founded it. A company, an army and a commune would never choose the same name.
  • Let the name age. "Claim Block 37B" might be called "Thirty-Seven" by its third generation.
  • For RimWorld and other colony sims, pick a style that matches your starting scenario, then let the description suggest your colonists' first story.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for a colony?
Names that tell you who founded it: New Seville for hopeful pioneers, Claim Block 37B for a company, Camp Redfern for the military, Open Sky Mutual for a commune, Hard Luck Basin for the frontier, or Cryo Meadow for a generation ship.
+What's a good RimWorld colony name?
Match it to your start. A tribal or scrappy start suits a frontier name like Bent Nail Coulee; a well-funded start suits a hopeful one like Goodmorrow Hollow; a crashed ship suits a generation-ship name like Airlock Wood.
+What do the Esperanto names mean?
Espero means hope, Amikeco friendship, Libereco freedom, Frateco brotherhood, Lumo light, Suno sun, Paco peace and Komunumo community.
