How tech companies name themselves

Some of the biggest tech names are borrowed real words: Apple, Amazon, Oracle. Google began as a play on googol, the number one followed by a hundred zeros. A real word is easier to spell and remember than an invented one with dropped vowels, and a suffix like Labs, Software, Digital or Systems says what kind of company it is.

Startups often use domains like .io, which is technically the country code for the British Indian Ocean Territory, when the .com is taken. That works for a software product; for local IT support, a plain name and a .co.uk or .com with your town in it will do more for you. Choose software, apps or IT services and the generator adjusts its words.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Every name here has a Check button, which opens the Business Name Checker: it looks up domains, social handles, the company register and trade mark searches in one place. Before you order signs or cards:

  • Company register. In the UK, search Companies House; a limited company's name can't be the same as one already registered. In the US, search your state's business register.
  • Trade marks. Search the UK Intellectual Property Office or the USPTO for marks in your line of work. Registering a company doesn't give you trade mark rights.
  • Domain and handles. Try the .co.uk or .com and the main social handles at the same time, and adjust the name rather than settle for a messy handle.
  • Local search. Look the name up with your town, as two similar firms a few miles apart confuse clients and reviews.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, read Business Naming Best Practices.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good tech company names?
Short real words with a clear suffix, like Matchbox Labs or Yak Software, or plain service names like Holmwood Computer Services for local IT support. Avoid odd spellings that are hard to type.
+What should I name my tech company?
A short, real word that's easy to spell ("Kettle", "Otter") with "Labs", "Software" or "HQ" after it is the classic startup shape, and leaves room to change product. If you fix computers for local people and firms, say so ("Northgate IT Support"): customers search for what you do and where. Avoid odd spellings and dropped vowels, which make your domain and handle hard to find.
+Should my tech company use a .io domain?
It's common for software startups when the .com is taken, but customers outside tech may not know it. For local IT services, a .co.uk or .com is safer.
+How do I check a tech company name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. In the UK, search Companies House; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Check your local area too, as two similar names nearby confuse customers.
