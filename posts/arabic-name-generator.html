Where the names come from: These are real historical names. Every first name is a real given name taken from Wikidata (CC0): given-name items with an Arabic spelling, borne by people from Arab League countries. Each one was checked by hand, written in one simple English spelling and kept with its Arabic spelling. European names were left out; names linked to a notorious figure, such as Saddam or Yazid, are kept, since other people have them too, but that figure's own full name never comes up. Names with ibn, bint, Abu or Umm are new combinations of those real names, and any that match a well-known person on Wikipedia, including the Prophet's family, were removed. No meanings are shown, because a meaning is only worth giving when it is certain.

The labels on each name: Real name a real name, found in historical records or in real use. Real parts a new combination of real names or name parts, made by this generator. Our Naming Library explains how we check names.

Arabic names are some of the most widely used in the world, from Muhammad and Yusuf to Layla and Maryam. This Arabic name generator draws on 601 real first names recorded for people from Arabic-speaking countries, each with its Arabic spelling, and can build them into the traditional forms: a name with the father's name (Hamza ibn Ahmad), a parent's name with Abu or Umm (Umm Yusuf) or a full classical name. Use it for a character, for a story set in the Arab world or just to explore how the names work.

Eight good Arabic names

  • Layla: a classic girl's name that is just as familiar in Britain as in the Arab world.
  • Yusuf: the Arabic form of Joseph, and far more popular in England and Wales now than in the 1990s.
  • Nur al-Din: a laqab-style name built with al-Din, 'of the faith'.
  • Abd al-Rahman: Abd, 'servant', joined to one of the names of God.
  • Hind bint Taha: a short, old girl's name with her father's name after bint, 'daughter of'.
  • Haidar ibn Saad: the same pattern for a son, with ibn.
  • Umm Marwan: a mother known by her son's name, as people often are among friends and neighbours.
  • Umm Ziad Khadija bint Mustafa: a full classical name, in the order kunya, ism, nasab.

How Arabic names are built

A traditional Arabic name is a chain of parts rather than a first name and a surname. The parts have names of their own:

  • Ism: the personal name, such as Khalid or Salma. Most are ordinary Arabic words: Muhammad means 'praiseworthy' and Ali 'exalted'.
  • Nasab: the line of descent, with ibn ('son of', often written bin) or bint ('daughter of'). Khalid ibn Adil is Khalid, son of Adil, and the chain can go back to a grandfather and beyond.
  • Kunya: Abu ('father of') or Umm ('mother of') with the name of a child, in theory the first-born. It's a familiar but respectful way to address someone, and it can also be a nickname that refers to no real child.
  • Laqab: an epithet or title. Many are compounds ending in al-Din, 'of the faith', like Nur al-Din and Salah al-Din, which later became ordinary first names.
  • Nisba: where a family comes from, such as al-Halabi, from Aleppo.

In most Arab countries today, everyday and official names leave out ibn and bint: the father's name simply follows the first name, then a family name. Some, especially in the Gulf, still write bin, as in the names of the Saudi royal family.

Abd names and names of God

One of the most common kinds of Arabic name joins Abd, 'servant', to the word for God or to one of God's names: Abdullah is 'servant of God' and Abd al-Rahman 'servant of the Most Merciful'. Abd is not usually joined to the name of the Prophet or anyone else, out of respect, so this generator only ever puts Abd with a name of God. The two words belong together: someone called Abd al-Karim is never just "Abd", and "Karim" alone is a different name.

Spellings in English vary a lot (Abd al-Aziz, Abdul Aziz, Abdulaziz), because Arabic has sounds and joined-up words that English letters don't show. This page uses one simple spelling for each name, without accents, and gives the Arabic beside it when you choose With Arabic script.

Arabic names, Muslim names and Arab Christian names

Arabic names and Muslim names overlap but aren't the same thing. Muslims around the world often choose Arabic names, especially the names of prophets and early Muslims, such as Ibrahim, Musa, Maryam and Khadija. Many Muslims also use Persian, Turkish, Urdu, Malay or other names. Arab Christians share most Arabic names too, along with Arabic forms of saints' names such as Butrus (Peter), and names like Bishara, 'good news'.

In England and Wales, Muhammad was the most popular boys' name of 2025, counting that spelling alone, and Yusuf, Ibrahim and Musa have all climbed a long way since the 1990s, according to the Office for National Statistics.

Using a name for a character

  • Pick the right form for the setting. A full classical name like Abu Basil Yusuf ibn Faraj suits a story set centuries ago; a modern character would more often go by a first name, the father's name and a family name.
  • Let other characters use different parts. Family and friends use the ism, neighbours might say Umm Yusuf, and official papers give the full name.
  • Names of the Prophet's family and early Muslims are common, everyday names, but treat them with the same respect real people would.
  • Naming a baby? Check the spelling and the meaning with family or someone who speaks Arabic well, since English spellings can blur two different names.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Is this a Muslim name generator too?
Yes, in the sense that most of these names are widely used by Muslims, including Muhammad, Fatima, Yusuf and Maryam. But they are Arabic names, also used by Arab Christians, and many Muslims use names from other languages.
+How do I get Arabic boy names or girl names?
Choose Boy names or Girl names, then First name under Name style for single names like Bilal or Kawthar.
+What do ibn and bint mean in a name?
Ibn means 'son of' and bint 'daughter of', so Rawan bint Zuhair is Rawan, daughter of Zuhair. Choose With father to get names in this form.
+What does Abu or Umm mean before a name?
Abu means 'father of' and Umm 'mother of'. Together with a child's name, usually the eldest son's, they make a kunya such as Abu Badr or Umm Dawud. Choose Abu or Umm.
+Can I see the names written in Arabic?
Yes. Set Writing to With Arabic script and every name comes with its Arabic spelling, such as Layla (ليلى). Arabic reads from right to left.
+Why are there no meanings?
Many names have more than one explanation, and a wrong meaning is worse than none, so the generator shows names only. Check a meaning in a good dictionary or with an Arabic speaker before you rely on it.
+Are these real names?
Yes. Every first name is a real given name recorded on Wikidata for people from Arabic-speaking countries, and it shows the Real name badge. Names with ibn, bint, Abu or Umm join real names into new combinations, marked Real parts.
