Real ghost stories name their ghosts in a few simple ways: by colour (the Grey Lady, the Brown Lady), by who they were (the Drummer, the Monk), or by where they're seen. This ghost name generator makes names in those patterns, along with Irish banshees, restless Victorian spirits and friendly ghosts for Halloween.

How real ghosts got their names

  • By colour: Grey Ladies are reported at castles and country houses all over Britain. The Brown Lady of Raynham Hall became famous after a photograph published in 1936.
  • By who they were: drummers, monks and nuns appear in many local legends, usually tied to an old story about how they died.
  • By place: Anne Boleyn is said to haunt the Tower of London, where she was executed in 1536.
  • By what they do: the Headless Horseman of Washington Irving's The Legend of Sleepy Hollow (1820), or the poltergeist, German for "noisy ghost".

Banshees

The banshee comes from Irish folklore: in Irish she is the bean sí, "woman of the fairy mound". She doesn't harm anyone. Her keening, a high mourning cry, warns a family that one of them is about to die.

  • Old families: tradition says she keens only for the oldest Irish families, and the names most often given are the O'Neills, O'Briens, O'Connors, O'Gradys and Kavanaghs.
  • How she's seen: sometimes a young woman, sometimes an old one, often combing her long hair. In some tales she is a washer at the ford, washing the clothes of the one who will die.

The Banshee style gives her a modern Irish first name with a keening epithet. The banshee is part of a living tradition, so treat her kindly.

Using the names

  • For a story or game, give the ghost a name the locals use (the Piper of Gallows Hill) and a real name someone has to find out.
  • For Halloween, the friendly ghost names suit costumes, decorations and pets.
  • For a haunted house, a colour and a figure is all it takes: every old house deserves a Grey Lady.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Why are so many ghosts called the Grey Lady?
Because witnesses describe what they see: a pale, grey figure in a long dress. The name stuck at many castles and houses across Britain.
+What does poltergeist mean?
Noisy ghost, from German: poltern (to make noise) and Geist (ghost).
+What's a good name for a friendly ghost?
Something soft and short: Wisp, Boo, Flicker or Glimmer. Choose "Friendly ghost".
+What is a banshee?
A spirit from Irish folklore, the bean sí or "woman of the fairy mound", whose keening cry warns a family of a coming death. She's a mourner, not a monster.
