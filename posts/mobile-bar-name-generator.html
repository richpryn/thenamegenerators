What makes a mobile bar name work

Your name goes on quotes, supplier lists and the vehicle, so it has to work in all three:

  • Name the vehicle (horsebox, prosecco van, tuk tuk, bar cart) because that's what couples and planners search for.
  • Give the van a name, like a pet. It makes the bar the star of the photos.
  • Weddings or corporate? Romantic and fizzy names suit weddings; a plain surname name suits office parties and conferences.

In England and Wales, selling alcohol at an event usually needs either the venue's premises licence or a Temporary Event Notice, and a personal licence helps. In the US, rules vary by state and many events use the caterer's or venue's licence, so check locally.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Press Check next to any name, or paste it into the Business Name Checker, to see whether the domain and social handles are free. Then search the company register (Companies House in the UK, your state's business register in the US) and the trade mark register for your kind of business. Wedding suppliers trade on Instagram, so make sure the handle is free before you letter the horsebox.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, read Business Naming Best Practices.

Tips for mobile bars

  • Big, simple lettering reads in the background of every wedding photo.
  • Avoid drink brands. Names of real gins or proseccos are trade marks.
  • Cover the area you serve in your bio, so planners know you'll travel.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for a mobile bar?
Puns about towing, horses and weddings (Hitched & Poured Bar Co., Fizz in a Field), a pet name for the van (Daisy the Bar Cart), or a classy wedding name like The Gold Leaf Wedding Bar.
+How do I check a mobile bar name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company name, a domain and a social media handle. Before you commit, search the official registers too: in the UK, Companies House and the UK trade mark register; in the US, your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Look on a map app for businesses with the same name nearby.
+What are catchy names for a mobile bartending business?
Puns about towing, horses and weddings work well for horsebox bars and prosecco vans (Tow & Tonic, Bridle & Bubbles), and a cute name for the van itself is easy to remember (Betty the Horsebox). For corporate events, a plain name with your surname reads as professional (Ashby Bar Services). Say what you do in your bio and on the van, so couples and planners can find you.
+Do I need a licence for a mobile bar?
Usually, yes. In England and Wales you'll normally need the venue's premises licence or a Temporary Event Notice to sell alcohol. Check the rules where you trade, as they differ in Scotland and in each US state.
