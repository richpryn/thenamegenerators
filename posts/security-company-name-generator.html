Strong, not scary

Customers are trusting you with their homes, staff and events, so the best names sound calm and capable. Surnames and towns with "Security" are the classic choice; tough natural images (oak, ironbark, owls, bulldogs) give you a logo; puns suit an alarm fitter better than a guarding firm. Say what you do if you can: "Guarding", "CCTV", "Alarms" or "Fire & Security" helps the right customers find you.

Licences: in the UK, people working as security guards, door supervisors, public-space CCTV operators, key holders and close protection officers need an SIA licence, and firms can join the SIA's Approved Contractor Scheme. Alarm and CCTV installers often gain NSI or SSAIB certification. In the US, most states license security guard companies. Never use words that suggest the police or government, such as "Police" or "Official".

Check it's free before you print the sign

Every name here has a Check button. Press it before you order business cards, and run through these yourself:

  • The company register: Companies House in the UK, or your state's business register in the US.
  • Trade marks: search the UK IPO or USPTO for the same or a similar name in your field.
  • Domains and handles: a matching .co.uk or .com, and the same handle on the platforms your clients use.
  • Licence names: make sure the name you trade under matches the one on your licences and approvals.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good security company names?
Calm, dependable names: a surname or town with Security (Hensley Security Group), a strong natural image (Ironbark Security Co.) or a reassuring phrase (Sleep Easy Security Co.).
+What's a good name for a security company?
Customers are trusting you with their homes, staff and events, so the name should sound steady and dependable rather than aggressive. A surname or town with "Security" is the classic choice ("Hartley Security Services"); strong words like Bastion or animals like Bulldog and Owl make a memorable logo; a light pun ("Sure Lock Security") suits an alarm fitter more than a guarding firm. Say what you do if you can: "Guarding", "CCTV" or "Alarms" helps the right customers find you.
+Do I need a licence to use "Security" in my business name?
Not for the word itself, but for the work. In the UK, security guards, door supervisors, public-space CCTV operators, key holders and close protection officers need an SIA licence, and firms can join the SIA's Approved Contractor Scheme; alarm and CCTV installers often gain NSI or SSAIB certification. In the US, most states license security guard companies. Never use words that suggest the police or government, or claim an approval you don't hold.
+How do I check a security company name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. In the UK, search Companies House; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Check your local area too, as two similar names nearby confuse customers.
