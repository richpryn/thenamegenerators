The ogre comes from fairy tales, where it is a huge, hungry giant who eats travellers, and from D&D, where ogres live in tribes and fight with clubs. This ogre name generator makes brutish names, fairy-tale ogres, D&D ogres with a tribe and funny ogres who mostly want lunch.

Where ogres come from

The word ogre first appears in French fairy tales, most famously in Charles Perrault's collection of 1697.

  • Perrault's ogres eat people, especially children: the ogre outwitted in "Puss in Boots" and the ogre in "Hop-o'-My-Thumb", whose seven-league boots are stolen.
  • An ogress appears in Perrault's "Sleeping Beauty", as the prince's mother, who wants to eat her grandchildren.
  • The origin of the word is uncertain. One suggestion is Orcus, a Roman god of the underworld.
  • In D&D, ogres are huge, greedy and live in tribes, which is why tribe names suit them.

Naming an ogre

  • Brutish ogres get short, thick names: Grubnak, Smashra.
  • Fairy-tale ogres get an old, local name: Crookshin of Thornwood.
  • D&D ogres get a tribe: Bolga of the Skull Rock.
  • Funny ogres are friendlier than they look: Sir Snackalot, Little Nigel.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Where does the word ogre come from?
It first appears in French fairy tales, most famously Charles Perrault's of 1697. Its origin is uncertain; one idea is the Roman underworld god Orcus.
+What's the difference between an ogre and a troll?
Ogres come from French fairy tales and eat people; trolls come from Scandinavian folklore and live in the mountains. In D&D, trolls regenerate and ogres don't.
+What's a funny name for an ogre?
Choose the funny style for names like Sir Snackalot, Mighty Mavis or Little Nigel.
