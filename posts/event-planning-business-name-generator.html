What event planning names say about you

Couples, parents and companies hire planners for very different things, and the name is how they tell you apart before they read your website.

  • Weddings and premium events suit calm, polished names: Event Design, Occasions or Weddings, with a material or colour like linen, pearl or gold. The Pearl Table sounds like someone who folds napkins properly.
  • Children's and family parties suit cheerful names with a party word in them: Tiny Sparkler Party Planning, Ribbon & Fizz.
  • Corporate events suit plain, confident names: a surname or place plus Events, like Everett Events.
  • A mix of everything suits a broad word like Celebrations or Event Co., so you don't turn work away because of your name.

Short names fit an Instagram handle and a business card, and they're easier to say when you're answering the phone at a venue.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Every name here has a Check button, which opens the Business Name Checker: it looks up domains, social handles, the company register and trade mark searches in one place. Before you order signs or cards:

  • Company register. In the UK, search Companies House; a limited company's name can't be the same as one already registered. In the US, search your state's business register.
  • Trade marks. Search the UK Intellectual Property Office or the USPTO for marks in your line of work. Registering a company doesn't give you trade mark rights.
  • Domain and handles. Try the .co.uk or .com and the Instagram and Facebook names at the same time, and change the name slightly rather than settle for a messy handle.
  • Local search. Look up the name with your town. Two similar businesses a few miles apart confuse customers and reviews.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, read Business Naming Best Practices.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What's a good name for an event planning business?
One that says what you plan and suits your clients. "Events", "Event Co." and "Celebrations" fit most planners; "Party Planning" says children's and family parties; "Event Design" and "Occasions" sound premium. Keep it short enough for a business card and an Instagram handle, and avoid tying it to one type of event if you want to grow.
+What should I call a party planning business?
Something cheerful with a party word in it, like Glitter & Lemonade or Tiny Sparkler Party Planning. Choose the Cute or Catchy style for names like these.
+Should I name my event business after myself?
It works well if clients are booking you personally, as many wedding planners find: Sarah Rose Events tells them who they'll be working with. A name that isn't yours is easier to grow into a team or sell later.
+How do I check an event planning business name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. In the UK, search Companies House; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Check your local area too, as two similar names nearby confuse customers.
