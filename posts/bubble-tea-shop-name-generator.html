What bubble tea names borrow from

Bubble tea comes from Taiwan, where it took off in the 1980s: sweet milk tea shaken with chewy tapioca pearls. The words that turn up in names come from the drink itself:

  • Boba and pearl both mean the tapioca balls.
  • Taro, brown sugar, lychee and oolong are favourite flavours and teas, and make good name words.
  • Cute animals and pastel colours suit a drink people photograph.

Pick the word your customers search for: boba in the US, bubble tea in the UK and Europe.

Check it's free before you print the sign

A name from a generator is a starting point, not permission to use it. Before you order the cups, menu board or sign:

  • Company register. In the UK, search Companies House; you can't register a limited company with the same name as an existing one. In the US, search your state's business register.
  • Trade marks. Search the UK trade mark register (or the USPTO in the US) for the goods and services you'll sell.
  • Domain and handles. Check the .co.uk or .com and the Instagram and TikTok handles you'll need.
  • Your area. Search a map app and the delivery apps for businesses with the same or a similar name nearby.

Press Check on any name, or use the Business Name Checker, to run most of these at once. For the full process, read our guide, Business Naming Best Practices. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

In the UK, any food business must register with the local council at least 28 days before it starts trading. Registration is free, and the council will inspect you and give you a food hygiene rating.

Tips for bubble tea shops

A large share of trade comes from people searching on their phone near you, so make the name match your Google Business Profile and add both boba and bubble tea to the description.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good bubble tea shop names?
Fun, short names that look good on a cup: Taro Me Away, Tea Rex Boba Shop, Tiny Panda Tea, Leaf & Pearl or Petal & Bamboo Tea House. Choose a style for punny, cute, aesthetic or modern names.
+How do I check a bubble tea shop name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company name, a domain and a social media handle. Before you commit, search the official registers too: in the UK, Companies House and the UK trade mark register; in the US, your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Look on a map app for businesses with the same name nearby.
+Should I call it boba, bubble tea or milk tea?
Use the word your customers search for. Boba is the usual word in the US, bubble tea in the UK, Europe and Australia, and milk tea in much of Asia. If you're not sure, choose a name without the drink in it (Tea Rex, Lilac Tea House) and put both words on your menu and your Google listing.
+Where does bubble tea come from?
Taiwan, where it became popular in the 1980s. It's usually sweetened milk tea or fruit tea with chewy tapioca pearls, called boba.
