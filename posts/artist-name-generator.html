A good artist name for a composer or producer is short, easy to search and fits the music: a single word like Coldfell for cinematic work, a lowercase name like sugarcube. for lo-fi. Lots of composers write library, sync and screen music under their own name and keep a separate alias for artist releases. This Artist Name Generator gives you single-word names, "The" names, first-name aliases, project names and initials in seven styles, from cinematic and ambient to synthwave, lo-fi and dark experimental.

Why composers use an artist alias

An alias keeps your artist releases apart from your library and sync work. A music supervisor searching your name finds your credits and your showreel, and listeners who find your ambient album on Spotify don’t land on a page of 30-second trailer cues.

It also keeps your paperwork tidy. Library tracks, commissions and artist releases are often registered and paid in different ways, and a separate name makes it much easier to keep metadata, writer splits and royalty registrations in order.

And each project gets its own identity. A piano record, a synthwave side project and a dark ambient album can each have a name, artwork and audience of their own, without confusing the people who hire you.

Seven styles, six formats

Each style has its own sound. Cinematic names are big and northern, ambient names are places and weather, neoclassical names borrow musical terms like Rubato, electronic names sound engineered, and dark names are hard and cold.

The formats decide the shape: one word, a "The" name, a first-name alias that reads like a person (Ellis Grey), a two-word project name, or initials and numbers.

For names in one genre, try the Synthwave, Lo-fi and Dark Ambient name generators, or see all the music genre name generators. To credit yourself under your own name, the Composer Name Generator makes full names and studio names, and the Stage Name Generator has names for performers.

Before you use a name

  1. Search Spotify and Apple Music for an existing artist with that name. If someone already uses it, your releases may end up on their profile, or theirs on yours.
  2. Check the name is free with your performing rights organisation (PRS, ASCAP, BMI and so on) before you register works under it, and ask how they handle aliases so the royalties still reach you.
  3. Check the social handles and a domain, so you can use the same name everywhere.

When you’re ready to release, compare music distributors side by side on Composer HQ, a free toolkit for composers, then claim your profile on Spotify for Artists. Renaming a project you’ve already released under? Here’s how to change your artist name on DistroKid.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good artist names for composers?
Short, searchable names that suit the music: a single word like Coldfell for cinematic work, Slowbrook for ambient, or sugarcube. for lo-fi. Pick a style and a format to get names like these.
+Should I release music under an alias?
Many composers do, to keep artist releases apart from library, sync and screen work. A separate name keeps your search results, profiles and royalty registrations tidy.
+How do I check an artist name is free?
Search Spotify and Apple Music for an artist with the same name, check social handles and a domain, and ask your performing rights organisation how it handles aliases before you register any works.
