Humans are the most varied people in D&D, and in the Forgotten Realms their names depend on where they come from. The fifth-edition Player's Handbook lists nine human ethnic groups of Faerûn, each with its own naming style. This D&D human name generator covers three of the most common in Sword Coast adventures: Chondathan (also used by Tethyrians), Illuskan and Damaran.

Human names in the Forgotten Realms

The Player's Handbook (2014) names nine human groups in Faerûn: Calishite, Chondathan, Damaran, Illuskan, Mulan, Rashemi, Shou, Tethyrian and Turami. Each has its own names, many loosely inspired by real-world languages.

This generator covers three:

  • Chondathan and Tethyrian: the most common names along the Sword Coast, in cities like Baldur's Gate and Waterdeep. They sound like English and Western European names, slightly altered.
  • Illuskan: the seafaring people of the cold northern Sword Coast, around Luskan. Their names have a Norse feel and their surnames often come from the sea.
  • Damaran: from Damara, in the Bloodstone Lands of north-eastern Faerûn. Their names have a Slavic sound.

All the names here are new, so you won't share one with an example from the book.

Choosing a name for your human

  • Start with where they grew up. A fisherman from Luskan and a noble from Waterdeep shouldn't sound alike.
  • Use the surname for backstory. Illuskan sea names (Saltbeard, Tidebreaker) say "sailor family" at once.
  • Mix for travellers. A human raised in a port city might have a Chondathan first name and an Illuskan surname.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are the human ethnicities in the Forgotten Realms?
The 2014 Player's Handbook lists nine: Calishite, Chondathan, Damaran, Illuskan, Mulan, Rashemi, Shou, Tethyrian and Turami.
+What names do humans use in Baldur's Gate?
Mostly Chondathan and Tethyrian names, the common style of the Sword Coast's cities, with Illuskan names among people from the north.
+Why only three styles?
They're the ones you'll meet most in Sword Coast adventures. The others are tied closely to real-world cultures, and deserve more care than a quick generator can give.
