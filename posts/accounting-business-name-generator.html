How accounting firms name themselves

Clients hand over their finances, so the name has to feel safe. Most practices use the partners' surnames or the town; bookkeepers and tax preparers can afford to be friendlier. The last word matters: Accountants and Accounting suggest a full service, Bookkeeping the day-to-day records, and Tax a seasonal service.

Be careful with titles. In the UK, anyone can call themselves an accountant, but Chartered Accountant and Chartered Certified Accountant belong to members of bodies such as the ICAEW and ACCA, and Chartered is a sensitive word that Companies House won't accept in a company name without permission. In the US, CPA is a licensed title regulated by each state. Use these only if you hold them.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Every name here has a Check button, which opens the Business Name Checker: it looks up domains, social handles, the company register and trade mark searches in one place. Before you order signs or cards:

  • Company register. In the UK, search Companies House; a limited company's name can't be the same as one already registered. In the US, search your state's business register. Words such as Chartered are sensitive and need permission.
  • Trade marks. Search the UK Intellectual Property Office or the USPTO for marks in your line of work. Registering a company doesn't give you trade mark rights.
  • Domain and handles. Try the .co.uk or .com and the main social handles at the same time, and adjust the name rather than settle for a messy handle.
  • Local search. Look the name up with your town, as two similar firms a few miles apart confuse clients and reviews.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, read Business Naming Best Practices.

In the UK, accountancy and bookkeeping businesses also need to be supervised for anti-money laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC, so register before you start trading. For a general firm name, try the Business or Consulting generators.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good accounting firm names?
Trustworthy names with a clear job: surnames like Blake & Hadley Bookkeeping, plain promises like The Tidy Desk, or a light pun like Carry the One Accounts.
+What should I name my accounting firm?
Clients want to trust you, so most firms use surnames ("Hartley & Rowe Accountants") or the town they serve; bookkeepers and tax preparers can be friendlier ("Numbers People Bookkeeping"). Only use protected titles you hold: "CPA" in the US and "Chartered" in the UK need the right qualification, and "Chartered" is a sensitive word, so Companies House needs permission before a company can use it.
+Can I call myself an accountant without qualifications in the UK?
Yes, accountant on its own isn't a protected title in the UK, but Chartered Accountant and Chartered Certified Accountant are, and Chartered needs permission in a company name. You'll also need anti-money laundering supervision.
+How do I check an accounting business name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. In the UK, search Companies House; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Check your local area too, as two similar names nearby confuse customers.
