Where the names come from: Some names are real and some are invented. How aasimar names work

D&D doesn't give aasimar a naming language of their own. They're born into ordinary mortal families, most often human ones, and take the names those families give them. That's why the first names here are real given names.

Many families who spot a glowing baby choose something hopeful, so some of the names carry meanings of light and fire: Lucia ('light' in Latin), Seren ('star' in Welsh), Stella ('star' in Latin), Aurelia ('golden') and Aidan ('little fire' in Irish). The rest are ordinary names from a fantasy world, which suits an aasimar trying to blend in.

Surnames are new compounds with a heavenly feel for protectors (Dawn-, Star-, Candle-), heat for scourges (Ember-, Pyre-, Flare-) and dusk for the fallen (Ash-, Rook-, Gloam-).

Protector, scourge and fallen

Volo's Guide to Monsters (2016) split aasimar into three kinds, and the generator uses them to set the mood of a name:

  • Protector aasimar are guardians of the weak, whose power shows as shining wings.
  • Scourge aasimar burn with radiant light that blazes out of them and scorches what's near.
  • Fallen aasimar have turned away from the light, and their power shows as a shroud of dread.

Newer rules, in Mordenkainen Presents: Monsters of the Multiverse and the 2024 Player's Handbook, drop the three kinds and give every aasimar a choice of transformation: Heavenly Wings, Inner Radiance or Necrotic Shroud. They line up with protector, scourge and fallen, so pick the kind that matches the transformation you use most.

Naming your angelic guide

In Volo's Guide to Monsters, an aasimar has an angelic guide, a celestial who speaks to them in dreams and steers them towards their calling. The book suggests deciding the guide's name and nature.

Choose Angelic guide's name and you get both: a new celestial name and a two-word nature, like Isathiel, patient and kind. The nature is the useful part at the table. A stern guide gives you an argument to have; a fussy one, a running joke; a sad one, a mystery.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do aasimar names work in D&D?
Aasimar are born into mortal families, usually human, and take the names those families give them, often something hopeful or bright. Choose First name and surname for names like Celeste Dawnmere.
+What are good female aasimar names?
Choose Female and First name only for names like Liora, Soleil, Seren or Estelle. Add a kind to change the mood: Protector for light, Scourge for fire, Fallen for dusk.
+What are good fallen aasimar names?
Names that feel like dusk or ash, such as Vesper Duskmere, Corvin Ashmere or Perdita the Unhaloed. Choose Fallen, and With an epithet for a title that says what was lost.
+What are some aasimar last names?
Compounds with a heavenly or dusky feel, like Dawnmere, Emberhall or Duskmere. Choose First name and surname, and pick a kind to match the surname to your character.
+What should I call my aasimar's angelic guide?
Choose Angelic guide's name for a celestial name and a nature, like Isathiel, patient and kind. The nature gives you a personality to play off in dream scenes.
+Are these names from Volo's Guide to Monsters?
No. The angelic guide names from the book are left out, along with archangel names and well-known aasimar characters, so your aasimar and their guide get new names.
