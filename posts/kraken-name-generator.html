Where the kraken comes from

The kraken comes from Norwegian sailors' stories of a sea monster so vast it could be mistaken for an island.

  • Pontoppidan: the Danish-Norwegian bishop Erik Pontoppidan described it in his Natural History of Norway in the 1750s, warning that sailors had landed on it thinking it was land.
  • Tennyson's poem "The Kraken" (1830) has it sleeping on the sea floor until the end of the world.
  • The real thing: the giant squid, which can grow to more than 12 metres, is the likeliest source of the legend. It was first filmed alive in its deep-sea home in the 2000s.

Naming a kraken

  • Monstrous names are all maw and coil: Deepmaw.
  • Norse names suit its home waters: Hafgrim, Sjovald, Stormulf.
  • Named for its waters: the Kraken of the Grey Deep, the Thousand-Armed Kraken of the Frozen Fjord.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Where does the kraken come from?
From Norwegian folklore. The bishop Erik Pontoppidan described it in the 1750s as a sea monster so big that sailors mistook it for an island.
+Is the kraken a real animal?
No, but the giant squid, which can be over 12 metres long, is probably where the legend began.
+What's a good name for a kraken?
A monstrous one like Deepmaw, a Norse-sounding one like Hafgrim, or a place, like the Kraken of the Grey Deep.
