How the Movie Title Generator Works

It works from 1,221 titles and name parts, which combine into over 640,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Genre: Any genre, Action, Comedy, Romcom, Family, Sci-fi, Thriller, Western, Horror, Christmas.
  • Format:
    • Any, e.g. The Revenge of Etta Hollis
    • Title only, e.g. The Brandt Account
    • Sequel, e.g. Rapid Velocity 2
    • Subtitle, e.g. The Dryden Confession: Collateral Damage

Where the names come from: Every name is invented. Titles are built from word lists and the patterns each kind of film uses: Operation codenames for action, "Falling for the…" for romcoms, B-movie "Attack of the…" creatures and Christmas films set somewhere snowy. Every result is checked against a list of famous film titles, and franchise names are left out.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Film titles have to work on a poster, in a trailer voice-over and in a group chat, so they tend to be short and loud. This movie title generator makes titles for nine kinds of film, from action and romcoms to westerns, B-movie creature features and Christmas films, and can turn any of them into a sequel.

Eight good movie titles

All eight came from the generator, one from each of eight genres:

  • Operation Nightjar: action's favourite shape, a mission with a codename that sounds secret but means nothing.
  • Dad Goes Rogue: a comedy where the title is the whole joke, and the trailer writes itself.
  • Accidentally Royal: a romcom premise in two words.
  • Penguins on Ice: a family film with an animal and a place, a pairing animated films use again and again.
  • Outpost Zero: sci-fi that tells you there's a base, it's remote and something is about to go wrong there.
  • The Kessler Files: a thriller with a surname and a folder nobody was meant to open.
  • Ambush at Red Mesa: a western with an event and a place, like the posters of the 1950s.
  • Attack of the Fifty-Foot Hamsters: a B-movie creature feature, silly on purpose.

How film titles work

Each genre has a shape audiences read in a second:

  • Action: two hard words (an adjective and a noun) or an Operation with a codename.
  • Comedy: a person against something small and annoying, or a relative with a secret job.
  • Romcom: the situation, like "Falling for the…" or a number of dates somewhere romantic.
  • Family and animated: an animal, a name or a "Great… Rescue".
  • Westerns: a showdown at a town, or "The Ballad of" a name.
  • B-movie horror: the 1950s creature feature, after real films such as It Came from Outer Space (1953) and Attack of the 50 Foot Woman (1958). For proper scares, use the Horror Movie Title Generator.
  • Christmas: the cosy TV films that fill December schedules: Christmas at somewhere snowy, or someone who saved Christmas.

Sequels and subtitles

Studios name follow-ups in three main ways, and the Format menu does all three. A number is the simplest (Toy Story 2, Paddington 2). Roman numerals feel grander (Rocky II). A subtitle after a colon gives each film its own identity while keeping the brand, as the Mission: Impossible films do.

Choose Sequel for numbers, Part Two and the odd subtitle, or Subtitle for titles like Dark Orbit: Exodus. They suit fake trailers, film-club quizzes and the franchise plan for your short film.

Before you use a title

In the UK and the US a film title on its own generally isn't protected by copyright, which is why so many films share one. A title that copies a famous film will confuse people and get lost in search results, though, and a franchise name can be a trade mark. Search IMDb and a video site before you print a poster or enter a festival. This is general information, not legal advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do I come up with a name for my movie?
Pick the genre first, because each one has a title shape audiences recognise. Generate a few batches, keep the two or three that suit your story, and say them out loud as if you were reading a trailer.
+What is a good title for a short film?
Short and specific works best, like Outpost Zero or The Kessler Files. Choose Title only so you don't get sequels, and pick the genre closest to your film.
+How do I make a sequel title?
Choose Sequel under Format for titles like Outpost Zero 2 or Operation Nightjar Part Two, or Subtitle for a title and a colon. Any genre works.
+What is a good Christmas movie title?
Something snowy and cosy, like The Tinsel Swap, Mistletoe at the Lighthouse or The Postman Who Fixed Christmas. Choose Christmas.
+Can two films have the same title?
Yes, it happens often, because titles alone usually aren't protected by copyright. Avoid copying a famous one, and search IMDb before you settle on yours.
+Does it make horror movie titles?
It makes light-hearted B-movie ones like Attack of the Fifty-Foot Hamsters. For hauntings, slashers and folk horror, use the Horror Movie Title Generator.
+Are these real film titles?
No. They're made up and checked against a list of famous films, so you won't get Jaws or Die Hard. A smaller film may share one by chance.
