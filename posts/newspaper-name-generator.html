How the Newspaper Name Generator Works

Where the names come from: Every name is invented. Names are built from the words real papers have used for centuries (Gazette, Courier, Chronicle, Mercury, Intelligencer) and from school, playground and fantasy words. Every result is checked against a list of real national newspapers, comics and famous fictional papers such as the Daily Prophet and the Daily Bugle. Local papers really do use "town + Gazette", so check yours isn't taken.

Many newspaper names still follow a pattern that's centuries old: a place, then a word for news, like Gazette, Courier or Chronicle. This newspaper name generator uses those words and plenty of newer ones for a local paper, a school or class newspaper, a paper in a fantasy world, an old-fashioned title, a funny one or a newsletter.

Eight good newspaper names

All eight came from the generator, one from each kind of paper plus a class name:

  • Hampton Gazette: your town and the oldest word for a newspaper. Type your own town into the box.
  • The Oakfield Scoop: a school paper that sounds as if it knows something you don't.
  • The Giggle Gazette: a kids' paper with the alliteration children love.
  • Hedgehogs Class Herald: for a primary class named after an animal or a tree.
  • The Highmere Crier: a fantasy town's paper, named after the town crier it replaced.
  • The Ashbury Mercury: an old-fashioned title using the messenger of the Roman gods.
  • The Weekly Whinge: a funny paper that admits what it's really for.
  • In the Loop: a friendly newsletter name for an office, club or street.

What the old newspaper words mean

Most of the classic words describe news as something carried, announced or recorded:

  • Gazette: from the Italian gazzetta, the name of news sheets in early modern Venice. It's usually said to come from a small Venetian coin, though not every expert agrees.
  • Courier and Messenger: someone who carries news.
  • Herald and Crier: someone who announces it in public.
  • Chronicle and Recorder: a record of events in the order they happened.
  • Mercury: the messenger of the Roman gods.
  • Argus: the many-eyed giant of Greek myth, who never stopped watching.
  • Courant: from the French for "running" or "current".
  • Intelligencer: an old word for a bringer of news, from intelligence in its old sense of news or information.

The Old-fashioned option uses these with invented towns and long old-style endings such as "and General Advertiser".

Naming a school or class newspaper

  • Put your school in it. Type the school's name into the box and choose School for names like The Oakfield Scoop.
  • Or name it after school life. The Corridor Chronicle and The Homework Herald work for any school and make good mastheads.
  • Keep it short for the masthead. Two or three words fit across the top of a page in big letters.
  • Let the class vote. Generate a list, pick five, and let everyone choose. Children care more about a paper they named.

The Class or kids option is for younger writers, with silly alliteration (The Crayon Courier) and class names like Owls Class News.

Check a local name isn't taken

Real local papers use exactly these patterns, from the Liverpool Echo to the Kentish Gazette, so a town plus Gazette or Echo may already belong to someone. Before you print a masthead or start a community newsletter, search the name with your town, and check the website address and social media handles you'd want. The generator leaves out the names of real national papers and famous fictional ones such as the Daily Prophet, but it can't know every local title.

For a paper in a story, let the name tell readers about the world: who prints it, who reads it and what they're worried about. The Fantasy Town Name Generator can give it a town to report on.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What should I call my school newspaper?
Use the school's name with a news word, like The Oakfield Scoop, or a school-life pun like The Corridor Chronicle. Choose School and type your school's name into the box.
+What is a good name for a class newspaper?
Something fun to say, like The Giggle Gazette, or your class name, like Hedgehogs Class Herald. Choose Class or kids.
+What is a good newspaper name for a story or fantasy world?
A town name and an old news word, like The Highmere Crier, or something only that world could have, like The Dragon's Dispatch. Choose Fantasy.
+What are some old-fashioned newspaper names?
Older papers used words like Mercury, Intelligencer and Courant, often with a long ending: The Ashbury Mercury and General Advertiser, for example. Choose Old-fashioned.
+What should I call a newsletter?
Something friendly and short, like In the Loop or The Allotment Chatter. Choose Newsletter for names that suit an office, club, church or street.
+Why do so many newspapers have the same names?
Words like Gazette, Echo and Chronicle have meant "newspaper" for centuries, so towns all over the world used them. That's why it's worth searching a name with your town before you use it.
