How kingdoms get their names

Real kingdoms were usually named after a people or a place. England was the land of the Angles. Wessex was the kingdom of the West Saxons and Essex the East Saxons, Northumbria lay north of the Humber, and Mercia took its name from an Old English word for the people of the border. Tolkien did the same in Elvish: Gondor means "stone-land".

  • Medieval names join English place-name parts, like -moor, -mark and -vale, or take a heraldic badge: Kingdom of the White Boar.
  • Elven realms are courts, hidden or moonlit: The Court of Naisse.
  • Dwarven kingdoms are holds and crowns of iron: The Holds of Hammerdeep.
  • Desert kingdoms take golden and sun thrones: The Sun Throne of Qaset.
  • Dark kingdoms are withered, cursed or named for what's gone wrong: The Kingdom of Silence, where no birds sing.
  • Orc kingdoms are warlands, clanlands and skull thrones.

Choose With a description for a one-line hook, like Ardmont, whose old king has no heir, which is half a plot already.

Putting a kingdom on the map

  • Give neighbours different sounds. An elven court next to an orc warland should sound nothing alike.
  • Let the description start a story. A kingdom "which sits between two greater powers" tells your players where the next war will be.
  • Name the capital and the castle too, so the court has somewhere to sit.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good kingdom names?
Names that suit the land and the people: Pennmark or Lornwyn for a medieval realm, The Starlit Court of Ysilesse for elves, The Deep Kingdom of Kadgarth for dwarves, The Golden Throne of Isharir for a desert kingdom.
+How do you name a fantasy kingdom?
Start with the people or the landscape, as real kingdoms did (Wessex was the kingdom of the West Saxons), then add a title that says how it's ruled: a Court, a Throne, the Holds, the Clanlands.
+What's the difference between a kingdom and an empire?
A kingdom is ruled by a king or queen over one people or land. An empire rules many peoples and lands, often conquered, under an emperor. Try the Empire Name Generator for the bigger kind.
