How the Tabaxi Name Generator Works

It works from 4,195 names and name parts, which combine into over 1,000,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Style: Any style, Sky, Animals, Numbers, Things, Curious.
  • Name format:
    • Name and nickname, e.g. Golden Lantern (Lantern)
    • Name only, e.g. One Ash
    • With a clan, e.g. Swallow by the Well (Swallow) of the Singing Mesa clan
    • Clan name only, e.g. Copper Peaks

Where the names come from: Every name is invented. Names are new phrases in the style the D&D tabaxi writeup describes: a long name made of everyday images, shortened to a one-word nickname, and clan names taken from a nearby landmark. The sample names and clans from Volo's Guide to Monsters are left out, along with song, film and brand titles.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Tabaxi are the curious cat folk of Dungeons & Dragons, and their names read like tiny poems: a long phrase chosen by the clan, shortened to one word for everyday use. This tabaxi name generator makes both, in five styles, and adds clan names taken from the landscape. It's a fan-made, unofficial page: D&D and the tabaxi belong to Wizards of the Coast, and every name here is new.

How tabaxi names work

Volo's Guide to Monsters (2016), where fifth-edition tabaxi first appeared, describes their names like this:

  • One name, chosen by the clan. A tabaxi usually has a single name, worked out by their clan through a complicated formula of astrology, prophecy, clan history and other things outsiders don't follow.
  • Not split by sex. The same name can go to any tabaxi, which is why this generator has no gender filter.
  • A nickname for daily use. Most tabaxi go by a nickname taken from, or inspired by, the full name. The book's own example is Cloud on the Mountaintop, known as Cloud.
  • Clan names from the land. A clan is usually named after a landmark in or near its territory: a waterfall, a cliff, a river.

The generator follows that pattern: the nickname in brackets is the word the rest of the phrase hangs on, or a short piece of it (Bird from Hummingbird, Spark from Firefly). Choose With a clan to add the clan after the name.

The five styles

  • Sky: weather and light, from Restless Cloud to Rain in the Valley.
  • Animals: the creatures of a jungle or a town, like Heron in the Reeds or Wandering Firefly.
  • Numbers: a number and a single word, like Nine Wind or Six Lanterns. The book's Five Timber and Seven Thundercloud are names of this kind, and they sound like the numbered day names of the Aztec calendar, such as One Reed. In the Forgotten Realms, tabaxi are said to come from Maztica, a land inspired by Mesoamerica.
  • Things: a treasured or odd object, like Copper Bell or Cup Without a Handle. These suit a character who picks up everything shiny.
  • Curious: ideas, sounds and moments, like Unfinished Riddle. Tabaxi are driven by curiosity, and these names lean into it.

Using the name at the table

The full name and the nickname do different jobs. The party will use the nickname all the time, so make sure it's easy to say. Save the full name for moments that matter: an introduction to a noble, a vow, or the clan elder who refuses to use anything shorter.

The long name is also a story prompt. If the clan read Lantern Left in the Rain in the stars, someone expected this kitten to be forgotten, or to wait for someone who never came. Your tabaxi can spend a campaign proving the prophecy wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do tabaxi names work in D&D?
A tabaxi has one long name chosen by their clan, usually shortened to a nickname, and belongs to a clan named after a local landmark. Choose Name and nickname for names like Cloud over the Temple (Cloud).
+What is a tabaxi nickname?
The short name a tabaxi goes by day to day, taken from or inspired by the full name. The generator shows it in brackets: Bright-Eyed Kingfisher is Fisher, Wandering Firefly is Spark.
+Are tabaxi names male or female?
Neither. In D&D the same tabaxi name can go to anyone, so every name here works for a male, female or non-binary tabaxi.
+What are some good tabaxi clan names?
Names taken from the land, like Thundering Gorge, Whispering Rocks or Singing Falls. Choose Clan name only for more, or With a clan to add one to a name.
+What are some cute or funny tabaxi names?
Choose Things or Curious for names like Borrowed Spoon, Polite Cricket or Half-Told Story. They suit a tabaxi who is more nosy than fierce.
+Are these names from Volo's Guide to Monsters?
No. They're new names in the same style; the book's sample names and clans are left out, so your tabaxi won't share a name with one from the book.
