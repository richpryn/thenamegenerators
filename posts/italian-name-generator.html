How the Italian Name Generator Works

It works from 1,976 names and name parts, which combine into over 500,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Gender: Any, Male, Female.
  • Era: Any era, Popular now, Born 2000–2009.
  • Name format:
    • Full name, e.g. Guglielmo Putzu
    • First name only, e.g. Dalila
    • Surname only, e.g. Tralli

Where the names come from: These are real recorded names. Every first name and surname is real. First names are the most given names for babies registered in Italy in 2022–2024 and 2000–2009, from Istat's survey Iscritti in anagrafe per nascita (via Istat's Conta nomi tool, CC BY 4.0). Istat doesn't publish surnames, so they come from the open data of 13 comuni from Lombardy to Sicily and Sardinia: Bergamo and Brescia (CC0), and Bitonto, Florence, Lamezia Terme, Lecce, Matera, Mentana, Milazzo, Olbia, Pescara, Piacenza and Taranto (CC BY 4.0), counting Italian citizens where the file says so. Each town gives its most common surnames. A full name joins a first name and a surname at random, so the pairing is new. Left out: brand names (Ferrari, Martini), a surname that is an offensive word in English, surnames better known as first names, and pairings that match a famous person, such as Valentino Rossi or Antonio Conte.

The labels on each name: Real name a real name, found in historical records or in real use. Real parts a new combination of real names or name parts, made by this generator. Our Naming Library explains how we check names.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Italian names tell you when and often where someone was born: Leonardo and Sofia are today's favourites, Francesco and Giulia topped the 2000s, and a surname like Sanna, Rota or Rizzo can point to Sardinia, Bergamo or the south. This Italian name generator uses Istat's official records of the names given to babies in Italy, with real surnames from the open data of 13 towns and cities from the Alps to Sicily. Get a full name, a first name or a surname.

Eight good Italian names

  • Leonardo Bianchi: Leonardo has been the top boys' name in recent years, and Bianchi is the plural of bianco, 'white'.
  • Sofia Ricci: Sofia leads the girls' names of 2022–2024; Ricci comes from riccio, 'curly'.
  • Ginevra Locatelli: Ginevra, Italian for Guinevere, is near the top of the girls' list, and Locatelli is one of Bergamo's most common surnames.
  • Francesco Pio Di Matteo: a compound first name with a surname that means '(son) of Matteo'.
  • Giulia Sanna: Giulia was the most given girls' name of the 2000s; Sanna is a Sardinian surname.
  • Tommaso Greco: Greco means 'Greek', and it's one of the most common surnames in Lecce.
  • Aurora D'Angelo: di becomes D' before a vowel, so 'of Angelo' is D'Angelo.
  • Alessandro Russo: Russo is the southern counterpart of Rossi; both come from rosso, 'red', probably for red hair.

How Italian surnames work

Italy has a huge number of surnames, and most are strongly regional. The lists this generator uses show it town by town: Rota and Locatelli lead in Bergamo, Rossi in Florence and Piacenza, Rizzo and Greco in Lecce, Deiana and Sanna in Olbia, and Di Giovanni in Pescara. (Ferrari leads in Brescia, but the generator leaves it out because of the car maker.)

  • Endings change from north to south. In the northern towns most surnames end in -i, like Rossi, Bianchi and Locatelli. Further south more end in -o or -e, like Russo, Greco and Leone.
  • Di, De and D' mean 'of', usually followed by a father's name: Di Giovanni, De Luca, D'Angelo. They are common in Abruzzo and the south.
  • Sardinia has its own surnames, such as Sanna, Piras, Pinna, Serra and Manca.
  • Many come from a first name, a job, a place or a nickname, as in other countries: Martinelli (little Martino), Barbieri (barbers), Lombardi (from Lombardy), Rossi (red).

For centuries a child simply took the father's surname. Since a 2022 ruling of the Constitutional Court (sentence 131/2022), a child takes both parents' surnames in the order they agree, unless they agree to give just one.

Italian first names, old and new

Istat's name counts start in 1999, so the generator has two eras:

  • Popular now (2022–2024): Leonardo, Edoardo, Tommaso, Francesco and Alessandro for boys; Sofia, Aurora, Ginevra, Vittoria and Giulia for girls.
  • Born 2000–2009: Francesco, Alessandro, Andrea, Matteo and Lorenzo; Giulia, Martina, Sara, Chiara and Alessia. These are names for characters in their twenties.

A few things to know:

  • Andrea is mainly a boy's name in Italy, though some girls are given it too.
  • Compound names such as Francesco Pio, Giuseppe Pio, Maria Vittoria and Francesco Maria are written as two words and count as one name.
  • Accents fall on the last letter in names like Nicolò, Niccolò and Giosuè. Istat's files write them with an apostrophe (NICOLO'), and the generator shows the accent.

Using an Italian name for a character

  • Let the surname set the region. A Sardinian character might be a Piras or a Sanna, a Bergamo family Rota or Locatelli, a Puglian one Rizzo or De Giorgi.
  • Match the first name to the age. Today's toddlers are Leonardo and Sofia; their older brothers and sisters, born in the 2000s, are more likely Francesco or Giulia.
  • Search the full name before you publish. Pairings of real names can match a real person; the generator blocks the famous ones it knows.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are popular Italian boy names?
For babies born in 2022–2024 the top names were Leonardo, Edoardo, Tommaso, Francesco and Alessandro. Choose Male and Popular now (2022–2024).
+What are popular Italian girl names?
Sofia, Aurora, Ginevra, Vittoria and Giulia led in 2022–2024. Choose Female and Popular now for today's names, or Born 2000–2009 for names like Giulia, Martina and Chiara.
+What are common Italian surnames?
It depends on the region: Rossi is common in the north and centre, Russo in the south, Sanna in Sardinia. Choose Surname only for real surnames from towns all over Italy.
+Are these real Italian names?
Yes. The first names come from Istat, Italy's national statistics institute, and the surnames from the open data of Italian towns. They're joined at random, so a full name is a new pairing.
+What do Di and De mean in Italian surnames?
Both mean 'of', usually followed by a father's name, so Di Matteo is '(son) of Matteo'. Before a vowel they become D', as in D'Angelo.
+Do Italian children take their father's surname?
They used to automatically. Since a 2022 Constitutional Court ruling, children take both parents' surnames in the order the parents agree, unless they choose one.
