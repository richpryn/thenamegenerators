Twitch's username rules

  • Length: 4 to 25 characters.
  • Characters: letters, numbers and underscores only. No spaces, hyphens, full stops or emoji, so Copper Otter becomes CopperOtter or Copper_Otter.
  • Capitals: your channel address is your username in lower case (twitch.tv/copperottertv). Your display name can add capital letters to make it easier to read.
  • Changing it: you can change your username once every 60 days, but not while you're live or while a video is uploading.
  • Taken names: Twitch doesn't release inactive usernames, so a name someone registered years ago usually stays taken.

Every name this generator makes fits those rules, and none starts with an underscore. Rules can change, so if a name won't go through, check Twitch's own help pages.

How streamer names work

A Twitch name has to work in three places: said out loud on stream, typed in chat and read in a list of live channels. The names that stick tend to follow a few patterns:

  • A brand plus a channel word. TV, Live, Streams, Plays or HQ on the end makes a name sound like a show, and it's the usual way to get a name when the plain version is taken.
  • Short and easy to type. Viewers tag you with @ and type your name to shout you out, so one or two short words beat a long phrase.
  • A mood. Cosy streams have soft, homely names; competitive ones sound sharp. The name is the first hint of what the stream is like.
  • A joke about yourself. Funny names that admit you're bad at something give chat a running joke from day one.

Read your name in lower case before you commit, since that's how it appears in your channel address: two words run together can spell something you didn't mean. Every name here has been checked for that.

Check it's free before you go live

The names here are free to use, but you need to check each one is available. Search twitch.tv/ followed by the name, or try it in your account settings. If you're building a channel, check the same handle on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram too, so viewers can find you everywhere under one name, and if the channel might grow into a brand, search the trade mark registers (the UK IPO or USPTO) as well. Frequently Asked Questions

+How long can a Twitch username be?
Between 4 and 25 characters, using only letters, numbers and underscores. Every name this generator makes fits.
+Can a Twitch name have spaces?
No. Use capital letters to show the words (CopperOtterTV), or choose Underscores in the Format box for Copper_Otter_TV.
+How often can I change my Twitch name?
Once every 60 days, and not while you're live. In most languages your display name can only change the capital letters of your username, but you can do that at any time.
+How do I make a Twitch name with my name in it?
Choose "Yes, use the word below" and type your name or nickname. With Milo you get names like MiloAndTea and MiloIsBadAtParking.
+What are good Twitch names for cosy streamers?
Choose Cosy and chill for soft, homely names like MossAndMilk and SleepyFernCorner, which suit slow games, crafts and chatting streams.
+What are some funny Twitch names?
Choose Funny for names that make fun of how you play, like RubbishAtParking, BadAtBossFights and ChaoticGooseEnergy.
+Is this an official Twitch tool?
No. It's a free, unofficial name generator and isn't connected with Twitch or Amazon. Check a name is available on Twitch before you use it.
