Most band names follow a handful of shapes: The and a plural (The Beatles, The Strokes), an adjective and a noun (Arctic Monkeys), a single word (Nirvana, Metallica) or something stranger. Genre changes the words you reach for, not the shapes. This band name generator builds names in all those shapes for six genres, so you can find one that sounds like your music before you check it's free.

Where famous band names came from

  • The Beatles: a pun on beetles and the beat, a nod to Buddy Holly's Crickets.
  • The Rolling Stones: from Muddy Waters' song "Rollin' Stone".
  • Radiohead: from the Talking Heads song "Radio Head", after the band started out as On a Friday.
  • Pink Floyd: from two blues musicians, Pink Anderson and Floyd Council.
  • Black Sabbath: from the 1963 horror film of the same name, starring Boris Karloff.
  • Led Zeppelin: from a joke that the band would go down like a lead balloon; the spelling was changed so people wouldn't say "leed".

The pattern is worth noticing: a song, a joke, a film, a pun. Names that came from somewhere usually have more character than names made up to sound cool.

How genre changes the name

  • Metal: dark, heavy and often ancient. Thrones, serpents, iron and frost.
  • Punk: short, rude and funny. A punk name should annoy someone's parents.
  • Indie: gentle and a little odd. Everyday objects, weather, small sad animals.
  • Folk: landscape and work. Rivers, hedgerows, fiddlers and ploughmen.
  • Electronic: light, signals and machines. Often one word, often invented.
  • Rock: movement and noise. Engines, highways, wolves and hearts.

Check it's free before you print the T-shirts

Thousands of bands share names, and finding out after your first release is painful. Before you settle on one:

  • Search the streaming services (Spotify, Apple Music) and Bandcamp for the exact name.
  • Search Discogs and MusicBrainz, which list older and smaller releases streaming may miss.
  • Check social handles and a domain, so fans can find you.
  • Check trade marks if you're serious about it: the UK IPO, USPTO and EUIPO all have free searches. This isn't legal advice; if in doubt, ask someone who can give it.

A small twist (a different article, an unusual spelling, an extra word) can make a taken name your own.

Picking the one

  • Say it out loud, as a gig announcement: "Please welcome... The Paper Lanterns!"
  • Imagine it on a poster and a T-shirt. Long names shrink; short ones shout.
  • Make it searchable. A common word on its own is hard to find online. Two words are easier.
  • Live with it for a week. If you still like it, it's probably the one.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do I check if a band name is taken?
Search Spotify, Apple Music, Bandcamp, Discogs and MusicBrainz for the exact name, then check social handles and domains. For a serious project, search trade mark registers too (UK IPO, USPTO, EUIPO).
+Should my band name start with "The"?
It's a classic shape (The Beatles, The Strokes, The Cure) and works best with a plural noun. Many bands drop it later, so pick a name that works either way.
+Can I use these names for my band?
Yes, free and with no credit needed, but check the name isn't already used by another band first.
