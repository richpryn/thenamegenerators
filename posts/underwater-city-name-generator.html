How the Underwater City Name Generator Works

It works from 1,128 names and name parts, which combine into over 100,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Style: Any style, Merfolk, Sunken ancient, Sci-fi undersea base, Deep-sea dark, Coral and tropical.
  • Name Format:
    • Name only, e.g. Sunken Reedthorpe
    • With a description, e.g. Khalaroth, that sank out of reach of the sun long ago

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Good underwater city names sound like the sea they're in: soft and shining for merfolk (Nacredeep), heavy and old for a sunken city (Drowned Withywold), cold and technical for a research base (Thermocline Station). This underwater city name generator builds names for every kind of city beneath the waves: shimmering merfolk courts, ancient cities lost to the sea, sci-fi bases on the seabed, lightless cities in the deep and bright towns on the coral reef. Add a short description to give your D&D campaign, story or game an instant hook.

Eight good underwater city names

All eight came from the generator, across its five styles:

  • Nacredeep: mother-of-pearl and the deep in one word, for a merfolk city.
  • The Kelp Bower of Aunura, built on the back of a sleeping sea turtle: a merfolk city with a story to explore.
  • Wethermouth Beneath the Waves, lost to the sea along with its church and market: an ordinary town that drowned, which makes it sadder.
  • Malador the Drowned, that sank with all its gold, or so the story goes: a legend with treasure in it.
  • Thermocline Station: a sci-fi base named after the layer where warm water meets cold.
  • Lumen Station, whose radio has picked up a signal from deeper down: a research base with a mystery underneath.
  • Siltrift, whose citizens have never seen the colour blue: a city of the dark deep.
  • Lumiro, whose children ride seahorses to school: a bright coral town.

Drowned cities in legend

The most famous underwater city is Atlantis, described by Plato in his dialogues Timaeus and Critias as a great island power swallowed by the sea in a single day and night. Breton legend has Ys, the city of King Gradlon, drowned when the sea gates were opened, and Welsh legend has Cantre'r Gwaelod, a lost kingdom beneath Cardigan Bay. Real coasts have lost towns too: most of Dunwich, once a busy port in Suffolk, has gone into the sea.

  • Merfolk cities are named for pearls, shells and foam: Moonshell Palace, Foamhaven.
  • Sunken cities keep their old names with Drowned, Sunken or Beneath the Waves added.
  • Sci-fi bases take ocean science words and code names: Habitat Isobath, Fathom Base Delta.
  • Deep-sea cities have cold, heavy names: Hollowtrench, Ulmugoth.
  • Coral towns are bright and warm: Angelfish Hollow, Calavi Lagoon.

Using an underwater city

  • Decide who can reach it. A city only merfolk can visit is a different adventure from a sunken ruin divers explore.
  • Let the description be the hook: a signal from deeper down, or a city that rises once a century.
  • Think about light. Names in the deep can be darker because nobody there has seen the sun.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for an underwater city?
Names that fit the sea and who lives there: Nacredeep for merfolk, Drowned Withywold for a sunken ruin, Thermocline Station for a sci-fi base, or Siltrift for a city in the deep.
+What is the most famous underwater city?
Atlantis, which Plato described as an island power that sank in a single day and night. Breton legend has the drowned city of Ys, and Welsh legend Cantre'r Gwaelod.
+Can I use these in D&D?
Yes. Choose With a description for a hook, such as a city that rises once every hundred years or a market that sells sea-glass and stories.
