The Hydra of Lerna

The Hydra of Greek myth lived in the swamps of Lerna, near Argos, and killing it was the second of Heracles's twelve labours.

  • Two for one: every head Heracles cut off grew back as two, until his nephew Iolaus burned each neck with a torch to stop it.
  • One immortal head could not be killed, so Heracles buried it under a great rock.
  • Poisoned arrows: he dipped his arrows in the Hydra's venom, and they later caused the deaths of several heroes, and of Heracles himself.
  • How many heads? Ancient writers can't agree. Nine is the usual number; some say many more.

Naming a hydra

A hydra name should hiss, or tell you where to keep away from.

  • Serpent names are all coil and venom: Venomcoil, Bilescale.
  • Lake names put it in its swamp: the Hydra of Blackmere, the Mire Hydra.
  • Greek names (Enneaphis, "nine-snake") suit a hydra of myth.
  • Epithets give the head count, like "the Nine-Headed".

Hydra is also the largest constellation in the sky. For other serpents, try the Wyvern and Dragon name generators.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How many heads did the Hydra have?
Usually nine, though ancient writers disagree. Every head cut off grew back as two.
+How did Heracles kill the Hydra?
He cut off the heads while his nephew Iolaus burned each neck so it couldn't grow back, then buried the immortal last head under a rock.
+What's a good name for a hydra?
A hissing serpent name like Venomcoil, a place like the Hydra of Blackmere, or a Greek one like Enneaphis, with a head count as an epithet.
