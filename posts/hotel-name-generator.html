A good hotel name promises a certain kind of stay: a grand name for chandeliers, two pretty words for a boutique, a house number for a seaside B&B. How hotels name themselves

Hotel names follow fashion. Coaching inns took the names of pub signs; the grand hotels of the 19th century chose names that sounded important; boutique hotels went small and clever.

  • Grand hotels: the Victorian railway and seaside hotels called themselves the Grand, the Metropole, the Imperial and the Esplanade. The Savoy in London opened in 1889, and the Ritz is named after the hotelier César Ritz.
  • Boutique hotels use two nouns, a colour or a material: Cedar & Fig, The Velvet Rooms.
  • Seaside B&Bs are known by a house name or a street number, and by their breakfasts.
  • Haunted hotels have a ghost story in the name: The Broken Chandelier Hotel.
  • Fantasy inn-hotels are lodging houses, waystations and travellers' halls.
  • Space hotels are named after astronomy words: aphelion, nadir, parsec. A motel, by the way, was a "motor hotel" for drivers.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good names for a hotel?
It depends on the stay: The Ravenmouth Esplanade Hotel for a grand seaside hotel, Tinder & Clove for a boutique one, or 7 Bay Terrace for a B&B. Choose a style to match.
+What makes a good boutique hotel name?
Two short, pleasant words, often joined with an ampersand, or a colour or material: Cedar & Fig, The Velvet Rooms, The Indigo Rooms.
+Can I use these names for a real hotel?
They're invented, but hotel names are often trademarked, so search the name and check with your local registry before you use it for a business.
