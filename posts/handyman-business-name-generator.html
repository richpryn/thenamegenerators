All eight came from the generator:

  • Handy Tom: your first name and the job in two words. Customers often hire the person, not the firm. Put your own name in the box.
  • Fix & Chips Handyman Services: a pun people repeat, with the job spelled out after it.
  • Grout & About Handyman Services: another pun, and it tells people you'll come to them.
  • Tick the List Home Repairs: promises to clear the jobs nobody else will do.

What makes a handyman name work

  • Choose your customers first. Handyman and odd jobs speak to homeowners with a list of small fixes. Property maintenance and facilities speak to landlords, agents and businesses, who want a firm that will still be there next year.
  • Your first name is a strength. People invite a handyman into their home, so Tom Can Fix It or Tom the Handyman builds trust quickly. Choose From my name in the Style menu.
  • Puns get remembered, but pair them with what you do: Fix & Chips alone could be a chip shop.
  • Don't promise work you can't legally do. In the UK there's no licence to be a handyman, but gas work needs Gas Safe registration, and in England and Wales notifiable electrical work, such as adding a new circuit, has to meet Part P of the Building Regulations. Keep Gas and Electrical out of the name unless you're registered.

Check it's free before you print the sign

A handyman name goes on a van, a website, a Google Business Profile and every invoice, so it's worth a few minutes' checking before anything is printed:

  • Press Check on any name above. The Business Name Checker looks up the domain, links to the company register (Companies House in the UK) and opens the trade mark search and social handles.
  • Search where you'll trade. Look the name up on maps, marketplaces and social media. Two businesses with the same name confuse customers and reviews.
  • Pick a name that still fits if you grow: Property Maintenance stretches further than Shelf Fitting.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, from shortlisting to registering, read our guide to Business Naming Best Practices.

Getting the name seen

  • Use the slogan. Choose Name with a slogan for a line like Measure twice, fix once to go under the name on the van.
  • Add your town for local search: Hampton Home Repairs tells a customer you're nearby before they click.
  • Keep the phone number bigger than the joke. On a van, the name gets a glance; the number needs to be readable at a junction.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What is a good name for a handyman business?
A good handyman name says what you do and is easy to remember from the side of a van: Handy Work, Odds & Ends Home Repairs or simply Handy Tom. Your own first name works well, because customers often hire the person rather than the firm. Choose From my name in the Style menu to try it.
+Should a handyman business be called handyman or property maintenance?
Handyman suits homeowners with a list of small jobs. Property maintenance suits landlords, letting agents and businesses, who often want a firm that sounds established. Choose Professional for names like Ironwood Property Maintenance.
+How do I check a handyman business name is free?
Press Check next to any name to see if it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. Then make sure: in the UK, search Companies House and the UK IPO trade mark register; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. A quick map search for the name in your area helps too.
+Do I need a licence to be a handyman in the UK?
There's no general licence, but some jobs are regulated: gas work needs Gas Safe registration, and notifiable electrical work in England and Wales has to meet Part P. Many handymen also take out public liability insurance.
