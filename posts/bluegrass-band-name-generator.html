Good bluegrass band names come from mountains, creeks and hollers, or from the leader out front, a habit that goes back to Bill Monroe and his Blue Grass Boys. This bluegrass band name generator makes band names, picker names and album titles in that high, lonesome style, plus a few jamgrass curveballs.

How bluegrass names itself

Bluegrass is named after one band: Bill Monroe and the Blue Grass Boys, who took their name from Kentucky, the Bluegrass State. By the late 1940s the line-up of mandolin, banjo, fiddle, guitar and bass was being copied so widely that the band's name became the genre's.

  • Leaders and their Boys. Lester Flatt and Earl Scruggs had the Foggy Mountain Boys, and the pattern stuck. Choose Band for names like Eli Stamper & the Mountain Rangers.
  • Brothers. The Stanley Brothers and the Osborne Brothers made family harmony part of the sound: The Fugate Brothers, Childers & Spurlock.
  • Places. Counties, creeks and forks in the river, many of them real places in Kentucky, Virginia and the Carolinas: The Rocky Fork Express, The Hiwassee Rangers.
  • Pickers often go by nicknames or initials: Shorty Rowe, C.W. Tackett.

Album titles sound like tunes from the jam: breakdowns, specials and waltzes, such as Clinch River Special or Bear Creek Waltz.

Using a bluegrass band name

  • Be careful with real places. Naming the band after a county or creek is traditional, but check nobody local already plays under it.
  • Nicknames suit pickers. A name like Red or Doc makes a sideman memorable on a festival line-up.
  • Jamgrass can be sillier. If you play long jams at festivals rather than straight bluegrass, a playful name fits.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good bluegrass band names?
A leader and their Boys, like Buddy Neace & the Mountain Boys, a pair of brothers, or a real creek or county with a job, like The Harlan County Pickers.
+Where does the name bluegrass come from?
From Bill Monroe's band, the Blue Grass Boys, named after Kentucky, the Bluegrass State. The band's sound was copied so widely that its name became the name of the genre.
+What is jamgrass?
A looser, festival-friendly offshoot of bluegrass with long improvised jams, closer to jam bands like the Grateful Dead. Its band names tend to be more playful.
