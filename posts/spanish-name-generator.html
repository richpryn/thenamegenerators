How the Spanish Name Generator Works

It works from 1,214 names and name parts, which combine into over 72,000,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Gender: Any, Male, Female.
  • Name format:
    • Full name, e.g. Elene Perelló Barceló
    • First name only, e.g. Luisa
    • Surnames only, e.g. Franco Pons

Where the names come from: These are real recorded names. Every first name and surname is real, from INE, Spain's national statistics institute (Apellidos y nombres más frecuentes). First names are the most frequent names of people born in Spain, decade by decade and province by province (Padrón, 1 January 2022), kept if at least 1,500 people in Spain have them. Surnames are the most frequent first surnames of people born in each province (Censos anuales, 1 January 2025). INE publishes names without accents or particles, so the accents are added back and compound names like María del Carmen, which can't be rebuilt for sure, are left out. Source: own work with data from the INE website, www.ine.es, reused under CC BY 4.0. The names are joined at random, so a full name is a new pairing. Left out: brand names, surnames better known as first names, and pairings that match a famous person, such as Fernando Alonso or Pedro Sánchez.

The labels on each name: Real name a real name, found in historical records or in real use. Real parts a new combination of real names or name parts, made by this generator. Our Naming Library explains how we check names.

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

A Spanish full name has two surnames, so Lucía Fernández Ruiz is Lucía, with Fernández from one parent and Ruiz from the other. This Spanish name generator builds names that way from INE's official lists of the names and surnames of people born in Spain, including Catalan, Basque and Galician first names. Eight good Spanish names

  • Lucía Fernández Ruiz: Lucía tops the list for girls born in 2020 and 2021, and Fernández means 'son of Fernando'.
  • Mateo Romero Blanco: one of the most given boys' names of the 2020s, with two common surnames.
  • María José Sánchez Vidal: a compound first name that pairs María with José, which is normal for a girl in Spain.
  • Mónica Pérez García: Mónica was a favourite for girls born in the 1970s, and García is Spain's most common surname.
  • Jordi Puig Ferrer: Catalan through and through. Puig is a hill and a ferrer is a smith.
  • Laia Roca Serra: another Catalan name; a serra is a mountain range.
  • Iker Etxeberria Ortiz: a Basque first name and a Basque surname meaning 'new house'.
  • Uxía Otero Varela: a Galician first name with two surnames common in Galicia.

Why Spanish names have two surnames

Everyone in Spain is registered with two surnames: the first surname of each parent. Traditionally the father's came first, so a child of Manuel García López and Teresa Ruiz Moreno would be García Ruiz.

  • Parents now choose the order. Under the Civil Registry Law of 2011 (Ley 20/2011), parents agree which surname goes first. If they can't agree, the registrar decides in the child's best interest, and the order chosen for the first child applies to their brothers and sisters.
  • Adults can swap them. At 18 a person can ask to change the order of their own surnames.
  • Marriage doesn't change them. Spanish women keep their own two surnames when they marry.
  • The first surname is the everyday one. Most people are known by their first name and first surname, which is why famous Spaniards are usually called by just one.

Many of the most common surnames end in -ez, which meant 'son of': Fernández from Fernando, Rodríguez from Rodrigo, Martínez from Martín, González from Gonzalo, Sánchez from Sancho, López from Lope.

The Surnames only format gives you a pair, ready to follow any first name.

Compound names and the languages of Spain

Compound first names are common, especially for people born before the 1980s: José Luis, Juan Carlos, Francisco Javier and Miguel Ángel for men; María José, Ana María and María Dolores for women. A man's name can include María (José María) and a woman's can include José (María José).

INE leaves out the little words in names like María del Carmen and María de los Ángeles, so the generator only uses compound names that are written without them.

Spain has several languages, and first names show it:

  • Catalan (Catalonia, the Balearic Islands, Valencia): Jordi, Pau, Arnau, Marc; Laia, Aina, Mireia, Montserrat.
  • Basque (the Basque Country, Navarre): Iker, Unai, Mikel, Xabier; Ainhoa, Nerea, Leire, Irati.
  • Galician (Galicia): Brais, Iago, Xoel; Uxía, Antía, Iria, Sabela.

Names change with the decades too. For people born before 1930 the top names were José, Antonio and Manuel, and María, Carmen and Josefa. For babies born in 2020 and 2021 they were Hugo, Martín and Mateo, and Lucía, Sofía and Martina.

Using a Spanish name for a character

  • Use both surnames on paper, one in speech. Forms, ID cards and introductions in formal settings use both; friends would call Lucía Fernández Ruiz just Lucía, or Lucía Fernández.
  • Keep the accents. They show where the stress falls: Martín, not Martin; Sofía, not Sofia.
  • Match the region. A Basque first name with a Basque surname, or a Catalan one with Catalan surnames like Puig, Ferrer or Serra, sounds right together.
  • Search the full name before you publish, in case it belongs to someone well known.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Why do Spanish people have two last names?
Every Spaniard takes the first surname of each parent. Traditionally the father's comes first, but since the 2011 Civil Registry Law parents choose the order. Choose Full name to get a first name with both surnames.
+Which surname comes first in a Spanish name?
Traditionally the father's first surname, then the mother's. Today parents agree the order when they register the birth, and the first surname is the one people use day to day.
+What are the most common Spanish surnames?
García, Rodríguez, González, Fernández, López and Martínez, according to INE. Choose Surnames only to see pairs of real surnames.
+What are popular Spanish girl names?
For girls born in 2020 and 2021 the top names were Lucía, Sofía, Martina, María and Julia. Choose Female for a mix of names from every generation.
+Do Spanish women change their surname when they marry?
No. A Spanish woman keeps her own two surnames all her life, and her children take one surname from each parent.
+Does this generator include Catalan, Basque and Galician names?
Yes. The first names come from INE's lists for every province, so you'll get names like Jordi and Laia, Iker and Ainhoa, and Brais and Uxía alongside José and Carmen.
