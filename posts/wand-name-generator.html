How the Wand Name Generator Works

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet, until the names for the choices you've made run out.

Some wands are tools and some are characters. This wand name generator does both: a full wand-maker's description (wood, length, core and flexibility, in the style made famous by Ollivander), a named wand with a story, or a magic item in the D&D style, like the Wand of Lingering Frost.

Wood, core, length and flexibility

In Harry Potter, Ollivander describes every wand the same way. Harry's is holly and phoenix feather, eleven inches, nice and supple; Hermione's is vine. Ollivander only uses three cores: phoenix feather, dragon heartstring and unicorn hair. Other wand-makers use other things, so the generator adds a few more, like kelpie mane and thestral tail hair.

  • Length runs from about 9 to 14 inches.
  • Flexibility says something about the owner: unyielding for the stubborn, supple for the adaptable, brittle for a wand that won't last.

What the woods mean

Wand woods borrow from the folklore of trees:

  • Rowan was planted by doors in Britain to keep witches and fairies out: a protective wood.
  • Hazel is the dowser's wood, used to find water underground.
  • Yew grows in churchyards and lives for thousands of years, linked with death and rebirth. Voldemort's wand is yew.
  • Elder was thought unlucky to burn or cut, which suits the most dangerous wand in the books.
  • Blackthorn and hawthorn are hedgerow woods of witches and fairies; holly stays green all winter.

Wands in games

  • D&D wands are magic items named for what they do: the Wand of Magic Missiles, the Wand of Fireballs. The "Wand of…" format gives you new ones to use as treasure.
  • Hogwarts Legacy lets you choose wood, core, length and flexibility for your character's wand, exactly the details the wand-maker format gives you.
  • A named wand is for something rarer: an heirloom, a stolen wand, a wand that chose its owner.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are the three wand cores in Harry Potter?
Ollivander uses phoenix feather, dragon heartstring and unicorn hair. Other wand-makers in the books use other cores; Fleur Delacour's wand contains a Veela hair.
+What wand wood should I choose?
Pick one whose folklore fits your character: rowan for protection, hazel for finding things, yew for something old and deathly, holly for hope in dark times.
+Can I use these for D&D or my own story?
