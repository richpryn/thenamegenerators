How consultancies name themselves

Clients buy a consultant's judgement, so the name has one job: make you sound like someone worth listening to. Most consultancies choose one of four shapes:

  • Your own name, as in Sarah Rivers Consultants. Best for a one-person practice, where clients are hiring you.
  • Surnames joined with & or Partners: Holbrook & Partners. These sound like a firm that has been around for years, even on day one.
  • A phrase or promise: Long Game Consulting Group, Spot On Strategy. Easy to remember, and it tells clients how you work.
  • One word: Lattice, Ballast, Outrider. Modern and roomy, but you'll need a line underneath saying what you do.

The last word matters too. Consulting and Consultancy are the plainest, Advisory sounds senior, Partners and Associates suggest more than one person, and Labs or Studio suit digital and creative work. Choose a field and the generator adds the matching words, from HR Solutions to Brand Strategy.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Every name here has a Check button, which opens the Business Name Checker: it looks up domains, social handles, the company register and trade mark searches in one place. Before you order signs or cards:

  • Company register. In the UK, search Companies House; a limited company's name can't be the same as one already registered. In the US, search your state's business register. Some words, such as Chartered, are sensitive and need permission before a company can use them, and a name that suggests a link with the NHS or a government body is restricted too.
  • Trade marks. Search the UK Intellectual Property Office or the USPTO for marks in your line of work. Registering a company doesn't give you trade mark rights.
  • Domain and handles. Try the .co.uk or .com and the Instagram and Facebook names at the same time, and change the name slightly rather than settle for a messy handle.
  • Local search. Look up the name with your town. Two similar businesses a few miles apart confuse customers and reviews.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full process, read Business Naming Best Practices.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What should I name my consulting business?
Most consultants choose one of three shapes: their own name ("Sarah Rivers Consultants"), which sells you as the expert; a surname-style firm name ("Rowe & Carrington"), which sounds established; or a single strong word ("Ballast", "Outrider"), which leaves room to grow. Add your field ("HR Consulting", "Tax Advisory") so people know what you do at a glance.
+Should I use Consulting, Consultancy or Advisory?
Consulting is the commonest and works everywhere. Consultancy is a little more British, and Advisory sounds more senior, which suits finance and strategy work. Partners and Associates suggest a team, so use them only if there is one.
+Can I use the word "Chartered" in my business name?
Only if you hold the chartered status it implies. In the UK, Chartered is a sensitive word, so Companies House needs permission before a company can use it in its name.
+How do I check a consulting business name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company, a domain and a social media handle. In the UK, search Companies House; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Check your local area too, as two similar names nearby confuse clients.
