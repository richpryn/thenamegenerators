Plenty of famous performers aren't using the name they were born with. A stage name gives you a persona, keeps your private life separate, or just sounds better on a poster. This stage name generator makes names in the shapes performers use most: a striking first name and surname, a single name, or an initial and a surname.

Famous stage names and where they came from

  • Freddie Mercury was born Farrokh Bulsara.
  • Elton John was born Reginald Dwight, and built his stage name from two musicians he worked with, Elton Dean and Long John Baldry.
  • David Bowie was born David Jones and changed his name to avoid confusion with Davy Jones of the Monkees.
  • Lady Gaga took her name from Queen's song "Radio Ga Ga".
  • Bruno Mars was born Peter Hernandez; "Bruno" was a childhood nickname.

Most of these began with something real: a nickname, a hero, a song. The generator gives you raw material; your story makes it stick.

Choosing a stage name

  • Easy to say and spell. People need to search for you after hearing your name once.
  • Fits your music. "Vesper Vale" suits dream pop; "Rocco Knight" suits rock.
  • Check it's free on streaming services, social media and as a domain, before you release anything.
  • Think long-term. You may be answering to this name for years.

For band names, try the Band Name Generator; for rap names, the Rapper Name Generator.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Do I need to legally change my name to use a stage name?
No. Many performers use a stage name professionally without changing their legal name. For contracts and royalties, check the rules where you live; this isn't legal advice.
+How do I know if a stage name is taken?
Search streaming services, social media and domain names for the exact name. A small change, like an initial, can make a taken name your own.
