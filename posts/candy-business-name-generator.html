Good candy and sweet shop names make people smile and look good on a jar label: a pun (Lolly Good Show), an old-fashioned shop (Mrs Thackeray's Sweet Shoppe) or something smart (The Gilded Cocoa Bean). This candy business name generator gives you names for a candy store or sweet shop (as it's called in the UK) in seven styles, from punny to old-fashioned and luxury, including ones built from your town or your own name.

Candy or sweets?

Candy is the everyday word in the US and Canada; in the UK and Ireland it's sweets, and a candy store is a sweet shop. Use the word your customers use, or a name with neither, like The Peppermint Jar, if you sell online to both.

  • Old-fashioned: jars, humbugs, sherbet and the spelling shoppe all say traditional sweet shop.
  • Pick and mix: a phrase British customers know and search for.
  • Luxury: chocolate words and French touches like Maison (house) suit gift boxes.
  • Seaside: a stick of rock with the town's name running through it is a British resort tradition, so a local name suits a seafront shop.

Check it's free before you print the sign

A name from a generator is a starting point, not permission to use it. Before you order the jars, labels, sweet bags or sign:

  • Company register. In the UK, search Companies House; you can't register a limited company with the same name as an existing one. In the US, search your state's business register.
  • Trade marks. Search the UK trade mark register (or the USPTO in the US) for the goods and services you'll sell.
  • Domain and handles. Check the .co.uk or .com and the Instagram and TikTok handles you'll need.
  • Your area. Search a map app and the delivery apps for businesses with the same or a similar name nearby.

Press Check on any name, or use the Business Name Checker, to run most of these at once. For the full process, read our guide, Business Naming Best Practices. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

In the UK, any food business must register with the local council at least 28 days before it starts trading. Registration is free, and the council will inspect you and give you a food hygiene rating.

Tips for sweet shops

Avoid the names of famous sweets and chocolate bars, which are trade marks. For more treats, try the Ice Cream, Dessert and Cookie business name generators.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good candy store names?
Cheerful names that look good on a jar or a bag: Lolly Good Show, Sherbet Dip Sweet Shop, Toffee & Gobstopper, The Peppermint Jar or, for chocolates, The Gilded Cocoa Bean. Choose a style for catchy, cute, classic, luxury or modern names.
+How do I check a candy business name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company name, a domain and a social media handle. Before you commit, search the official registers too: in the UK, Companies House and the UK trade mark register; in the US, your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Look on a map app for businesses with the same name nearby.
+Should I call it a candy shop or a sweet shop?
Use the word your customers use. Candy is the everyday word in the US and Canada, sweets in the UK and Ireland, though candy now reads as fun and modern in the UK too. Selling online to both, a name without either word (The Sherbet Jar, Bonbon Voyage) works everywhere; add the trade word in your listing.
+Can I name my sweet shop after a famous sweet?
Not after a brand. The names of branded sweets and chocolate bars are trade marks, so leave them out. Traditional sweets like humbugs, sherbet lemons and fudge are ordinary words and fine to use.
