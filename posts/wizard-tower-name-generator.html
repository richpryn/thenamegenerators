How towers get their names

Towers have always been named for who built them or what they were for. Real ones show the pattern: Tycho Brahe's observatory on the island of Hven, built in the 1570s, was called Uraniborg, after Urania, the muse of astronomy. The follies that 18th-century landowners put up on their estates were named after the owner or the view. In fiction the wizard's tower became a type of its own, and Saruman's Orthanc at Isengard is the one most readers picture.

  • Arcane towers are needles, spires and pinnacles, often with a jewel or a colour: The Indigo Spire.
  • Necromancer towers take bones, graves and the names of the dead: The Tower of Ivo the Gravewalker, Dirgemere.
  • Elemental towers name their element: The Tower of Falling Hail, Blazetor.
  • Observatories watch the sky: Clearfell Observatory, The Comet Watch.
  • Ruined towers say what's left: The Roofless Tower of Wickby.
  • Funny towers belong to wizards whose neighbours have complained: Old Whiffle's Extremely Tall Tower.

Choose With a description for a line about the tower itself, which is often the best hook for a visit.

Using a tower in your story

  • Name it after its owner if the wizard is still alive, and after its fate if they aren't.
  • Let the description set the quest: a door that opens to a true name means someone has to find that name.
  • Give each floor a purpose, from the library to the room nobody goes into.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What is a good name for a wizard tower?
One that tells you about the wizard: The Sapphire Needle of Mallowfell for a scholar, The Spire of the Tolling Bell for a necromancer, or Hepzibah's Meteor Tower for an astronomer. Choose a style to match your wizard.
+Can I get a description with the tower name?
Yes. Choose With a description for a line about the tower, such as a door that only opens to its true name or a library that is bigger on the inside.
