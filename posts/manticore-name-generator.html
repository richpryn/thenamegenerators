The manticore has a lion's body, a human face with three rows of teeth and a tail that shoots spines. Its name comes, through Greek writers, from an old Persian word meaning man-eater. Where the manticore comes from

The manticore's name comes from Persian, through Greek, and means "man-eater".

  • Ctesias, a Greek doctor at the Persian court around 400 BC, described it: a red, lion-bodied beast with a human face, three rows of teeth, and a tail that shot spines.
  • Pliny the Elder repeated the description in his Natural History, and from there it passed into medieval bestiaries.
  • A tiger? Many think the legend began with travellers' tales of tigers, grown with each telling.

Naming a manticore

  • Savage names are all spine and fang: Spinetail, Fanghunter.
  • Persian-sounding names echo its origin: Martikhor, Varesh, Taravash.
  • Named for the wastes: the Manticore of the Scorched Plains, the Grinning Manticore of the Dust Sea.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What does manticore mean?
Man-eater. The word came into Greek from Persian.
+Who first described the manticore?
Ctesias, a Greek doctor at the Persian court, around 400 BC. Pliny the Elder later repeated his description.
+What's a good name for a manticore?
A savage one like Spinetail, a Persian-sounding one like Martikhor, or a place, like the Manticore of the Scorched Plains.
