How the Wild West Town Name Generator Works

It works from 1,354 names and name parts, which combine into over 35,000 possible results.

The choices:

  • Name Format:

Each press gives you 8 names. "Show 8 more" adds 8 you haven't seen yet. Once you've seen them all, "Start again" shuffles the whole list again.

Good Wild West town names say what the town was built for: a strike (Bedrock City), a water stop (Yucca Wells), a river crossing or a fort. The Wild West Town Name Generator names the towns of the Old West: gold-rush boomtowns, cattle towns at the end of the trail, desert water stops, abandoned ghost towns and lonely frontier forts, with a short description if you want one.

How Old West towns got their names

Frontier towns were named fast, often by whoever got there first, and the names stuck.

  • A bit of luck or bad luck. Tombstone, Arizona, got its name because the prospector Ed Schieffelin was told the only thing he'd find in that country was his tombstone. He named his first claim Tombstone, and the town that grew up took the name.
  • The landscape. Deadwood, South Dakota, was named after the dead trees in its gulch. A gulch is a steep ravine, a butte is a lone flat-topped hill, and in the dry South-West a tank is a pool or hollow that holds water.
  • The work. In the California gold rush, a bar was a gravel bank in a river where miners panned, and many camps were named after one. Diggings meant the same thing on dry land.
  • A fort or a railway. Dodge City, Kansas, took its name from nearby Fort Dodge. Towns on the railway were often called a junction or a station.

Using a Wild West town

  • Give it a reason to exist. A frontier town is there because of gold, cattle, water, a railway or a fort. When that goes, it becomes a ghost town.
  • Use the description as a plot hook. A town "whose richest claim was won in a game of cards" is a story waiting to start.
  • Name a few at once for a map of a whole territory, from the fort to the ghost town in the hills.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good Wild West town names?
Names that say why the town is there: a strike like Bedrock City, a water stop like Yucca Wells, a crossing like Bridle Junction, or a fort like Fort Iron Gate. Ghost towns get names like Stopped Clock Camp.
+How did Tombstone get its name?
The prospector Ed Schieffelin was told the only thing he'd find there was his tombstone. He named his first silver claim Tombstone, and the town took the name.
+Can I use these names in a western story or game?
Yes. They're invented, so they're free to use in a story, a tabletop western or a video game.
