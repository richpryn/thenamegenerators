Good therapy practice names feel calm, safe and professional: a nature word (Cedar Counselling Rooms), your own name, or a gentle phrase (Space to Talk). A client often finds a therapist on a hard day, so the name should reassure rather than impress. This Therapy Practice Name Generator suggests names for therapy, counselling and psychotherapy practices with US or UK wording (counseling or counselling), including names built from your town or your own name.

Words, titles and what clients search for

Clients usually search for the service, so say it: counselling, psychotherapy, family therapy. Choose a region for the right spelling: counseling and behavioral health in the US, counselling and wellbeing in the UK.

Be careful with professional titles. In the UK, "practitioner psychologist" titles such as clinical psychologist and counselling psychologist are protected, and only people registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) can use them. Counsellor and psychotherapist aren't protected by law, but clients often look for membership of a professional body such as the BACP or UKCP. In the US, titles like Licensed Professional Counselor are set by each state's licensing board.

The calm style draws on trees, water and open space; the warm style on phrases about talking and finding your way. Avoid names that promise a cure or sound clinical when you aren't a clinic.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Press Check on any name, or use the Business Name Checker, to see whether it's free as a domain and a social handle and to search the company register. Before you commit:

  • Company register: Companies House in the UK, or your state's business register in the US.
  • Trade marks: search the UK IPO or USPTO register for anyone using the name in your field.
  • Locally: search Google Maps for your area, since parents and clients will find you there first.
  • Your professional register: make sure the practice name sits comfortably with the title on your registration or membership, so clients can check you easily.

For the full process, see Business Naming Best Practices. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Choosing between your name and a place name

A solo practitioner often uses their own name, because clients are choosing a person. A place or nature name is better if you plan to add colleagues or rent rooms to other therapists. For other care businesses, try the Home Care Name Generator.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good therapy practice names?
Calm, professional names: a nature word like Cedar Counselling Rooms, a gentle phrase like Space to Talk, or your own name plus the service. Choose US or UK wording for counseling or counselling.
+Should I use my own name for my therapy practice?
Many therapists in solo practice do, because clients are choosing a person: Counselling with Sarah or your full name plus Psychotherapy feels personal and is easy to check against your professional registration. A place or nature name (Willow Tree Counseling) suits you better if you plan to add colleagues or sell the practice one day. In the UK, avoid protected titles such as clinical psychologist or counselling psychologist unless you're registered with the HCPC.
+How do I check a therapy practice name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it's free as a company name, a domain and a social media handle. Before you commit, search the official registers too: in the UK, Companies House; in the US, your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search.
+Can I call my practice a psychology practice?
Only if it fits your registration. In the UK the protected titles are the practitioner psychologist titles, such as clinical psychologist, which need HCPC registration; in the US, rules on psychology titles are set by each state. If you're a counsellor or psychotherapist, say that instead.
