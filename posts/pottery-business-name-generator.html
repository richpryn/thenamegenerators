A good pottery business name is warm, handmade and short enough for a maker's stamp: a clay pun, a homely object, or a studio name for classes and pottery painting. Eight good pottery business names

All eight came from the generator:

  • Coil & Trouble Ceramics: a pun on coil pots and Macbeth's witches.
  • Glaze Days Pottery: a cheerful pun that suits a studio.
  • The Smiley Milk Jug: cute and homely.
  • Plum & Slip: slip is liquid clay, so it's a potter's in-joke.
  • Saggar: a saggar is a box that protects pots in the kiln: short and modern.
  • The Old Coach House Pottery: a classic name tied to a building.
  • Try the Wheel: a studio name that tells beginners what to do.
  • Sarah & Kiln: your name and the potter's oven.

Clay words that make good names

  • Kaolin is china clay, named after Gaoling, a hill in China where it was mined.
  • Slip is clay thinned with water, used to join and decorate.
  • Bisque is a first, unglazed firing.
  • Wedging is kneading clay to push out air.
  • Raku and pit firing are firing methods that leave dramatic marks.

The Studio style suits classes and pottery painting, where the name should sound fun and welcoming: Messy Potters Club, Pot Shed Pottery Café.

Check it's free before you print the sign

Before you pay for a logo, labels or a stall banner, press Check on the name you like, or paste it into the Business Name Checker. It looks for:

  • Company names: in the UK, Companies House won't register a limited company name that's the same as one already on the register. In the US, check your state's business register.
  • Trade marks: search the UK Intellectual Property Office and, if you sell abroad, the USPTO. A registered company name doesn't give you trade mark rights.
  • Web and social: the .com or .co.uk, plus the Instagram, TikTok and Etsy handles pottery customers will look for.

If you sell mugs, plates or bowls, the glazes must be food-safe. In the UK there are legal limits on how much lead and cadmium ceramics may release into food, so check your glazes and test your pieces.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For the full checklist, read Business Naming Best Practices.

Tips for a pottery business

  • Fit it on a stamp. Many potters mark the base of each piece, so a short name or initials works best.
  • Pottery or ceramics? Pottery sounds handmade; ceramics sounds design-led.
  • Classes need a friendly name. People booking a first lesson want to feel welcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good pottery business names?
Warm, short names: Glaze Days Pottery, Plum & Slip, Saggar for a modern studio, or Try the Wheel for classes.
+How do I check a pottery business name is free?
Press Check on any name to see whether it is free as a company name, a web address and a social media handle. In the UK, search the Companies House register; in the US, search your state's business register and the USPTO trade mark search. Do this before you pay for a logo, labels or signage.
+Should a pottery business name say pottery or ceramics?
Pottery sounds warm and handmade and suits a studio, a shop or classes; Ceramics sounds more design-led and suits a tableware brand or a gallery maker; Clay Co. sits between the two. If you mostly sell online, a short name without either word (such as Kaolin) is easier to fit on a maker's stamp and a handle.
+Do I need anything special to sell mugs and plates?
Your glazes need to be food-safe. In the UK, ceramics that touch food must stay within legal limits for lead and cadmium release, so check your glazes and test your pieces.
