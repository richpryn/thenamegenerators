Good lo-fi names are soft and lowercase, like a note left on the kitchen table: a cosy object, a sleepy animal, a Japanese word, a full stop at the end. This lo-fi name generator makes producer aliases, collectives and beat tape titles for lo-fi hip hop and chillhop.

How lo-fi names itself

Lo-fi hip hop draws on the dusty, off-grid beats of J Dilla and the jazzy instrumentals of the Japanese producer Nujabes, who worked on the soundtrack of the anime Samurai Champloo (2004). It found its huge audience on YouTube, through round-the-clock streams like the one on the channel now called Lofi Girl, which began as ChilledCow.

  • Lowercase everything. Capital letters feel too loud for music made for 2am, so names stay small: marmalade, lamplight.
  • A full stop after a one-word name makes it feel finished and quiet: chestnut., asa.
  • Japanese words for the moon, rain or the sea are common, in honour of the anime and Nujabes. ame vol. 5 is "rain".
  • Beat tapes are named after small moments: kitchen tape, plant sitting, the slow way round.

Using a lo-fi name

  • Keep the lowercase everywhere, on Spotify, Bandcamp and your artwork, so the name always looks the same.
  • Common words are hard to search. A name like fern is lovely but shared by a lot of people, so a full stop, a second word or "& friends" helps listeners find you.
  • Number your tapes. "vol. 1", "vol. 2" turns single releases into a series people collect.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good lo-fi artist names?
Soft, lowercase names built from cosy things: kettle., sleepy attic, marmalade or tsuki. Add a full stop to a one-word name, or "& friends" for a collective.
+Why are lo-fi names lowercase?
It matches the music: quiet, unhurried and a bit homemade. Capitals feel too loud for beats meant for studying or falling asleep.
+What should I call a lo-fi beat tape?
Something small and everyday: a room, a chore or a season, like kitchen tape, plant sitting or autumn b-sides. Choose Beat tape in the generator.
