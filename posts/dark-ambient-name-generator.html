Good dark ambient names set the scene before a note is heard: a Latin word, a buried place, a numbered ward. Dark ambient is usually one person and a lot of reverb, and the name does much of the work. How dark ambient names itself

Dark ambient grew up in the 1980s and 90s alongside industrial music, with artists like Lustmord turning drones, field recordings and deep reverb into something closer to a place than a song. Drone goes back further, to the long, held tones of composers like La Monte Young, and Brian Eno's Music for Airports (1978) set the pattern for ambient album titles that name a space.

  • Dark ambient names are cold and buried: Latin words, frozen places, catacombs. Crypta, Hiems, Inside the Permafrost.
  • Drone names are calmer and very British here: estuaries, reservoirs, radio masts and long tones. Mills of the North Sea, Music for Reservoirs.
  • Horror ambient names sound like a found tape: a house and a year, a hospital ward, someone's job. Wetherby House, 1938, The Sexton, The Whitlock Tapes.

Collaborations join two projects with an ampersand, the way split releases do: The Projectionist & the Lamplighter.

Using a dark ambient name

  • Pick a name that suggests a cover. A frozen lake or a numbered ward gives you the artwork as well as the title.
  • Single Latin words are popular, so search Bandcamp and Discogs first: there may already be a Somnus or two.
  • Let the album titles tell a story. Horror ambient especially works well as a series: the same house, a different year each time.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good dark ambient project names?
A single cold word, often Latin, like Glacies or Crypta, or a buried place, like The Weeping Mine. Choose Dark ambient for names in that style, or Horror ambient for names that sound like found recordings.
+What's the difference between dark ambient and drone?
Drone is built on long, held tones and changes very slowly. Dark ambient uses drones too, but adds field recordings, deep reverb and a sense of menace.
+How do you name a horror ambient album?
Make it sound like something found: a house and a year (Wetherby House, 1938), a set of tapes, or a quiet hymn for an ordinary place, like Hymns for Damp Walls.
