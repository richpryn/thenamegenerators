A haunted house name sets the mood before anyone walks in: a grand old hall promises a ghost story, a pun promises fun for the little ones, and a cellar with a number promises screams. This haunted house name generator makes names for Halloween attractions, school fairs, ghost tours, escape rooms and games, with a tagline if you need one.

What makes a good haunted house name

  • A real-sounding house: English country houses are called Hall, Grange, Manor, Court or Priory, so Blackwood Grange sounds like somewhere with a past. Borley Rectory in Essex, once called "the most haunted house in England", shows how much an ordinary house name can carry.
  • An address: "The House on Gallows Lane" works because it sounds like the start of a ghost story.
  • A promise: the name should tell people how scared to expect to be. Puns and cosy words (Cobweb Cottage) say family fun; a numbered cellar or motel says screams.
  • A forgotten place: old mills, piers, theatres and schools make good settings because everyone can picture them empty.

Want to spend a night in the real thing? Our sister site Haunted Hotels UK lists haunted castles across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland that you can actually sleep in, with the ghost story behind each one.

Naming a Halloween attraction

  • Match the name to the scare level, and put an age guide on the poster as well. Parents at a school fair will thank you.
  • Add a tagline for posters and tickets: "nobody leaves before midnight" does a lot of work.
  • Don't use a real address or a real tragedy. Make the place up, and leave real people's homes and real events out of it.
  • Charging entry? Search the name first to check another local attraction isn't already using it.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What should I call a haunted house for children?
Something that sounds friendly before anyone goes in: a pun or a cosy word, like Cobweb Cottage or the Not-So-Haunted House. Choose the family-friendly style.
+How do you name a scary haunted attraction?
Short and specific works best: a place and something wrong with it (The Rot Cellar), or a building with a number (Motel 31). Add a tagline to finish the job.
+Can I name my haunted house after a real place?
It's better not to. Make the place up, so you're not using a real person's home or a real tragedy, and check no local attraction already has the name.
