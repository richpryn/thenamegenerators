Horror titles do a lot in very few words: one strange word, or the name of a place where something went wrong. This horror movie title generator makes titles in six subgenres for scripts, stories, student films and party games, with a tagline if you want one.

How horror titles work

  • One word: a single strange word or place name is the classic horror title. It tells you nothing and promises everything.
  • "The X of Y": a title with a name in it (The Undoing of Clara Hale) makes it personal, and suits hauntings and possession stories.
  • A tagline does the selling on the poster: short, a little cryptic, and usually a warning.

The subgenres

  • Slasher: the killer with a mask and a body count. John Carpenter's Halloween (1978) set most of the rules.
  • Folk horror: old customs, isolated villages and the land itself. The term was popularised by Mark Gatiss's BBC series A History of Horror (2010), which named Witchfinder General, The Blood on Satan's Claw and The Wicker Man as its "unholy trinity".
  • Found footage: the film is the evidence. The Blair Witch Project (1999) made it famous.
  • Haunting, possession and creature features each have their own words: houses and names for hauntings, the body and the soul for possession, and teeth and water for creatures.

The generator never rebuilds a real film title. Search a title before you use it for a real film, though, as there are a lot of horror films out there. For the cast, try the Villain, Monster and Ghost name generators.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What makes a good horror film title?
Short and strange. One unsettling word, or a place or person something terrible happened to, then a tagline to do the scaring.
+What is folk horror?
Horror rooted in old customs, the countryside and the land: isolated villages, strange rituals and the past refusing to stay buried.
+Can I use these titles for my film?
Yes, they're free to use. Search the title first in case a film already has it.
