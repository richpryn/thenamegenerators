A necromancer is a wizard who studies death itself: in D&D, the School of Necromancy covers spells that drain life and raise the dead. This necromancer name generator makes gothic scholar names like Mortimer Graveson, grim sorcerous names and hedge necromancers who live a little too close to the churchyard.

Where necromancy comes from

Necromancy began as fortune-telling: the word comes from the Greek nekros, "dead", and manteia, "divination".

  • Asking the dead: in Homer's Odyssey, Odysseus calls up the spirits of the dead to ask the way home.
  • Raising the dead came later. By the Middle Ages, necromancy meant dark magic of any kind.
  • In D&D, Necromancy is a school of wizard magic, covering spells that drain life or raise the dead.

Naming a necromancer

  • Gothic scholars have names that sound like a family of undertakers: Mortimer Graveson, Ursula Ravensworth.
  • Grim necromancers have names that sound already dead: Vaelthas, Sepith.
  • Hedge necromancers are village bone-readers: Old Mother Marrow, Old Father Thistle.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What does necromancy mean?
Divination by the dead, from the Greek nekros (dead) and manteia (divination). It later came to mean raising the dead.
+What's a good necromancer name?
A gothic scholar's name like Mortimer Graveson, a grim one like Vaelthas, or a hedge necromancer like Old Mother Marrow.
+Is necromancy a D&D class?
Not on its own: it's a school of wizard magic. A necromancer is usually a wizard who specialises in it.
