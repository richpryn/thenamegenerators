Symphony, Philharmonic, Sinfonia: what the names mean

  • Symphony and Philharmonic orchestras are the same size; philharmonic is Greek for "loving harmony". Cities with more than one orchestra use both, which is why London has the London Symphony Orchestra and the London Philharmonic.
  • Chamber orchestra and sinfonia mean a smaller band, often of a few dozen players, as with the City of London Sinfonia.
  • Camerata recalls the Florentine Camerata, a group of musicians and thinkers in late 16th-century Florence.
  • Consort was the Elizabethan word for a group of instruments, and early-music ensembles still use it.
  • Quartets often take one word, a person or a place: the Amadeus, Brodsky and Kronos Quartets.

Choose Orchestra for a full or chamber orchestra, Ensemble or quartet for smaller groups like Red Cedar Piano Trio, and Concert or album for season and programme titles like Midsummer Dances or Halloween Spooktacular.

Naming your orchestra

  • Lead with the place if you're a community or youth orchestra. It's how audiences and funders will look for you.
  • Use the right size word. A twenty-player group calling itself a Philharmonic sets expectations it can't meet.
  • For concerts, name the programme or the occasion: a composer and a season, a theme, or the films you're playing.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What's the difference between a symphony and a philharmonic orchestra?
Nothing in size or instruments. Both are full orchestras; the different names usually just tell apart two orchestras in the same city, like the London Symphony and London Philharmonic.
+How do you name a community orchestra?
Put the town first and the kind of orchestra second, like Ashcombe Sinfonia or Oak Valley Philharmonic Orchestra. Choose a size word that matches how many players you have.
+What is a sinfonia?
A smaller orchestra, usually around chamber-orchestra size. The word is Italian for symphony, and orchestras like the City of London Sinfonia use it in their names.
