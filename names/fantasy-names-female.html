Fantasy Names for Girls and Women

These are original female names for fantasy characters, grouped by the kind of character they suit: elves, dark elves, dwarves, orcs, fae courtiers, dark queens and hedge witches. Each one came from one of the name generators on this site, and we picked the ones that sound best and are easy to say.

They are invented names, so they have no real-world meaning. We checked them against well-known names from Tolkien, Warcraft and Dungeons & Dragons and dropped any that matched, so you can use them for a book, a game or a campaign without borrowing someone else's character.

Matching a name to a character

Sound does a lot of the work. Soft letters (l, s, th, v) and open vowels make a name feel light and graceful, which is why elvish names here lean on -iel, -wen and -ara. Hard stops (k, g, r, z) and short syllables feel heavy and fierce, which suits orcs and villains: compare Lirisse with Skargra.

Dwarvish names here borrow the shape of Old Norse women's names, ending in -hild, -dis, -gerd and -frid, just as Tolkien took his dwarves' names from Norse sources. If you want a real historical name instead, our Old Norse names list has recorded ones.

Making a name your own

If a name is nearly right, change one part. Swap the ending (Vymira, Vymessa, Vymiel), drop a letter, or join the first half of one name to the second half of another. Saying the name out loud matters more than how it looks: if readers can't guess how to say it, they will skip it.

Keep names in one culture consistent. If your elves end in -iel and -wen, an elf called Gromsha will stand out, which is only useful if you want her to.

Checking a name before you use it

We have removed names we recognised from major fantasy series, but nobody can check every book and game. Before you publish, search the name in quotes with words like "fantasy" or "character", and check it isn't a real word with an unfortunate meaning in another language.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do you come up with fantasy names for characters?
Decide what the character's people sound like, then build names from a few repeated syllables and endings: soft sounds for elves, hard ones for orcs. A name generator gives you lots of options to choose from; pick the ones that are easy to say and change a letter or ending until the name fits.
+Do these fantasy names have meanings?
No. They are invented names made by our generators from sound patterns, so they have no meaning in a real language. If you want names with real meanings, see our lists of names that mean moon, star, light and more.
+What is a good fantasy name for a girl?
It depends on the character. Elvish names like Lirwen or Aurelwen suit a graceful character, Velmira or Kezzara a villain, and Brynfrid or Osthild a dwarf. A good fantasy name is easy to say, fits the character's culture and doesn't belong to someone famous already.
