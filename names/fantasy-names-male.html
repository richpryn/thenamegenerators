These are original male names for fantasy characters, grouped by the kind of character they suit: elves, dark elves, dwarves, orcs, dark lords and demons, and goblins and gnomes. Every name came from one of the name generators on this site, and we kept the ones that sound strongest and are easy to read.

They are invented, so they have no real-world meaning. We checked them against well-known names from Tolkien, Warcraft and Dungeons & Dragons and dropped any matches, so you can use them for your own story, game or campaign.

What makes a name sound heroic, elvish or evil

Readers pick up a lot from sound alone. Long names with soft consonants and endings like -dil, -ion and -or read as elvish and old. Short names packed with k, g, r and z read as harsh, which is why the orc names here are mostly two hard syllables (Gashgar, Kragrum). Villain names mix both: a smooth start and a sharp end, as in Thraxis or Azgaroth.

Dwarvish names here follow Old Norse patterns, ending in -ulf, -grim, -rik and -mund. Tolkien did the same thing, taking his dwarves' names from the Old Norse poem Völuspá. For recorded Viking names, see our Old Norse names list.

Giving a name more weight

A single name can carry a character, but a second part tells readers where he comes from. Add a family or clan name (Brokdak Stonefist), a place (Saelorin of the Silver Wood) or an epithet (Morkhar the Ashen). The generators linked below can add these for you.

Try the name in a sentence your characters would actually say. "Run, Zakinth!" works; a name you can't shout in a hurry may be too long.

Avoiding famous names

We removed names we recognised from major series, such as the elf names printed in the Dungeons & Dragons Player's Handbook and Tolkien's characters. That is a good start, not a guarantee: search any name you plan to publish, and check it doesn't mean something awkward in another language.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do you make a fantasy name?
Pick the sounds your character's people would use, then combine a few syllables and a typical ending: Fael + anor, Brok + dak. Say it aloud, change one letter if it looks too like a real word, and keep the style consistent across the same culture.
+Are these fantasy male names unique?
They are original names from our generators, and we removed ones that match well-known characters from Tolkien, Warcraft and Dungeons & Dragons. Because generators work from sound patterns, someone else may have used the same name, so search it before you publish.
+Do fantasy names have meanings?
Not these. They are built from sounds, not words in a real language. If you want a meaning, borrow from a real language (Old Norse, Welsh, Latin) or see our lists of names that mean 'fire', 'strength' or 'night'.
