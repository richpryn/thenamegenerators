About this list

Many of the English-style names are built from genuine Old English place-name parts such as -thorpe (outlying farm), -wick (dairy farm or trading place) and -combe (valley), which is why they sound believable. We avoided names of real towns.

Why English-style names sound real

Real English place names are made of a few dozen recurring parts, and readers know them without realising. A name ending in -thorpe, -by or -wick sounds like a village because thousands of real villages end that way. Add a second word the way real places do, such as a church (St Giles), a position (Lower, Nether, Great), a river (-on-the-Wold) or a market (Chipping, Market), and the name gains a history.

Our Naming Library page on English place-name elements explains what each part originally meant, so you can build names that describe the place: a -combe sits in a valley, a -hithe is a landing place on a river or coast.

Naming a whole map

Places in the same region should sound related. Pick two or three endings for each region and reuse them, as real counties do: a coast full of -hithe and -cove towns, a north of -gard and -heim. Then let the odd name break the pattern to show history, like an elven ruin in human lands or a dwarven name on a hill fort.

Vary the length too. A map where every town has four syllables is hard to read; mix short names like Ashscar with longer ones like Dunbourne-on-the-Wold.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How do you name a fantasy town?
Decide what the town is known for (fishing, mining, a market, a castle) and who lives there, then build the name from a feature and a place-name ending: Gull + port, Anvil + fell. Adding a second word, as in Chipping Saltford or Wynthorpe St Margaret, makes it sound older.
+Do fantasy town names have meanings?
Many of the English-style ones do, because they are built from real Old English place-name parts: -thorpe was an outlying farm, -combe a valley and -wick a farm or trading place. The elven and dwarven names are invented and have no meaning in a real language.
+How do you name a medieval town?
Use English place-name patterns: a personal name or feature plus an ending such as -by, -thorpe, -ham or -ton, and perhaps a church or market word, like Market Mercerwich. Our village generator's medieval style builds names from Old English and Old Norse personal names.
