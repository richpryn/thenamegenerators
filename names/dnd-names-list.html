How each race names itself

  • Elves use long, flowing names and often take an adult name when they come of age.
  • Dwarves have a short personal name and a clan name, such as Stoneaxe or Goldvein.
  • Halflings have short, friendly first names and homely family names drawn from the countryside.
  • Tieflings carry either an infernal name or a "virtue" name for an idea they want to live up to, like Reverie or Verity.
  • Dragonborn put the clan name first, then the personal name, so in Bhorrandesk Rhazkar the clan is Bhorrandesk.
  • Orcs use short, hard names; gnomes collect several names and nicknames over a long life.
  • Humans vary by region, which is why this list mixes Sword Coast names like Perrin Westwater with northern ones like Kjeld Greysail.

Picking a name at the table

Choose a name the other players can say. If the table keeps stumbling over Malthrenkazhar, they will call your dragonborn "Mal" by the second session, so you might as well plan a short form. A name that hints at the character's past (a halfling called Quickwater who can't swim) gives you something to role-play.

For a whole party, vary the first letters and lengths so names don't blur together in play: Vymira, Dagith and Ozzie are easier to keep apart than Vymira, Vireth and Velryna.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What are good D&D names?
Good D&D names fit the character's race and are easy to say at the table. Long flowing names suit elves (Selavyre, Thalvaris), sturdy names with a clan suit dwarves (Rurmund Stoneaxe), and homely ones suit halflings (Millie Quickwater).
+What are good tiefling names?
Tieflings use either an infernal-sounding name, such as Morthazel, Vexathos or Ulzira, or a virtue name that describes an idea, such as Reverie, Verity or Threnody.
+How do dragonborn names work?
Dragonborn give their clan name first and their personal name second, because the clan matters more to them than the individual. Childhood names are often used among clutchmates as well.
