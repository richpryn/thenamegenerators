Names for the battlefield

Warhammer Name Generators

Names for Warhammer 40k and Warhammer Fantasy characters: every 40k faction from Space Marines to Necrons, plus Orks and Ogryns.

Warhammer names tell you who someone serves. A Space Wolf sounds like a saga hero, an Ultramarine like a Roman legionary, a T'au carries caste, rank and sept in their name, and an Ork is named for being loud and hitting things. These generators follow the setting's styles, with examples from the books and games.

How to approach Warhammer names

In Warhammer, a name is mostly a rank and a reputation. Space Marines put their title first and their Chapter last, so the name tells you who they are and who they answer to. Sisters of Battle carry saintly Latin-sounding names. Orks pile on titles and nicknames until the name runs out of breath, and Ogryns are known by a simple nickname, often in quotes. Decide the character's faction and rank first, then give them a name that sounds like the army they belong to.

Quick tips

  • Put the rank before the name.
  • Name the Chapter, Order or clan.
  • Give Orks a title they gave themselves.
  • Keep Ogryn nicknames short.
  • Make it sound like the army.

Examples

Veteran Sergeant Lo'van Flameward of the Salamanders

SPACE MARINE

A full name that reads like a service record.

Rank, name and Chapter in one line. 'Flameward' suits the Salamanders, whose whole style is fire and the forge.

Sister Hospitaller Justina Dolorosa of the Order of the Sacred Rose

SISTER

A name that sounds like a saint in training.

Justina comes from the Latin for 'just', and Dolorosa means 'sorrowful'. Both fit an order that heals as well as fights.

Big Mek Muksnag

ORK

An Ork whose name tells you he builds things that explode.

The rank comes first and says what he does. Muksnag is two short, ugly syllables, the sound of something being dropped on a workbench.

Grob 'Mister Shovel'

OGRYN

An Ogryn nickname with dignity, of a sort.

A one-syllable name and a nickname in quotes, taken from the tool he never puts down. The 'Mister' makes it funnier.
