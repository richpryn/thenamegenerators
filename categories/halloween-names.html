Halloween Name Generators

Spooky names for Halloween: vampires, ghosts, zombies, werewolves, witches and demons, for costumes, parties, haunted houses and stories.

A good Halloween name tells you what kind of scary you're in for. A vampire wants an old title and a family name with history behind it. A ghost is usually named for the colour it wears or the place it haunts, like the Grey Ladies of English ghost stories. A zombie can be grim or downright silly, and a werewolf name often means "wolf" in an old language.

The Witch, Black Cat and Haunted House name generators cover costumes, pets and attractions. Use any of these for a costume character, a haunted house at the school fair, a one-shot for your gaming group or a spooky short story. For the party itself, the Event Name Generator has party names, and the Villain and Demon generators cover anything with horns or a cape.

For witches' gatherings there's the Coven and Spell name generators. For things that lurk, try Monster, Skeleton, Mummy and Scarecrow. And if you're writing the story yourself, the Horror Movie Title Generator will give it a name.

For a proper ghost-story night, the Local Legend Generator makes up British folk legends, the Haunted Pub generator gives every inn its ghost, and there are names for ghost hunting teams and paranormal podcasts.

If you'd like to spend Halloween somewhere with a ghost of its own, our sister site Haunted Hotels UK has haunted hotels to book for the night, with the famous rooms you can request.

Once the pumpkins are on the compost, the Christmas name generators take over, from Elf on the Shelf names to Christmas quiz teams.

How to approach Halloween names

A good Halloween name tells people what kind of scary they're in for before they read another word. Britain's ghosts are named the way witnesses described them, by colour and by who they seemed to be, which is why so many old houses have a Grey Lady. Vampires borrow titles and old family names, haunted inns borrow the pattern of real pub signs, and costumes and pets suit a pun. Decide first whether you want a shiver or a grin, then pick the style that matches.

Quick tips

  • Match the name to the scare level.
  • Name a ghost by its colour or its job.
  • Give a vampire a title and a family.
  • Puns suit costumes and pets.
  • Keep children's names friendly.

Examples

The Grey Ploughboy

GHOST

A local ghost with a sad story you can almost hear.

British ghosts are often named for their colour and who they were, like the Grey Ladies of old houses. A ploughboy makes it a farm ghost, out in the fields at dusk.

Dame Liliane Dunraven

VAMPIRE

An old-world vampire who expects to be addressed properly.

A grand title, an elegant French first name, and a surname built from 'dun', an old word for dull greyish-brown, and 'raven'. It sounds like a family that has owned the same castle for centuries.

The Toad and Chain

HAUNTED PUB

A pub sign you'd hesitate to walk under.

It follows the 'A and B' pattern of real English pub names, like the Rose and Crown, but swaps in two grim objects. The familiar shape makes it believable; the toad and chain make it uneasy.

Thrift Meadowsweet and her owl, Professor

WITCH

A kindly hedge witch with a familiar who knows best.

Thrift and meadowsweet are both wild British plants, the kind a hedge witch would gather. The owl's name adds the joke.
