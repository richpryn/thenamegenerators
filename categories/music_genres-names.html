Names by genre

Music by Genre

Band, artist and album names for 35 genres, from black metal and K-pop to bluegrass and film scores.

Every genre names itself differently. Black metal reaches for winter and old tongues, K-pop for short, bright words, country for real-sounding names and dirt roads, lo-fi for soft lowercase. Each generator here is built from how acts in that genre actually name themselves, with at least 200 names for every choice and no real bands or artists.

Start with the Metal Band Name Generator, the K-pop Name Generator or the Country Singer Name Generator. Writing for picture? Try the Score Title Generator and the Composer Name Generator. For a name that works across every style, the Band Name Generator is still the place to begin.

Before you print the T-shirts, read our guide: How to Name Your Band.

How to approach genre names

Every genre has its own naming habits, and listeners notice when a name breaks them. Black metal reaches for winter and old tongues, K-pop for short, bright words, country for real-sounding names with a middle name or a nickname, and lo-fi for soft lower case. Before you name a project, look at what sits next to it on a playlist: the right name sounds like it belongs there, but not so much that it gets lost.

Quick tips

  • Learn your genre's naming habits.
  • Fit in, but don't copy.
  • Check the name on a playlist.
  • Search streaming services first.
  • Keep it easy to spell.

Examples

Sepulchral Catacomb

METAL BAND

Doom and death metal in two heavy words.

'Sepulchral' comes from the Latin sepulcrum, a tomb, so the name is about burial twice over. Long Latin-rooted words are a metal tradition.

Johnny Ray Whitlock

COUNTRY ARTIST

A name that sounds like it's been on the radio for years.

Two first names together, then a solid surname, is a long-standing country habit. It sounds like a real person from a real small town.

The Lammas Fair

FOLK BAND

A folk band named after an old English festival.

Lammas, on 1 August, was the harvest festival when bread from the new wheat was blessed; the name comes from the Old English for 'loaf mass'.

Marguerite "Sweet Lou" Ingersoll

JAZZ ARTIST

A jazz singer whose nickname arrives before she does.

A nickname in quotation marks between first name and surname is a jazz tradition, and 'Sweet Lou' tells you about the voice.
