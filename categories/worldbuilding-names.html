World-building elements

World-Building

Names for every corner of a fantasy world: kingdoms, continents, castles, forests, rivers, dungeons and the cities of elves and dwarves.

A fantasy map is easiest to name from the outside in. Start with the world and its continents, divide them into kingdoms and empires, scatter islands off the coast, then fill the land with villages, castles and the dungeons beneath them.

Each generator follows the way real places get their names: a feature of the land, the people who live there, or an old word that has worn smooth with use. Most have fantasy, medieval, elven and dwarven styles, and several can add a line of history or a hook for your next session.

For the towns and cities on the map, see the Fantasy Town, Fantasy City and Fantasy Country generators. To fill them with people, the D&D name generators have every race and class.

How to approach world names

Name a world from the outside in. Start with the world and its continents, then the kingdoms on them, then the mountains, rivers and forests that divide them, and the villages and dungeons in between. Each layer can borrow from the one above: a northern continent gives its rivers northern names, and a dwarven kingdom names its mountains in its own tongue. Real landscapes were named the same way, by whoever lived nearby, which is why a fell, a tor and a ben are all hills.

Quick tips

  • Start big: world, continent, kingdom.
  • One language per region.
  • Fell in the north, tor in the south-west.
  • Name some places after people.
  • Keep a list so names don't repeat.

Examples

Sumarland

CONTINENT

A continent whose name is a translation.

Old Norse for 'land of summer'. A name with a meaning lets you decide what the first people to arrive thought of the place.

Pedlar's Law

LANDMARK

A hill with a story in its name.

'Law' is an old word for a rounded hill in Scotland and the north of England, as in North Berwick Law. 'Pedlar's' suggests whoever crossed it, or never made it to the other side.

Cowslip Covert

FOREST

A small, gentle wood that sounds completely real.

A covert is a small wood kept as cover for game, and cowslips are a spring flower of English meadows. Two plain words make a wood you can walk through.

The Lantern Warren

DUNGEON

A labyrinth that tells players to bring a light.

A warren is a maze of burrows, so the name promises tunnels that twist back on themselves; 'lantern' hints that someone, or something, down there keeps the lights on.
