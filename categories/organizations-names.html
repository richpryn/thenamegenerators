Groups and businesses

Organisation Name Generators

Names for businesses, companies and brands, and for guilds and clans in fantasy worlds.

This category covers two kinds of group: real ones and imagined ones. For a real business, start with the Business Name Generator, whose guide explains how to turn a word into a name and check it's free to use. The Company and Brand generators give you invented and brandable names.

For fantasy worlds, the Guild and Clan generators build names for a faction, a noble house or your gaming guild.

How to approach organisation names

An organisation's name tells people what it stands for before they know anything else. Real guilds and companies named themselves after their trade, their patron or a symbol on their sign, which is why so many old firms are 'the Company of' something. A modern business needs a name that's short, easy to spell and free to use as a web address. Decide whether the group is real or imagined first: a fantasy guild can be grand, but a real café needs a name people can find.

Quick tips

  • Decide: real business or fictional?
  • Keep real names short to spell.
  • Check the domain and handles.
  • Search trademarks before you print.
  • Give fictional groups a symbol.

Examples

Lodestar

BUSINESS

A business name that promises to point you the right way.

A lodestar is a star used to steer by, usually the Pole Star, so the name suggests guidance without saying what the business does.

The Company of the Copper Hound

GUILD

An adventurers' company that sounds like it has a sign over its door.

'The Company of the' plus an object follows old trade and inn-sign naming, and a copper hound suggests something loyal but not too grand.

Clan Thornmere, motto: "First In and Last Out"

CLAN

A clan you'd want on your side.

Thorn and mere (an old word for a lake) make a believable place-name, and a short motto in plain words follows the Scottish clan tradition.

Page & Spoon

CAFÉ

A café that tells you it's good for a long read.

Pairing two everyday objects with an ampersand is a popular café pattern, and these two promise books and food.
