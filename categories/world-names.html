Real names, country by country

Names from Around the World

Real names from official records: French, Spanish, Italian, Polish, Irish and Arabic names today, plus Viking, Roman and medieval names from history.

These generators use real names, taken from official records rather than made up. The French, Spanish, Italian, Polish and Irish name generators draw on national records of names people have today, with each country's surname rules built in, and the Arabic Name Generator shows how Arabic names are put together. For British and American names, try the Random Name Generator. For names from the past, the Viking, Roman and Anglo-Saxon generators use names recorded in sagas, inscriptions and charters, and explain how each culture put a name together.

How to choose a name from another country

Start with where and when your character was born, because names follow both. Polish babies born in 2025 were most often called Zofia and Nikodem, but the commonest names among all Poles are Anna and Piotr. In Ireland, Mary and John led in the 1960s; in 2025 it was Lily and Rían. Then follow the country's surname rules: in Poland a woman's surname often ends in -ska where a man's ends in -ski. Keep the spelling and accents the country uses, and check a full name isn't a famous person's before you use it.

Quick tips

  • Choose the birth year before the name.
  • Follow the country's surname rules.
  • Keep the accents and special letters.
  • Check a full name isn't someone famous.
  • Mix eras across a family's generations.

Examples

Aleksandra Kamińska

POLISH

A real Polish first name with a surname in its feminine form.

Her brother would be Kamiński: surnames ending in -ski take -ska for a woman, because they work like adjectives.

Saoirse McGrath

IRISH

A modern Irish-language first name with a classic surname.

Saoirse is Irish for 'freedom' and a favourite for baby girls today; Mc means 'son', so McGrath began as 'son of' someone.

Herjolf Halfdansson

VIKING

An Old Norse name that says who your father was.

Halfdansson means 'son of Halfdan'. Norse surnames changed every generation, so his children would be named after Herjolf.

Gaius Hortensius Sabinus

ROMAN

A Roman citizen with all three names.

A personal name, a family name and a cognomen. Sabinus means 'Sabine', after a people who lived near Rome.
